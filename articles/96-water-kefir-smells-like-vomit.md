# Why Does My Water Kefir Smell Like Vomit? (Butyric Acid, and How to Fix It)

Water kefir should smell lightly sweet, fruity, a bit yeasty, maybe a little like cider. So opening a jar and getting a whiff of **vomit** (or baby spit-up, or rancid butter) is alarming.

The culprit is usually a compound called **butyric acid**. Here's what causes it and what to do.

## What's causing the smell

Butyric acid is the compound responsible for the smell of rancid butter and, yes, vomit. It's produced by certain bacteria when conditions in your ferment favor them over the normal yeast and bacteria in water kefir grains.

In other words: **something tipped the balance of your culture.**

## Common triggers

**1. Unrefined or mineral-heavy sugars**
Some sugars, such as **coconut sugar**, very dark sugars, or molasses, can shift the culture and are commonly linked to off smells in water kefir. Many makers find plain white sugar, or a mix with a little unrefined sugar, more reliable.

**2. Fermenting too long or too warm**
Over-fermented batches, especially in hot kitchens, can develop off flavors and smells.

**3. Stressed or starving grains**
Grains left in the same water too long without fresh sugar can weaken.

**4. Contamination**
Dirty jars, utensils, or bits of fruit left to rot in the jar.

**5. Water issues**
Chlorinated water or unusual mineral content can affect grain health.

## Should you drink it?

**No. Throw out that batch.** A vomit smell isn't a pleasant or normal flavor. Don't drink it, and don't use it to make soda.

**Throw out the grains too if you see:**
- **Mold** (fuzzy spots, any color) on the surface or grains
- Grains that are **slimy and falling apart**
- The smell returns every batch despite resetting

## How to reset your grains

1. **Discard the liquid.**
2. **Rinse the grains gently** in cool, dechlorinated or filtered water.
3. **Wash and rinse the jar** thoroughly, with hot water. Avoid leaving soap residue.
4. **Make a fresh batch** with **plain white sugar** (or your usual reliable sugar), filtered water, and the grains.
5. **Ferment for a shorter time** than usual, around 24–48 hours at room temperature.
6. **Smell it.** It should be pleasant. Repeat for a few batches until the grains are back to normal.

Some makers add a tiny pinch of mineral source (like a small piece of dried fruit or a very small amount of unrefined sugar) once grains are healthy again. Change one variable at a time so you know what works.

## How to prevent it

- Stick to a **sugar you know works**
- Keep ferments at **comfortable room temperature**, not hot
- **Don't over-ferment**; strain on schedule
- Keep **jars and tools clean**
- Remove **fruit pieces** each batch
- Give grains a **rest in the fridge** in fresh sugar water if you're taking a break

## FAQ

**Why does my water kefir smell like vomit?**
Usually because of butyric acid produced by unwanted bacteria, often triggered by certain sugars, over-fermentation, heat or contamination.

**Is water kefir that smells like vomit safe to drink?**
No. Discard the batch.

**Can I save my water kefir grains?**
Often, yes. Rinse them, clean the jar, and ferment a few short batches with plain sugar. Discard grains that are moldy or slimy.

**Can you use coconut sugar for water kefir?**
Many makers find it can cause off smells. White sugar is generally more reliable.
