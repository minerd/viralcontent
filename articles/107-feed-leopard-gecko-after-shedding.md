# Should I Feed My Leopard Gecko After Shedding? What's Normal Around a Shed

Your leopard gecko went pale and dull, skipped a meal or two, then shed. Now they look bright and fresh. Should you feed them right away?

**Yes, you can offer food after the shed is finished.** Many geckos are hungry again once the shed is done. Here's what's normal around shedding and what to check first.

*General care information. See a reptile vet if your gecko has repeated stuck sheds or refuses food for an extended time with weight loss.*

## Why geckos eat less around a shed

Before a shed, a leopard gecko's skin turns **pale, grayish or whitish**. During this time many geckos:
- **Eat less or refuse food**
- **Hide more**
- Move less

This is normal. Shedding takes energy, and many reptiles simply aren't interested in food until it's done.

## They may already have eaten something

Leopard geckos usually **eat their shed skin**. It's thought this helps recover nutrients and avoid leaving traces for predators. So if you don't see the shed skin, your gecko probably ate it. That doesn't replace a proper meal, but it's normal behavior.

## Before you feed: check for stuck shed

Shed that doesn't come off completely is common and can cause problems, especially on:
- **Toes** (stuck shed can restrict blood flow and cause toe loss)
- **Tail tip**
- **Around the eyes**

**If you see stuck shed:**
1. Increase humidity in the **humid hide** (a hide with damp moss or paper towel).
2. Offer a short, shallow, **lukewarm soak** (a few minutes, water no higher than their shoulders).
3. Gently rub with a damp cotton swab. **Don't pull** stubborn pieces.
4. If it won't come off or toes look swollen or dark, see a vet.

## When to offer food

- **Once the shed is complete**, offer a normal meal
- If they refuse, **try again next feeding day**
- Keep fresh water available

Most geckos resume eating within a day or two after shedding.

## When it's not just shedding

Be concerned if:
- Your gecko **keeps refusing food** well after the shed
- There's **weight loss** or a **thinning tail**
- Sheds are **frequently stuck**
- Your gecko is **lethargic** outside of normal shed behavior

Check **temperatures** (a warm side that's too cool reduces appetite) and **humidity**, and see a reptile vet if problems continue.

## Tips for easy sheds

- Always provide a **humid hide**
- Keep a **temperature gradient** with a proper warm side
- Weigh your gecko regularly in grams to track health

## FAQ

**Should I feed my leopard gecko right after it sheds?**
Yes, you can offer food once the shed is complete. Many geckos are hungry again after shedding.

**Do leopard geckos stop eating before they shed?**
Often, yes. Appetite commonly drops while the skin is pale before a shed.

**Is it normal for leopard geckos to eat their shed?**
Yes. Most leopard geckos eat their shed skin.

**How do I help my leopard gecko with stuck shed?**
Provide a humid hide, offer a short lukewarm soak, and gently use a damp swab. See a vet for stubborn shed on toes.
