# Is It OK for a Pellet Stove to Run All the Time? What 24/7 Running Means for Wear, Fuel and Safety

It's getting cold, and you're wondering if you can just leave your pellet stove running day and night. Is that safe? Will it wear out faster?

**In general, yes.** Most pellet stoves are designed to run for long periods, including continuously through the heating season, as long as they're **properly installed, cleaned and maintained**. Always check your own stove's manual, since designs differ.

## Continuous running vs frequent restarts

Many pellet stove owners find that running continuously on a **low setting** can be gentler than turning the stove on and off repeatedly:
- Each start-up uses the **igniter**, which is a common wear part
- Start-ups can produce more smoke and soot while the fire gets going
- A steady low burn keeps the house at a more even temperature

Some stoves have thermostat modes that cycle on and off automatically. Others can modulate heat output without shutting off. Your manual will explain which your stove does.

## What 24/7 running means for pellets

Pellet use varies hugely depending on:
- Your stove's heat setting
- Outdoor temperature
- Home size and insulation

In very cold weather on high settings, some households burn through a bag a day or more; on low settings in milder weather, much less. Track your own use for a week to estimate how many bags you need for the season.

## Maintenance for heavy use

Running continuously means ash and soot build up faster. A typical routine (check your manual for specifics):

**Daily or every couple of days**
- Check and **clean the burn pot** so air holes stay clear
- Glance at the flame: a lazy, dark orange, smoky flame often means it needs cleaning

**Weekly (or per bag count in your manual)**
- **Empty the ash pan**
- Clean the **glass**
- Brush down **heat exchanger tubes** if your stove has a scraper rod

**Monthly in heavy use**
- Clean ash from **behind panels** and around the fan areas as your manual describes

**Annually**
- A full **professional service** and **vent cleaning**

**Ash safety:** put ash in a **metal container with a lid**, away from the house and anything flammable. Ashes can stay hot for days.

## Safety checklist

- **Carbon monoxide detectors** on every level, especially near sleeping areas
- **Smoke detectors** working
- Keep the required **clearance** around the stove
- **Vent pipe** inspected and clean
- Use **good-quality pellets** (low ash, low moisture); poor pellets mean more ash and more cleaning
- Have a plan for **power outages**: most pellet stoves need electricity for fans and augers. Some owners use a battery backup, per manufacturer guidance

## Signs something's wrong

- Smoke smell indoors
- Frequent shutdowns or error codes
- Pellets piling up unburned in the burn pot
- Unusual noises from fans or auger

Stop using the stove and consult the manual or a technician if you notice these.

## FAQ

**Can you run a pellet stove 24/7?**
Most are designed to run for long periods, including continuously, with proper maintenance. Check your manual.

**Is it better to leave a pellet stove on or turn it off?**
Many owners find a steady low burn reduces igniter wear and keeps temperatures even. It depends on your stove and heating needs.

**How often should you clean a pellet stove that runs all the time?**
Burn pot every day or two, ash pan and glass weekly, and a full professional service annually. Follow your manual.

**Is it safe to leave a pellet stove on overnight?**
With proper installation, maintenance, clearances, and working smoke and CO detectors, many people do.
