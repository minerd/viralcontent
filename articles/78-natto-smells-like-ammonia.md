# Why Does My Natto Smell Like Ammonia? Normal Aging vs Past Its Prime

Natto already has a reputation. Sticky, stringy fermented soybeans with a strong smell that people either love or can't stand.

But if you open a pack and get a sharp, **cleaning-product ammonia** smell, that's something a bit different from natto's usual funk. Here's what's going on.

## Why natto can smell like ammonia

Natto is made by fermenting soybeans with a bacterium called ***Bacillus subtilis*** (often sold as the variety *natto*). As the bacteria work, they break down the protein in soybeans.

Part of that protein breakdown releases **ammonia**. A little is normal. The longer the fermentation continues, the more ammonia builds up. And importantly, natto keeps slowly fermenting even in the fridge.

So a stronger ammonia smell usually means:
- The natto is **older**, closer to or past its best-by date
- It was **stored somewhere too warm** at some point
- It was **fermented longer** (common with homemade natto)

## Is ammonia-smelling natto safe?

A **mild** ammonia note, especially right when you open it, is common and generally just a sign of age. Many natto fans barely notice it.

A **very strong** ammonia smell that makes you pull away usually means the natto is past its best. It's not the flavor it's supposed to have, and many people find it unpleasant to eat.

**Throw it out if you see:**
- **Mold** that's green, black, pink or fuzzy (natto can have a white, powdery look from the bacteria, which is normal, but colorful fuzzy growth isn't)
- **Dry, crumbly, gritty beans** with a lot of white crystal-like spots
- **Slimy liquid** that isn't the usual sticky threads
- **Sour or rotten smells** rather than natto's usual earthy funk
- **Anything way past its date**

When in doubt, don't eat it.

## How to reduce the ammonia smell

If it's just mildly ammonia-ish:
1. **Open it and let it sit** for a few minutes. Ammonia is volatile, so some of it airs out.
2. **Stir vigorously.** Whipping natto (many people stir 50 times or more) builds the sticky threads and changes the aroma and texture.
3. **Add the classic seasonings**: the included sauce, soy sauce, karashi mustard, chopped green onion. These balance the smell a lot.
4. **Serve it over hot rice.** The warmth and rice help mellow it.

## How to keep natto fresh

- Keep it **refrigerated** consistently
- Eat it **well before** the best-by date for the mildest flavor
- **Freeze** extra packs. Natto freezes well and freezing slows fermentation a lot. Thaw in the fridge.

## For homemade natto makers

If your homemade natto smells strongly of ammonia:
- **Shorten the fermentation time**
- Check your **temperature**, as too warm or too long pushes ammonia up
- After fermenting, let it **rest in the fridge** for a day. Many makers find the flavor improves and the smell calms down.

## FAQ

**Is it normal for natto to smell like ammonia?**
A mild ammonia smell can be normal, especially in older natto. A very strong ammonia smell usually means it's past its best.

**Can you eat natto that smells like ammonia?**
Mild ammonia natto is commonly eaten. Discard natto with colorful mold, rotten smells, or very dry, gritty beans.

**How do you get rid of the ammonia smell in natto?**
Let it air briefly, stir it well, and add seasonings like soy sauce, mustard and green onion.

**Can you freeze natto?**
Yes. Freezing slows fermentation and keeps it fresher for longer.
