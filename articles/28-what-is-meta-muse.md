# What Is Meta Muse? The #1 App in America, Explained (and Whether to Trust It)

Ten days after launch, a new app from Meta took the top spot on the US App Store. On September 19 it hit number one on Google Play too.

It's called **Muse**, and it's not another chatbot. It's an AI agent, which means it doesn't just answer questions. It does things.

Here's what it is, what it costs, and the one question everyone should ask before connecting it to their life.

## What Muse does

Muse is Meta's personal AI agent. You tell it what you need, and it goes and does it. According to Meta, it can:

- Send emails and invitations
- Book travel
- Try to lower your bills
- Fill out forms
- Turn a recipe into a shopping list and order the groceries
- Make purchases (payments run through Link by Stripe)
- Make plans across your calendar

It connects to a long list of services: email, calendars, payments, health and fitness apps, smart home devices, restaurants, shopping, music and events. TechCrunch reports shopping partners include big names like Walmart, Sephora, Instacart and Expedia.

## Where you can use it

Muse launched September 8, 2026 on the web, iOS, Android and WhatsApp. A Mac version followed, and support for Meta's AI glasses is planned. It's currently available in the US (TechCrunch also reports Canada).

Meta also showed off **Muse Charm**, a small keychain-sized gadget with a screen and a little avatar, as a way to talk to Muse. It's expected in December.

## How much it costs

- **Free:** limited use, with a meter that shows how much you have left
- **Power:** $20 a month
- **Maximum:** $100 a month

Meta says it expects most people to stay on the free tier.

## Why it took off so fast

Two reasons.

First, **it's from Meta**, which means it can reach billions of people through WhatsApp, Instagram and Facebook. Few AI startups can match that distribution.

Second, **it's actually doing what people wanted AI to do**. The last few years of AI mostly gave us better answers. Muse promises less typing and fewer errands. TechCrunch reports its early download growth outpaced ChatGPT's first weeks on mobile.

## The trust question

Here's the uncomfortable part. To be useful, an agent like Muse needs access to your email, calendar, payment methods and accounts. That's a lot of access to give any company, and Meta specifically has a long privacy track record: multiple FTC settlements, the Cambridge Analytica scandal, and years of congressional hearings.

Meta says it has built Muse differently:
- Muse runs its tasks in a separate, dedicated secure computer with its own browser (Meta calls it **Muse Secure VM**).
- A separate "Sentinel" agent monitors it for safety.
- Muse can't see your passwords or payment methods, according to Meta.
- Meta says your Muse conversations don't feed into its ad systems.

Those are meaningful promises. They're also promises, and you'll have to decide how much weight to give them.

## If you want to try it

A few sensible steps:

1. **Start on the free tier** with low-stakes tasks: a shopping list, a restaurant idea, a trip plan you'll book yourself.
2. **Connect one service at a time.** Don't link everything on day one.
3. **Keep purchase confirmations on.** Always approve payments yourself.
4. **Review connected accounts monthly** and remove what you don't use.
5. **Read the privacy settings** in the app, especially anything about data used for training or personalization.

## What about Instinct?

Muse isn't the only "AI that does your errands" getting attention. **Instinct**, a startup founded by 23-year-old Noah Shinn, lets you text or call an AI that plans trips, buys groceries and cancels subscriptions. It raised $350 million at a $2.5 billion valuation in August, though it's still in private beta, and some users have raised concerns about how much app access it asks for.

Two very different companies, same big idea: 2026 is the year AI assistants started running errands. The question for all of them is the same too. How much do you trust them with your accounts?

## FAQ

**What is Meta Muse?**
A personal AI agent app from Meta that can complete tasks like shopping, booking travel and sending emails for you.

**Is Meta Muse free?**
There's a free tier with limited usage. Paid plans cost $20 or $100 a month.

**Is Meta Muse safe?**
Meta says Muse runs tasks in an isolated secure environment, can't see your passwords or payment details, and doesn't use your conversations for ads. Start with low-risk tasks and limited access.

**Is Muse available outside the US?**
At launch it's available in the US, with reports of Canada as well. Check the app store in your country.
