# Why Are My Sourdough Pancakes Gummy? 6 Fixes for Fluffy Discard Pancakes

Sourdough pancakes are one of the best uses for starter discard: tangy, tender, great with butter and syrup. Until they come out gummy, dense or raw-tasting in the middle.

Here are the usual culprits and how to fix them.

## 1. Pan too hot (outside done, inside raw)

The most common problem. If the pan is too hot, the outside browns quickly while the center stays raw and gummy.

**Fix:** cook over **medium or medium-low heat**. Pancakes should take a couple of minutes on the first side. Flip when **bubbles form on the surface and the edges look set**, not just when the bottom is brown.

## 2. Batter too thick

Very thick batter makes thick pancakes that are hard to cook through.

**Fix:** thin the batter slightly with milk or water, a spoonful at a time. It should pour and spread a bit on its own.

## 3. Batter too wet or heavy

Discard is already wet, and adding lots of discard plus milk can make batter loose and heavy.

**Fix:** follow a recipe designed for discard, and measure by weight if you can.

## 4. No baking soda (or not enough)

Sourdough discard is **acidic**. Baking soda reacts with acid to create bubbles, lift and tenderness. Without enough, pancakes can be flat and dense.

**Fix:** use a recipe that includes **baking soda** (often along with baking powder). Add it just before cooking, and don't let the batter sit too long after, so you don't lose the bubbles.

## 5. Overmixing

Overmixing develops gluten, which can make pancakes chewy and dense.

**Fix:** mix until **just combined**. A few small lumps are fine.

## 6. Pressing them down

Pressing pancakes with a spatula squashes out air.

**Fix:** flip once, and don't press.

## Other tips for fluffy sourdough pancakes

- **Let the pan preheat** properly before the first pancake
- **Use a little butter or oil**, wiping between batches
- **Rest cooked pancakes** on a rack or in a warm oven rather than stacking them tightly (steam can make them soggy)
- **Very sour discard?** Older, very acidic discard can make pancakes tangier and may need the right amount of baking soda to balance it
- **Overnight batter:** some recipes ferment the batter overnight for flavor, then add eggs and baking soda in the morning

## FAQ

**Why are my sourdough pancakes raw in the middle?**
Usually the pan is too hot or the batter too thick. Lower the heat and thin the batter slightly.

**Do sourdough pancakes need baking soda?**
Most discard pancake recipes use baking soda to react with the acidity and add lift.

**Can you use sourdough discard straight from the fridge for pancakes?**
Yes, many recipes use unfed discard straight from the fridge.

**Why are my sourdough pancakes dense?**
Too little leavening, overmixing, or thick, heavy batter are common causes.
