# Why Does My Chinchilla Make Noises at Night? A Guide to Every Chinchilla Sound

Chinchillas look like quiet balls of fluff. Then night falls, and you discover they bark, chirp, chatter, squeak and redecorate their cage at 3 a.m.

There's a simple reason: **chinchillas are crepuscular and nocturnal**, most active around dawn, dusk and through the night. Night is their day. But some sounds mean more than "I'm awake." Here's a guide.

## The sounds and what they mean

**Soft chirping or squeaking**
Often contentment, curiosity or general chatting, especially between bonded chinchillas or when they see you.

**Loud, repeated barking or "kacking"**
An alarm call. Something startled them: a noise outside, a shadow, another pet, a car. It can go on for a while and sounds surprisingly loud.

**Teeth chattering**
Can mean different things depending on context. Soft chattering is often contentment. Loud, aggressive chattering, especially with a raised stance, can be a warning.

**Grunting or "cooing"**
Often part of social or mating behavior, especially in males.

**Loud squealing**
Pain, fear or a fight. Check on them.

**Crying or wheezing sounds**
Can signal distress, pain or a breathing problem. See below.

## Noises that aren't vocal

A lot of night noise isn't the chinchilla's voice at all:
- **Running and jumping** around the cage
- **Chewing** on toys, cage bars or wooden ledges
- **Rattling** food bowls or water bottles
- **Wheel spinning** (use a solid, chinchilla-safe wheel)
- **Dust baths**, which are noisy and very necessary

## When to worry

Night activity and chatter are normal. Pay attention if you notice:
- **Squealing** that sounds painful
- **Wheezing, clicking or rattly breathing**
- **Drooling or a wet chin**, often linked to dental problems
- **Eating less** or fewer droppings
- **Hunching** or not moving much during their active hours
- **Constant alarm barking** with no obvious cause, which may mean ongoing stress

Chinchillas hide illness well, so see an exotic-animal vet if something seems off.

## How to get a quieter night

You can't turn a chinchilla into a daytime animal, but you can reduce the noise:
- **Move the cage out of your bedroom** if possible.
- **Choose quieter accessories**: ceramic bowls instead of metal, a quiet solid wheel, securely attached ledges.
- **Give lots of chew toys**, which reduces bar chewing.
- **Play time in the evening**, before bed, to burn off energy.
- **Keep the room calm and consistent**: minimize sudden noises and lights that trigger alarm barking.
- **Keep the room cool.** Chinchillas are very sensitive to heat, and a comfortable, cool room keeps them calmer and safer.

## FAQ

**Why does my chinchilla make noise at night?**
Chinchillas are most active at night and around dawn and dusk, so running, chewing and chatting at night are normal.

**Why is my chinchilla barking at night?**
Loud barking is usually an alarm call because something startled them.

**Is it normal for chinchillas to chatter their teeth?**
Soft chattering can mean contentment. Loud chattering with a defensive posture can be a warning.

**How do I keep my chinchilla quiet at night?**
Move the cage out of the bedroom, use quieter accessories, provide chew toys and give evening play time.
