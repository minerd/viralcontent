# Is Algae Bad for a Shrimp Tank? Good Algae, Bad Algae, and the One That Isn't Algae

If you keep freshwater shrimp, you may have noticed something funny: the tank everyone calls "dirty" often has the happiest shrimp. That's because **for shrimp, a bit of algae is lunch.**

So no, algae isn't automatically bad in a shrimp tank. But some kinds signal a problem, one "algae" isn't actually algae at all, and the way you remove algae matters a lot, because some common treatments can kill shrimp.

## Why shrimp like algae

Dwarf shrimp spend most of their day grazing. They pick at **biofilm**, the thin layer of microorganisms and algae that grows on every surface, plus soft algae on plants, rocks and glass. It's a big part of their natural diet, especially for baby shrimp, who rely on it.

A tank that's been scrubbed spotless can actually be a worse home for shrimp than one with a light coating of growth.

## Algae that's usually fine (or even good)

- **Biofilm:** a thin, often invisible layer on surfaces. Great food.
- **Soft green algae** on glass, rocks and decorations: shrimp graze it happily.
- **Light green film** on hardscape: normal in a healthy tank.
- **Some brown diatoms** in new tanks: common early on and often fades as the tank matures. Shrimp may eat them.

## Algae that suggests something is off

Algae itself doesn't usually harm shrimp directly. But **heavy growth** tells you the tank is out of balance, usually too much light, too many nutrients, or both.

Watch for:
- **Algae covering plants** thickly enough to block their light
- **Hair or thread algae** tangling everywhere (shrimp eat some, but large mats can trap tiny shrimplets)
- **Green water**, where the water itself turns cloudy green
- **Very fast regrowth** after cleaning

## The one that isn't algae: blue-green "algae"

"Blue-green algae" is actually **cyanobacteria**. It looks like a slimy, dark green or blue-green sheet that peels off in layers, and it often has a strong, unpleasant smell.

It's worth taking seriously:
- Shrimp generally **don't eat it**
- It can **smother plants and surfaces**
- It often points to **poor water flow**, **excess nutrients**, or **decaying waste**

Remove it manually, siphon it out, improve circulation, and look at feeding and maintenance. Ask in a shrimp-keeping community or your aquarium shop for shrimp-safe approaches if it keeps coming back.

## How to control algae without harming shrimp

**Do:**
- **Cut the light.** Many shrimp tanks do well with a lighting period around 6–8 hours a day. Use a timer.
- **Feed less.** Uneaten food fuels algae. Shrimp eat surprisingly little.
- **Keep up small, regular water changes**, using water matched in temperature and dechlorinated.
- **Add fast-growing plants** (like floating plants) that compete with algae for nutrients.
- **Remove algae by hand**: scrape glass, pull out hair algae, siphon debris.
- **Keep some algae** on a rock or the back wall as a food source.

**Don't:**
- **Don't use algaecides or treatments unless they're clearly labeled shrimp-safe.** Many aquarium treatments are dangerous to invertebrates.
- **Watch out for copper.** Copper is highly toxic to shrimp, and it's found in some medications and algae products. Always check ingredient labels.
- **Don't make big, sudden changes** to water chemistry chasing algae. Shrimp are more sensitive to sudden swings than to a bit of green.

## FAQ

**Is algae bad for shrimp?**
Usually not. Most algae and biofilm are food for shrimp. Heavy growth suggests imbalance in light or nutrients.

**Do shrimp eat algae?**
Yes, many dwarf shrimp graze on soft algae and biofilm throughout the day.

**Is blue-green algae harmful to shrimp tanks?**
Blue-green algae is cyanobacteria. Shrimp generally don't eat it, and it can smother surfaces, so remove it and fix the underlying cause.

**Are algae treatments safe for shrimp?**
Many are not. Only use products clearly labeled shrimp-safe, and avoid anything containing copper.
