# Can You Cut Polymer Clay After Baking? Yes: Tools, Tricks and What to Avoid

You baked your earrings, charms or sculpture, and now something's not quite right. An edge is too long, the shape is uneven, or you want to split a piece in two.

**Yes, you can cut polymer clay after baking.** Fully baked polymer clay is a sturdy, slightly flexible plastic, so it can be cut, trimmed, sanded and drilled. The trick is choosing the right tool and working with the clay, not against it.

## The warm-clay trick

Polymer clay is **easiest to cut while it's still warm** from the oven. As it cools, it gets harder and more brittle.

If you know you'll need to trim a piece, have your blade ready and cut **shortly after baking**, once it's cool enough to handle safely. Many makers do quick trims at this stage.

**Always protect your hands.** Warm clay and sharp blades are a risky combination. Use a cutting mat and keep fingers out of the blade path.

## Best tools for each job

**Thin pieces (earrings, charms, slices)**
- **Tissue blade or clay blade**: press straight down for clean cuts
- **Sharp craft knife**: for curves and small trims
- **Heavy-duty scissors**: can work on very thin baked pieces, though they may leave slightly rough edges

**Thicker pieces**
- **Jeweler's saw or fine-toothed hobby saw**: slow, controlled strokes
- **Rotary tool with a cutting disc**: fast, but go slowly and wear eye protection

**Holes**
- **Hand drill or pin vise**: the gentlest option for jewelry holes
- **Rotary tool with a small bit**: at low speed

**Smoothing cut edges**
- **Wet sanding** with fine sandpaper, working from coarser to finer grits
- **Buffing** with a soft cloth or denim for shine

## How to avoid cracks and chips

- **Make sure it's fully baked.** Under-baked clay is weak and crumbly, and it's much more likely to crack when cut. Follow your clay brand's temperature and time, and use an oven thermometer.
- **Don't twist the blade.** Press straight down or saw gently.
- **Support the piece** on a flat surface right up to the cut line.
- **Score, then cut.** For thicker pieces, score a line first, then cut or saw along it.
- **Go slow near edges and thin points**, where chips are most likely.

## Sanding safely

Sanding baked polymer clay creates fine plastic dust. **Wet sanding** (sanding with the piece and sandpaper under a little water) keeps dust down and gives a smoother finish.

## Can you re-bake after cutting?

Yes. Polymer clay can generally be baked more than once, which is useful if you want to add new clay to a cut piece. Watch for darkening or scorching with repeated baking, especially in light colors.

## FAQ

**Can you cut polymer clay after it's baked?**
Yes. Use a sharp blade, craft knife or saw depending on thickness. It's easiest while the clay is still warm.

**Why does my baked polymer clay crack when I cut it?**
Usually because it's under-baked, or the blade is twisting. Bake fully and cut straight down.

**Can you drill baked polymer clay?**
Yes. A hand drill or pin vise works well for small holes.

**How do I smooth cut edges on polymer clay?**
Wet sand with fine sandpaper, then buff with a soft cloth.
