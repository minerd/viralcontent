# Why Is My Air Dry Clay Cracking While I'm Molding It? (Not While Drying)

Almost every article about air dry clay cracking talks about cracks that appear **while it dries**. But there's a different, equally frustrating problem: the clay cracks **in your hands**, while you're rolling, bending or shaping it.

That usually means one thing: **the clay has lost moisture before you're done working it.** Here's why it happens and how to fix it.

## Why it cracks while you work

**1. The clay is already too dry**
Air dry clay starts drying the moment it's exposed to air. A package that's been opened before, not sealed well, or stored in a warm place may already be too stiff.

**Clue:** cracks appear as soon as you bend or roll it, even at the start.

**2. Your hands are drying it out**
Warm hands and a dry room pull moisture out quickly, especially from small pieces you handle a lot.

**Clue:** starts fine, gets crumbly after a few minutes of handling.

**3. You're overworking it**
Kneading, rolling and reshaping the same piece again and again speeds up drying.

**4. Thin shapes and edges**
Thin edges, points and small details dry fastest and crack first.

**5. A dry, warm workspace**
Heaters, fans, air conditioning, direct sun. All of these dry clay while you work.

## How to fix clay that's too dry

**For slightly dry clay:**
- **Wet your hands** lightly and knead the clay. Add water a little at a time; too much makes it sticky and mushy.

**For stiffer clay:**
1. Poke several holes in the lump with a pencil or chopstick.
2. Add a few drops of water into the holes.
3. **Wrap tightly** in plastic wrap and seal in a zip-lock bag.
4. Leave it for **several hours or overnight** so the moisture spreads evenly.
5. Knead well before using.

**For very dry, crumbly clay:** it may be past saving for detailed work, but you can often still use it for simple shapes, or break it down with water into a paste for filling cracks (see below).

## How to prevent cracking while you work

- **Only take out what you need.** Keep the rest wrapped and sealed.
- **Keep a damp cloth nearby** to cover pieces you're not working on.
- **Keep a small cup of water** or a spray bottle to moisten your fingers, not to soak the clay.
- **Work in a cool spot**, away from heaters, fans and sunlight.
- **Work efficiently.** Plan the shape before you start so you're not reshaping endlessly.
- **Add thin details last**, when the main shape is done.

## Fixing cracks as you go

- **Small surface cracks:** smooth them with a damp finger.
- **Deeper cracks:** mix a little clay with water into a paste (often called **slip**), press it into the crack, and smooth it over.
- **Joining pieces:** score both surfaces (scratch cross-hatch lines), add a little slip, and press together firmly. This helps prevent cracks at joins later.

## Storing clay so it doesn't dry out

- Squeeze out air and **wrap tightly** in plastic wrap
- Put it in an **airtight bag or container**
- Add a **damp paper towel** in the container (not touching the clay directly) for longer storage
- Store somewhere **cool**

## FAQ

**Why does my air dry clay crack when I bend it?**
Usually because it's already too dry, or it's drying out from handling. Rehydrate it with a little water and work quickly.

**How do I soften hardened air dry clay?**
Add small amounts of water, wrap tightly and let it rest for several hours or overnight, then knead.

**Can I fix cracks in air dry clay while it's still wet?**
Yes. Smooth small cracks with a damp finger and fill deeper ones with slip (clay mixed with water).

**How do I stop air dry clay from drying out while I work?**
Keep unused clay wrapped, cover pieces with a damp cloth, moisten your fingers lightly, and avoid warm, drafty spots.
