# Is It Normal for Tepache to Be Slimy? What "Ropy" Tepache Means and What to Do

Tepache is a lightly fermented Mexican drink made from pineapple peels, piloncillo or sugar, spices and water. It should be sweet, tangy and a little fizzy.

So when you lift the lid and find it **thick, stringy, slimy or syrupy**, like it's been mixed with egg whites, it's unsettling.

Here's what's usually happening and what to do.

## What causes slimy tepache

Slime in ferments is often called **"ropiness."** It's usually caused by certain types of bacteria (lactic acid bacteria are commonly involved) that produce thick, stringy compounds as they ferment sugar.

It shows up in other ferments too: kombucha, water kefir, kvass, even wine and cider.

It's more likely when:
- The ferment is **warm**
- There's **a lot of sugar**
- The ferment is **young** and the microbes haven't balanced out yet

## Is it safe?

Slime alone isn't always a sign of danger, and in some ferments a ropy phase passes on its own. **But:**

**Throw it out if you see or smell:**
- **Fuzzy mold** of any color on the surface or peels
- **Pink, black, green or colorful** growth
- **Rotten, putrid or "off" smells** rather than sweet-sour pineapple
- Anything you're genuinely unsure about

**When in doubt, throw it out.** Pineapple peels are cheap.

## If it smells good and there's no mold

Some fermenters choose to:
1. **Give it another day or two** to see if the ropiness breaks down
2. **Stir it gently**
3. **Move it somewhere cooler**

If it's still slimy and unappealing, discard it and start fresh.

## How to prevent slimy tepache

- **Ferment at cool room temperature**, away from heaters or sun
- **Keep the batch short.** Tepache is usually fermented for just a few days, often around 2–3, depending on temperature. Taste daily.
- **Use clean equipment**, well rinsed
- **Use a reasonable amount of sugar**; don't overload it
- **Keep peels submerged** under the liquid
- **Use organic or well-rinsed pineapple** (rinse off dirt but don't scrub away all the natural yeast)

## FAQ

**Why is my tepache slimy?**
Often because certain bacteria produced thick, stringy compounds, a condition called ropiness. Warmth and lots of sugar make it more likely.

**Is slimy tepache safe to drink?**
Discard it if there's mold, colorful growth or a bad smell. If you're unsure, throw it out.

**Can ropy tepache recover?**
Sometimes ropiness breaks down with a bit more time, but if it stays slimy, start over.

**How long should tepache ferment?**
Often a few days at room temperature. Taste daily and refrigerate when you like the flavor.
