# Why Is My Tegu Breathing Heavy? After Meals, Basking, or a Respiratory Infection

Tegus are big, active lizards, and their breathing can be surprisingly visible. If you've noticed your tegu's sides heaving, you're probably wondering whether it's normal.

**Often it is**, especially after eating or activity. But heavy breathing can also be a sign of respiratory infection. Here's how to tell.

*General information. See a reptile vet if you notice any signs of illness.*

## Normal reasons for heavy breathing

**After a big meal**
Tegus can eat large meals. A full stomach takes up space inside the body cavity, so breathing may look deeper or more effortful for a while.

**After activity**
Reptiles take longer than mammals to recover from exertion. After running around, climbing, or a struggle during handling, a tegu may breathe heavily for some time.

**While basking**
Warm tegus are more active and have higher metabolism, so breathing can be more visible.

**Lizards breathe differently**
Lizards use body wall muscles to breathe, so breathing movements are often more noticeable than in mammals. Some owners notice it for the first time and worry, even though it's normal.

## Signs of a respiratory infection

Contact a reptile vet if heavy breathing comes with:
- **Wheezing, clicking or whistling** sounds
- **Open-mouth breathing** when not basking or hot
- **Bubbles or mucus** at the nose or mouth
- **Head held up** or stretching the neck to breathe
- **Runny nose**
- **Lethargy**
- **Loss of appetite**

Respiratory infections in reptiles are often linked to husbandry: **temperatures too low**, **humidity problems**, poor ventilation, or unclean enclosures.

## Husbandry check

- **Basking and ambient temperatures** measured with a temperature gun and digital probes
- A clear **temperature gradient**
- **Humidity** appropriate for your tegu species, measured with a hygrometer
- **Good ventilation**
- **Clean, appropriate substrate** that holds humidity without staying soggy
- **UVB lighting** appropriate for the species

## Brumation note

Many tegus **brumate** (a winter slowdown) and breathe more slowly and shallowly during that time. That's different from labored breathing. If a brumating tegu has respiratory signs, contact a vet.

## FAQ

**Is it normal for tegus to breathe heavily?**
After eating, activity or basking, yes. Heavy breathing at rest with other symptoms isn't normal.

**What are signs of respiratory infection in a tegu?**
Wheezing, open-mouth breathing when not hot, bubbles or mucus, head held up, lethargy and loss of appetite.

**What causes respiratory infections in reptiles?**
Often low temperatures, humidity problems, poor ventilation or unclean conditions.

**Do tegus breathe differently during brumation?**
Yes, breathing is often slower and shallower during brumation.
