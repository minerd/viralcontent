# Why Is My Blue Tongue Skink's Belly Red? Normal Pink vs Scale Rot

You turn your skink over — or notice it as it climbs the glass — and the belly looks **pink, red, or blotchy**. Forum threads are full of this question, with no clear answer page anywhere.

**Most of the time it's blood flow showing through pale scales.** But there are three things it can also be, and two of them need a vet.

## The normal explanations

**1. Blood flow showing through**
Belly scales are thin and lightly pigmented. Blood underneath shows through as pink. It becomes more obvious when the skink is **warm, active, excited, basking, digesting or has just been handled**, and fades when the animal is cool and resting. Some Indonesian and Northern bloodlines are much pinker than others at baseline.

**2. Shed cycle**
Colours shift through the cycle: duller and greyer as a shed approaches, then noticeably **brighter and pinker straight after**, when the new skin is fresh and thin.

**3. Stress or recent change**
A new substrate, a move, a cage rearrangement or a long handling session can bring a flush of colour that settles within a day or so. Skinks that "go pink when excited" are a well-known pattern among keepers.

In all three cases: colour **comes and goes**, scales look smooth and intact, and the animal eats, basks and behaves normally.

## When it's a problem

**Scale rot (bacterial dermatitis)**
- Redness that **stays put** and doesn't fade when the animal is cool
- **Raised, pitted, crusty, brown or weeping** scales
- Usually on the belly and underside — the parts in contact with substrate
- Almost always caused by **substrate that's too damp, dirty, or both**

**Thermal burns**
- Redness in a **defined patch** matching where the animal lies
- Blistering, darkening, or peeling skin
- Caused by heat mats or rocks without a thermostat, or an overly hot basking surface
- Measure **surface temperature** with an infrared thermometer, not a dial on the wall

**Injury or abrasion**
Rubbing against rough decor or glass, or a substrate that abrades. Look for grazed, broken scales rather than a general flush.

Either of the first two needs a **reptile vet**. Scale rot does not resolve on its own and gets deep.

## Work out which you have

| Clue | Likely answer |
|---|---|
| Pink comes and goes with activity/warmth | Normal blood flow |
| Brighter right after a shed | Normal shed cycle |
| Appeared after a substrate or enclosure change | Stress, or new substrate irritating skin |
| Fixed redness, crusty/pitted/weeping scales | Scale rot — vet |
| Patch matching where it lies, blistered | Burn — vet, and fix the heat source |
| Grazes and broken scales | Abrasion — review decor |

## Husbandry to check either way

- **Substrate dry**, deep enough to burrow, spot-cleaned and fully changed on a schedule
- **Humidity** appropriate for your subspecies (Indonesian skinks need more than Northerns) — but the ground they lie on should not be soaking
- **All heat sources on a thermostat**, with surface temperatures measured
- **No heat rocks**
- **Hides** at both warm and cool ends, so the animal can regulate
- **Weight and appetite** tracked, so you can tell a colour change from a sick animal

## FAQ

**Is a pink belly normal for a blue tongue skink?**
Often yes — blood flow through thin, pale belly scales. It's more obvious when the skink is warm or excited.

**Does red belly mean scale rot?**
Not by itself. Scale rot looks like fixed redness with damaged, crusty or weeping scales, usually with damp or dirty substrate behind it.

**Why is my skink pinker after shedding?**
New skin is thinner and fresher, so colour is more intense for a while.

**When should I see a vet?**
If the redness doesn't fade, scales look damaged, there's blistering or discharge, or the skink stops eating.
