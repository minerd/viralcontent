# 5 Custom Instructions Worth Copying Into Every Chatbot

Most people never touch the setting that changes every single answer their chatbot gives them.

ChatGPT calls it custom instructions or personalization. Claude and Gemini have their own versions, usually under personal preferences or profile settings. Whatever it's called, it's a box where you tell the AI how you want it to behave, and it quietly applies that to every conversation.

It's the most useful five minutes you can spend on an AI tool. Here are five instructions I think almost everyone should copy. Use all five or just the ones that fix what bugs you.

## 1. Kill the fluff

> Get to the point. Skip introductions like "Great question!" and don't restate my question. Don't end with a summary of what you just said or an offer to help further unless it's genuinely useful.

**What it fixes:** The opening paragraph that says nothing and the closing paragraph that repeats everything. This alone makes answers about a third shorter and a lot easier to read.

## 2. Stop agreeing with me

> Don't flatter me or my ideas. If I'm wrong, say so directly and explain why. If I push back on something you said, only change your answer if I've given you an actual reason, not just because I disagreed.

**What it fixes:** Sycophancy, the habit AI has of telling you your plan is great and then folding the moment you question it. This is one of the most common complaints about chatbots, and this instruction noticeably helps.

## 3. Tell me how sure you are

> When you're not confident about something, especially facts, numbers, dates, prices, or anything that may have changed recently, say so clearly. Don't make up sources or quotes. It's fine to say "I don't know."

**What it fixes:** Confident nonsense. AI will still make mistakes, but this makes it much more likely to flag the shaky parts, which tells you what to double-check.

## 4. Match the format to the question

> Match the length and format to the question. Short questions get short answers. Use bullet points and headers only when they actually help, like for steps or comparisons. For simple explanations, write normal paragraphs.

**What it fixes:** Asking "what time zone is Chicago in?" and getting three headers and a bulleted list. Also the opposite: a complicated comparison crammed into one paragraph.

## 5. Ask when it's unclear

> If my request is ambiguous and the answer would change a lot depending on what I mean, ask me one short clarifying question first instead of guessing.

**What it fixes:** Long, confident answers to the wrong question. One quick question up front saves you rereading and re-asking.

## The all-in-one version

If you just want to paste something and be done, here are all five combined:

> Get to the point: no "great question," no restating my question, no closing summary unless it's useful. Don't flatter me. If I'm wrong, say so and explain why, and only change your answer if I give you a real reason. Flag when you're unsure, especially about facts, numbers, dates, or recent events, and never invent sources. Match length and format to the question: short questions get short answers, and only use lists or headers when they genuinely help. If my request is ambiguous in a way that matters, ask one clarifying question first.

## Add a line about you

The instructions above are about *how* the AI should answer. Most tools also let you tell it something about *who you are*. A single line helps a lot:

> I'm a [job/role] and I mostly use this for [main uses]. I'm comfortable with [technical level].

For example: "I'm a small business owner and mostly use this for emails, marketing ideas, and spreadsheet help. I'm not technical, so explain code simply." The answers will be pitched at the right level from the first message.

## What not to put in there

- **Private information.** Custom instructions are applied to every chat, so don't include anything sensitive. See our guide to [AI chat privacy](/ai-chat-privacy-settings).
- **Twenty rules.** The more you add, the more they compete. Five to eight clear lines work better than a page of demands.
- **Contradictions.** "Be very detailed" plus "keep it short" just confuses things.

## Give it a week

Custom instructions change the feel of a chatbot more than you'd expect. Use it for a week with these in place, then adjust. If answers feel too blunt, soften instruction 2. If they're still too long, make instruction 1 stricter.

## FAQ

**Where are custom instructions in ChatGPT?**
In the settings, usually under personalization or custom instructions. The exact location changes with updates, so search the settings if you can't find it.

**Do custom instructions work in Claude and Gemini?**
Both offer ways to set preferences that apply across conversations. The names and locations differ, but the same instructions work.

**Do custom instructions apply to old chats?**
Usually only to new conversations started after you save them.

**Can custom instructions make AI more accurate?**
They can't add knowledge the model doesn't have, but they can make it more honest about uncertainty, which makes it easier to spot errors.
