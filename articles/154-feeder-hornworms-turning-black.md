# Why Are My Hornworms Turning Black? (Feeder Hornworm Die-Offs, Explained)

You opened a cup of feeder hornworms for your bearded dragon or chameleon, and a few of them are **black, dark brown or shriveled**. A day later, more are turning.

Hornworms that turn black or very dark are almost always **dead or dying**. Here's what usually causes it and how to protect the rest.

**Never feed black, mushy, limp or smelly hornworms to your reptile. Remove them right away.**

## Common causes

**1. Temperature stress during shipping**
Hornworms are sensitive to extreme heat and cold. Cups left in a hot mailbox or a freezing delivery truck can arrive stressed, and some will die over the next few days.
**Tip:** open shipments promptly and check them right away.

**2. Heat at home**
Warm conditions make hornworms grow extremely fast, which burns through their food and can stress them. Direct sun or a spot near a heater can overheat a cup.
**Tip:** keep cups at room temperature, out of sunlight and away from heat sources.

**3. Condensation and mold**
Moisture on the inside of the lid or cup, especially when temperatures swing, can lead to mold and bacteria.
**Tip:** keep cups at stable temperatures, wipe away excess condensation if you can do so without contaminating the food, and remove frass (droppings) regularly.

**4. Running out of food**
Hornworms eat constantly. If the chow dries out or runs out, they can starve.
**Tip:** check the chow daily; move worms to a fresh cup of food if needed.

**5. Overcrowding**
Too many large hornworms in one cup can lead to fouled food and stress.
**Tip:** feed off the biggest ones first, or split them into more cups.

**6. Pupation**
Hornworms nearing the end of their larval stage stop eating, change color, and wander looking for somewhere to burrow. Pupae turn **brown**, not black and mushy. If you see brown, hard pupae, that's normal development.

## How to tell dead from pupating

| What you see | Likely meaning |
|---|---|
| Black, soft, deflated, smelly | Dead, remove immediately |
| Dark and not moving, doesn't respond to touch | Dying or dead |
| Restless, wandering, color shifting, not eating | Getting ready to pupate |
| Hard, shiny brown capsule | Pupa (normal) |

## Keeping the rest healthy

- **Remove dead hornworms immediately**, since they can foul the cup
- Keep cups **at room temperature**
- **Don't overcrowd**
- Keep **chow fresh**
- **Feed them off** in a reasonable time; they grow very fast

## Never use wild hornworms

Hornworms from tomato or tobacco plants are not safe feeders. They eat plants that contain toxic compounds and may carry pesticides or parasites. Only use hornworms raised as feeders.

## FAQ

**Why are my feeder hornworms turning black?**
They're usually dead or dying, often from temperature stress, heat, moisture, mold or lack of food.

**Can I feed black hornworms to my reptile?**
No. Remove them immediately.

**How do I keep feeder hornworms alive?**
Keep cups at room temperature, out of sun, not overcrowded, with fresh chow.

**What color is a hornworm pupa?**
Brown and hard, not black and soft.
