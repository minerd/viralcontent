# Why Is My Guinea Pig Coughing When Eating? Choking, Dust, or Something More

Guinea pigs don't cough much. So when yours coughs, gags or makes a "hacking" sound while eating, it's worth paying attention.

It can be something simple, like a bit of hay dust. It can also be the start of a respiratory infection, or, more urgently, choking. Here's how to tell.

*This is general information, not veterinary advice. Guinea pigs can decline quickly, so contact an exotic-animal vet if you're unsure.*

## Is it an emergency?

**Call a vet immediately if your guinea pig:**
- Is struggling to breathe or breathing with an open mouth
- Keeps gagging, stretching the neck, or pawing at the mouth
- Has food or liquid coming from the nose
- Seems distressed, weak or collapses

Guinea pigs can't vomit, so a blocked throat is serious.

## Common causes

**1. Dust from hay or pellets**
Dusty hay or crumbly pellets can make a guinea pig cough or sneeze while eating, the same way dust makes you cough.

**Clues:** a quick cough or sneeze now and then while eating hay, then normal. More often from the bottom of the bag.

**Fix:** shake hay out before serving (outside), buy good-quality, low-dust hay, and don't feed the powdery bits at the bottom of pellet bags.

**2. Eating too fast**
Some guinea pigs gulp food, especially pellets or treats, and briefly choke or cough.

**Fix:** offer pellets in a wider, shallow bowl, scatter a few, or feed smaller portions.

**3. Dry food getting stuck**
Dry pellets can occasionally lodge in the throat.

**Fix:** always have fresh water available, and make sure your guinea pig is drinking.

**4. Respiratory infection**
Guinea pigs are prone to respiratory infections, which can become serious.

**Clues:** coughing or sneezing at other times too, wheezing, clicking or crackly breathing, discharge from eyes or nose, fluffed up, less active, eating less.

**Action:** see a vet promptly.

**5. Dental problems**
Overgrown or misaligned teeth can make chewing and swallowing awkward.

**Clues:** dropping food, drooling, wet chin, weight loss, choosing soft foods.

**Action:** vet check. Dental issues are common in guinea pigs and treatable.

## What to do at home

1. **Watch closely** for a day. Is it one cough while eating hay, or repeated?
2. **Check the environment**: dusty bedding, drafts, ammonia smell from dirty bedding, cold room.
3. **Weigh your guinea pig** and keep a record. Weight loss is an early warning sign.
4. **Check droppings**: normal size and amount means they're eating.
5. **Listen to breathing** when they're resting. Any clicking or wheezing = vet.

## FAQ

**Why is my guinea pig coughing while eating hay?**
Often hay dust. Shake hay before serving and use good-quality hay. If coughing continues or other symptoms appear, see a vet.

**Can guinea pigs choke on food?**
Yes. Signs include repeated gagging, stretching the neck and distress. Seek veterinary help immediately.

**Is guinea pig coughing serious?**
Occasional coughs from dust may be minor, but guinea pigs are prone to respiratory infections, so ongoing coughing needs a vet.

**Can guinea pigs throw up?**
No. Guinea pigs can't vomit, which is why choking or blockages are serious.
