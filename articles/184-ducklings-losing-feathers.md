# Why Are My Ducklings Losing Feathers? Juvenile Moult vs Picking

Most pages on duck feather loss are about **adult ducks moulting**, or about drakes wearing the back of a hen's neck bare. Ducklings are a different stage, with a different normal.

**Ducklings change their covering twice on the way to adult plumage** — so patchy, scruffy, half-bald phases are common and usually fine. The question is whether what you're seeing is growth or damage.

## The normal pattern

1. **Down** (the fluff they hatch in)
2. **Juvenile feathers** push the down out, starting roughly from a couple of weeks old
3. **Adult plumage** follows, with the awkward in-between weeks that make ducklings look moth-eaten

During these changes you'll see:
- **Pin feathers**: waxy quills with a blood supply, often in bare-looking lines
- **Down caught** on new feather tips
- **Uneven patches** — chest and flanks feather up before heads and necks
- Birds **preening constantly** and looking generally untidy

This is growth, not loss. Ducklings in this phase need **more protein**, not less, and plenty of rest.

## When it isn't just moult

**Feather picking by other ducklings**
- Bare patches with **broken quills, red skin or blood**
- Always the **same bird**, or the same area (back, tail base, neck)
- You can sometimes see it happening

Common triggers: crowding, boredom, bright light 24 hours a day, too little protein, no access to swim/forage. Fix those, and separate any bleeding bird — ducklings target blood quickly.

**Protein shortage**
Feather growth is expensive. Ducklings on chick feed that's too low in protein, or on a diet padded with bread, scratch or treats, grow poor feathers and pick more. Use a proper waterfowl or multi-bird starter/grower, and remember ducklings also need **niacin** — short niacin shows up as leg problems rather than feathers, but it travels with the same cheap-diet pattern.

**Wet, dirty bedding**
Constantly soaked bedding ruins the feather coat, chills the birds and causes skin problems. Ducklings make water go everywhere; the bedding still needs to be dry and changed often.

**Parasites**
Mites and lice cause itching, over-preening and feather damage. Check at night around the vent and under wings, and look for tiny moving specks or clusters at feather bases.

**Rough handling or a tight brooder**
Feathers rubbed off against wire, a doorway, or by too many birds squeezing past the same spot.

**Illness**
A duckling that's losing condition, standing hunched, not eating, or has discoloured droppings alongside feather loss needs a vet, not a diet tweak.

## Quick checklist

- Are the patches **growing new pin feathers**? → moult, normal
- Is there **blood, broken shafts or redness**? → picking or parasites
- Is it **one bird only**, and is it the smallest? → bullying
- Is the **bedding wet** and the brooder crowded? → management
- What's the **protein level** of the feed? → upgrade it
- Any **specks moving** at the skin? → parasites

## What helps either way

- **Correct starter/grower feed**, with treats kept minimal
- **Dry bedding**, changed often, with water placed to limit splash onto bedding
- **Space** — ducklings outgrow a brooder fast
- **Supervised water access** for proper preening, then dry warmth afterwards
- **Dimmer light**, and darkness at night once they're established
- **Company**: never rear a single duckling if you can avoid it

## FAQ

**At what age do ducklings lose their down?**
Juvenile feathers start replacing down from around two weeks, and the change continues for weeks.

**Is it normal for ducklings to look patchy and scruffy?**
Yes — mid-change ducklings look untidy. Growing pin feathers in the bare areas confirm it's normal.

**Why is one duckling bald on its back or neck?**
That's usually picking by the others. Check for blood, separate the victim, and fix crowding, light, boredom and protein.

**Do ducklings need more protein while feathering?**
Yes. Feather growth is protein-hungry; a proper waterfowl grower matters most during these weeks.
