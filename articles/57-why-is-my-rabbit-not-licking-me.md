# Why Is My Rabbit Not Licking Me? (It Doesn't Mean They Don't Love You)

You've seen videos of rabbits licking their owners' hands and faces. Yours? Nothing. Maybe a sniff, maybe a nudge, and then off to the hay.

Here's the reassuring truth: **lots of happy, well-bonded rabbits never lick their humans at all.** Licking is just one way rabbits show affection, and not every rabbit uses it.

## What licking means in rabbit language

In a pair or group of rabbits, grooming each other is a big deal. It's affection, but it's also social: rabbits groom the rabbits they're bonded to, and grooming often goes up the social ladder, with one rabbit grooming another it respects.

So when a rabbit licks you, it's treating you as part of its little family. That's lovely. But it also means a rabbit that doesn't lick you might simply see the relationship differently, not badly.

## Common reasons your rabbit doesn't lick you

**1. It hasn't been long enough.**
Trust with rabbits builds slowly. Weeks, often months. Rescue rabbits or rabbits with a rough past can take much longer.

**2. Your rabbit sees you as the one in charge.**
When you stroke a rabbit's head, you're doing what a dominant rabbit does to a lower-ranking one: grooming them. Some rabbits happily accept that and don't feel the need to groom you back.

**3. Personality.**
Some rabbits are cuddly, some are independent, some are "near me but don't touch me" types. None of those are wrong.

**4. They're showing affection in other ways (see below).**

**5. Something's off.**
If a rabbit that used to lick you has suddenly stopped, and is also less active, eating less, or hiding, that's different. Sudden behavior changes can signal pain or illness. Rabbits hide discomfort well, so a vet check is worth it.

## Other signs your rabbit likes you

Look for these. Many are more common than licking:
- **Flopping** over onto their side near you, fully relaxed
- **Binkies**: joyful jumps and twists in the air
- **Circling your feet**
- **Nudging** you with their nose for attention
- **Soft tooth purring** (a gentle grinding) while you stroke them
- **Lying down next to you** or following you around
- **Coming to greet you** when you enter the room
- **Loafing** calmly with you nearby

If you're seeing several of these, your rabbit is bonded to you. Licking is a bonus, not the test.

## How to build trust (and maybe earn a lick)

- **Get down to their level.** Sit or lie on the floor and let your rabbit come to you. Towering over them feels like a predator.
- **Let them choose.** Don't chase or grab. Rabbits trust people who let them decide.
- **Most rabbits dislike being picked up.** Keep lifting to what's necessary.
- **Stroke the forehead and cheeks**, which most rabbits enjoy, rather than the belly or feet.
- **Keep a routine.** Same feeding times, same calm voice.
- **Use small treats sparingly** (a bit of herb or leafy green), mainly to reward them approaching you.
- **Just hang out.** Read on the floor near them. Boring time together builds trust.

Some owners find that once a rabbit is very comfortable, gently "grooming" them with strokes for a while prompts the rabbit to return the favor. No guarantees, but it's a nice way to spend time together.

## FAQ

**Why won't my rabbit lick me?**
Many rabbits don't lick humans even when bonded. It may be personality, time, or your rabbit showing affection in other ways.

**Do rabbits lick to show affection?**
Yes, licking is a grooming behavior that usually signals affection and bonding.

**How long does it take to bond with a rabbit?**
It varies widely, from weeks to months, and longer for nervous or rescued rabbits.

**Should I worry if my rabbit stopped licking me?**
If it comes with other changes like eating less, hiding or lethargy, see a vet, since rabbits often hide illness.
