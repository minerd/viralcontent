# Why Is My Mead Bubbling Over? Foam, Airlocks and How to Stop the Mess

You check on your mead and find foam oozing out of the airlock, sticky honey-yeast mess on the counter, and a very enthusiastic fermenter.

It's messy, but it's usually a good sign: **your yeast is working hard.**

## Why mead bubbles over

**1. Not enough headspace**
The most common reason. Active fermentation creates foam (called krausen) on top. If the vessel is filled close to the top, the foam has nowhere to go but up and out.

**2. Very active fermentation**
Warm temperatures, lots of healthy yeast, and plenty of nutrients can make the first days of fermentation very vigorous.

**3. Fruit and additions**
Fruit in melomels (fruit meads) can create a lot of foam and push up into a "cap."

**4. Stirring, degassing or adding nutrients**
Adding nutrients or stirring can release a sudden rush of gas and foam. It's often called a "mead volcano."

## What to do right now

1. **Clean up** around the fermenter and wipe the outside.
2. **Remove the airlock**, clean and sanitize it, and refill it.
3. **Replace it with a blow-off tube** if foam keeps coming: a sanitized tube from the top of the fermenter running into a jar of sanitizer or water. Foam can escape safely.
4. **Move the fermenter somewhere cooler** (within your yeast's recommended range) if it's warm.
5. **Put a tray** under it.

It's generally fine to let fermentation continue. The foam will settle as the most active phase passes, often within the first several days.

## How to prevent it next time

- **Leave plenty of headspace** during primary fermentation. Many brewers leave a good amount of empty space, especially for fruit meads.
- **Use a bigger vessel** for primary fermentation, then transfer to a smaller one for aging when fermentation slows.
- **Use a blow-off tube** from the start for vigorous batches.
- **Degas and add nutrients slowly**, a little at a time, to avoid eruptions.
- **Control temperature**. Cooler, steady fermentation is less explosive and often produces better-tasting mead.

## Is my mead ruined?

Almost certainly not. Losing some foam doesn't hurt the mead. Just make sure the airlock or blow-off tube is **clean and sanitized** so nothing unwanted gets in.

## FAQ

**Why is my mead foaming out of the airlock?**
Usually vigorous fermentation plus not enough headspace in the vessel.

**Is mead bubbling over a bad sign?**
No, it usually means healthy, active fermentation. Clean up and sanitize the airlock.

**What is a blow-off tube?**
A tube running from the fermenter into a jar of sanitizer or water, letting foam escape safely.

**How much headspace should I leave for mead?**
Enough to allow foam to rise during primary fermentation, especially for fruit meads. Many brewers use a larger vessel for primary.
