# Can You Eat the Beets From Beet Kvass? Yes — Here's How to Use Them

Every beet kvass recipe tells you how to make the drink. Almost none tell you what to do with the jar of pale, soft beets left behind.

**Yes, you can eat them.** They're fermented vegetables — safe, probiotic-ish, and perfectly usable. They just aren't what they were.

## What the leftover beets are like

- **Softer**, sometimes slightly slippery
- **Saltier**, because they've sat in brine
- **Milder and less sweet** — a lot of the sugar fed the ferment and a lot of the colour and flavour went into the liquid
- **Tangier**, with a lactic sourness

If your first batch was strong and short (a few days), the beets still have plenty left in them. After a long ferment, or a second or third brew from the same beets, they're mostly spent.

## Should you reuse them for a second batch first?

Most people get **one strong brew and one weaker second brew** out of the same beets. Some traditional recipes top the jar back up with fresh salted water for a second round.

A practical approach:
1. **First brew** — the good one
2. **Second brew** — top up with water and a little salt; expect it lighter
3. **Then eat the beets** — by now they've given up most of what they had

Going beyond that gives you thin, bland kvass and mushy beets.

## Six ways to use them

1. **Chopped into salads** — treat them like any pickled beet; good with goat's cheese, walnuts, apple
2. **Blended into hummus or a dip** — colour plus tang; thin with a splash of the kvass
3. **Folded into potato or grain salads** — they stain everything pink, which is half the appeal
4. **In a cold soup** — a quick fermented take on borscht; blend with the kvass, cucumber, dill and yoghurt or kefir, served cold
5. **Chopped into a slaw** with cabbage and carrot
6. **Roasted** — this kills the live cultures but drives off some of the salt and concentrates what flavour is left

A note on heat: cooking destroys the live bacteria. If you're after them specifically, use the beets **raw/cold**.

## Watch the salt

Fermented beets carry brine. Taste before you add salt to whatever you're making, and rinse them briefly if they're very salty. If you're watching sodium, treat them as a condiment rather than a side dish.

## When to throw them out

Bin them if you see or smell:
- **Fuzzy mould** (white, pink, green or black) on the beets or the surface
- A **putrid, rotten or solvent-like** smell (sour and earthy is right; rotten is not)
- **Slimy, ropy brine** that doesn't improve, together with off smells
- Beets that have turned **mushy and grey**

Beet kvass does often turn **slimy or thick** in early fermentation — that alone is a known phase and usually passes. Combined with a foul smell, don't risk it.

## Storage

Keep leftover beets **submerged in some of their brine, in a sealed jar, in the fridge**, and use them within a couple of weeks. Out of brine they dry and spoil faster.

## FAQ

**Do the beets have any nutrition left?**
Some. A lot of the water-soluble goodness and colour moved into the liquid, but they're still fermented vegetables with fibre.

**How many times can you reuse beets for kvass?**
Usually one strong brew plus one weaker second brew; after that they're spent.

**Can you cook them?**
Yes — roasting or soup works, though heat kills the live cultures.

**Can you eat them straight from the jar?**
Yes, if the ferment smells and looks clean. They're salty, so most people chop them into something rather than eat them plain.
