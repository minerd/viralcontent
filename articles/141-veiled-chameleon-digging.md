# Why Is My Veiled Chameleon Digging? (If She's Female, Read This First)

Veiled chameleons are tree-dwellers. They spend nearly all their time on branches and leaves. So a chameleon on the floor of its enclosure, **digging**, is doing something unusual, and the most important question is: **is it a female?**

*General information. See a reptile vet promptly if you suspect egg-binding.*

## Female veiled chameleons: probably laying eggs

This surprises many owners: **female veiled chameleons can produce eggs without ever meeting a male.** Those eggs are infertile, but she still needs to lay them.

**Signs she's getting ready to lay:**
- **Digging** or searching the floor of the enclosure
- Coming down from branches and **wandering**
- **Eating less** or refusing food
- A **rounder belly**, sometimes with visible lumps
- **Color changes** (females often show gravid coloring)

**What she needs: a laying bin**
- A container of **moist, packable sand or sand/soil mix** that holds a tunnel shape
- **Deep enough** for her to dig a full tunnel (keepers commonly use containers several inches to a foot deep)
- Placed on the enclosure floor with **privacy**: some cover around it
- Minimal disturbance while she digs and lays, which can take many hours

After laying, she'll usually cover the eggs. Offer water and food afterward, as she'll be depleted. Remove the eggs if you're not breeding.

## Watch for egg-binding

Egg-binding (dystocia) is a serious, potentially fatal condition where a female can't pass her eggs.

**Warning signs:**
- She **keeps digging** but doesn't lay over several days
- She **stops digging**, becomes **lethargic**, sits with eyes closed
- **Sunken eyes**, weakness, not drinking
- Has been gravid for a long time without laying

**If you see these, contact a reptile vet quickly.**

**Reducing egg-laying frequency:** many keepers manage female veileds with appropriate diet, feeding amounts, and temperatures to avoid constant large clutches. A reptile vet can advise.

## Male veiled chameleons digging

Males don't lay eggs, so digging usually means something else:
- **Looking for water**: dehydration is common in chameleons. Make sure there's enough misting or a dripper; chameleons usually drink droplets from leaves, not from bowls
- **Too hot or too cold**: check basking and ambient temperatures
- **Escape attempts or stress**: glass enclosures, reflections, or nearby pets can stress chameleons
- **Hunting** fallen feeders

## FAQ

**Why is my female veiled chameleon digging?**
She's most likely looking for a place to lay eggs. Females can lay infertile eggs without a male.

**Do veiled chameleons need a laying bin?**
Adult females do. Provide a deep container of moist, packable sand or sand/soil mix.

**What are signs of egg-binding in chameleons?**
Digging without laying, lethargy, closed or sunken eyes, and weakness. See a vet quickly.

**Why is my male chameleon on the floor?**
He may be thirsty, too hot or cold, stressed, or hunting fallen insects.
