# Is It Normal for Ducklings to Sneeze? When It's Fine and When to Worry

Ducklings are messy. They dunk their bills in water, shovel food, splash everything, and yes, they sneeze. A lot, sometimes.

**Occasional sneezing is usually normal in ducklings.** But persistent sneezing with other symptoms can mean a respiratory problem. Here's how to tell.

*General information. Contact a vet who treats poultry or waterfowl if you see signs of illness.*

## Why ducklings sneeze (the normal reasons)

**1. Clearing their nostrils**
Ducks have nostrils (nares) on the top of their bills. Ducklings dunk their heads in water specifically to **rinse food, dust and debris out of their nostrils and eyes**. Sneezing and head-shaking after eating or drinking is part of that.

**2. Dry food or dust**
Crumbly starter feed and dusty bedding can get into nostrils.

**3. Just drank or bathed**
Water up the nose leads to a sneeze or two, then all is well.

## The water depth rule

Ducklings need water **deep enough to dunk their whole bill and clean their nostrils**, not just sip. If they can't clean their nares, crusty buildup can cause problems.

**But be careful:** very young ducklings can get chilled or drown in deep water. Use a waterer designed for ducklings, or add marbles/stones to a dish so they can dunk their bills without climbing in.

## When sneezing is a warning sign

Be concerned if sneezing comes with:
- **Discharge** from the nostrils, or crusty, **blocked nares**
- **Bubbly or foamy eyes**, swollen eyes
- **Wheezing, rattling or open-mouth breathing**
- **Lethargy**, fluffed up, not eating or drinking
- **Several ducklings** sneezing a lot at once

**Possible causes:**
- **Respiratory infections**
- **Ammonia** from wet, dirty bedding
- **Moldy bedding or feed**
- **Very dusty environment**

**Action:** clean the brooder, check for mold, improve ventilation, and contact a vet. Separate sick ducklings to reduce spread.

## Keeping a healthy brooder

- **Change bedding often.** Ducklings make it wet fast, and wet bedding creates ammonia and mold.
- **Use low-dust bedding** (like large-flake pine shavings; avoid cedar, which is commonly advised against for birds and small animals).
- **Good ventilation** without drafts.
- **Fresh feed**; throw out anything damp or moldy.
- **Appropriate duckling feed**: ducklings need adequate **niacin**; many keepers use a waterfowl starter or add a niacin source as recommended.
- **Correct temperature** from a heat source, with room to move away if too warm.

## FAQ

**Why do my ducklings sneeze?**
Usually to clear food, water or dust from their nostrils, which is normal.

**Should ducklings have water deep enough to dunk their heads?**
Yes, they need to dunk their bills to clean their nostrils, but keep it safe so they can't drown or get chilled.

**When is duckling sneezing a problem?**
When combined with discharge, bubbly eyes, wheezing, lethargy or poor appetite.

**Can dirty bedding make ducklings sneeze?**
Yes. Wet, dirty bedding produces ammonia and mold that can irritate airways.
