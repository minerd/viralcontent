# Why Does My Sake Taste Like Vinegar? Storage, Spoilage and What Fresh Sake Tastes Like

Good sake can taste fruity, floral, rich, dry, creamy or savory. It can be a little tart. But it shouldn't taste like **vinegar**.

If yours does, something has probably gone wrong with storage, age, or (for homemade sake) fermentation.

## What good sake tastes like

Depending on style, sake can be:
- **Fruity and floral** (think melon, pear, banana)
- **Rich and ricey**
- **Dry and crisp**
- **Savory** with umami

Some styles have noticeable acidity, but it should feel balanced and fresh, not sharp and sour like vinegar.

## Store-bought sake: why it turns sour

**1. It's been open too long**
Once opened, sake is exposed to oxygen and starts to change. Over days to weeks, it can lose aroma and become flat, sour or stale.

**2. It was stored warm or in light**
Heat and light speed up changes in sake. A bottle kept in a warm cupboard or near a sunny window can develop off flavors.

**3. It's unpasteurized (namazake)**
**Namazake** is unpasteurized sake. It's fresh and lively, but more delicate. It should be **kept refrigerated** and is more prone to spoiling if stored warm.

**4. It's old**
Most sake is meant to be enjoyed relatively fresh. Some aged sakes exist, but most everyday bottles don't improve with years on a shelf.

**5. Spoilage organisms**
Certain bacteria can spoil sake, making it sour, cloudy or off-smelling.

## How to store sake

- **Unopened:** cool, dark place; **namazake in the fridge**
- **Opened:** reseal and **refrigerate**, and drink it within a reasonable time (many sources suggest about a week or two for best flavor)
- **Keep bottles upright**, away from light

**Discard sake that smells sharply of vinegar, looks unusually cloudy (when it wasn't a cloudy style like nigori), or has an off smell.**

## Homemade sake or doburoku

If you're brewing at home and your sake tastes like vinegar:
- **Acetic acid bacteria** may have gotten in, often due to **oxygen exposure** and warm temperatures
- **Sanitation** matters a lot
- **Keep fermentation cool** and limit air exposure once active fermentation slows
- **Don't leave headspace** open to air for long periods

If it smells strongly of vinegar, it's usually best to discard and start again.

## FAQ

**Why does my sake taste sour?**
It may have oxidized after opening, been stored warm or in light, or spoiled.

**How long does sake last after opening?**
Refrigerated, many sources suggest drinking it within about a week or two for the best flavor.

**Does sake need to be refrigerated?**
Opened sake and unpasteurized namazake should be refrigerated. Many unopened pasteurized sakes can be stored somewhere cool and dark.

**Can sake go bad?**
Yes. It can oxidize and spoil, especially after opening or when stored warm.
