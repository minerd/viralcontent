# Why Is My String of Hearts Not Pink? (It Might Be the Wrong Variety)

You bought a string of hearts because of those photos: trailing vines of little hearts blushing pink and cream. Yours? Green. Maybe with silvery marbling, maybe with purple undersides. But not pink.

Here's the thing most care guides bury halfway down the page: **most string of hearts plants aren't supposed to be pink.** And the ones that are need the right conditions to show it.

## Step 1: Figure out which variety you have

**Standard string of hearts** (*Ceropegia woodii*)
- Green leaves with **silvery marbling** on top
- Undersides often **purple**
- Stems purple-ish
- **Doesn't turn pink.** Green with silver is exactly right.

**Variegated string of hearts** (often sold as 'Variegata')
- Leaves with **cream, white or pink edges**
- Pink stems
- This is the one that turns pink, especially on new growth and in bright light

**Other named varieties** (like 'Silver Glory' and 'Silver Heart') have more silvery or differently shaped leaves. Their color can vary, but they're not typically the bright pink you see in photos.

If yours is the standard variety, congratulations: it's healthy and doing what it should. No amount of light will make it pink.

## Step 2: If it IS variegated, give it more light

Variegated string of hearts shows its pink best when it gets **bright light**. In low light:
- Pink fades to cream or green
- New leaves come in greener
- Vines get leggy, with big gaps between leaves

**How to boost the pink:**
- Move it to your **brightest window**. Many growers find some **gentle direct sun** (like morning sun) brings out stronger color.
- Introduce more sun **gradually** to avoid scorching.
- In dark months, a **grow light** helps a lot.

## Step 3: Other things that affect color

**New vs old growth.** Pink is often strongest on **new growth**. Older leaves may fade.

**Reversion.** Variegated plants can sometimes produce all-green growth. If you see a vine with plain green leaves, you can trim it back so the variegated vines keep growing.

**Mild stress.** Some growers notice more intense color with bright light and slightly drier soil, similar to how succulents "stress color." Don't overdo it; a healthy plant is more important than a pink one.

**Overwatering.** Wet soil and poor light often go together and lead to pale, weak growth. String of hearts likes to **dry out between waterings**.

## Quick checklist

- [ ] Do the leaves have cream/pink edges? If not, green is normal.
- [ ] Is it in your brightest spot?
- [ ] Does it get some gentle sun?
- [ ] Are you letting the soil dry between waterings?
- [ ] Is new growth coming in pinker than old growth?

## FAQ

**Why is my string of hearts green and not pink?**
Most likely it's the standard variety, which is naturally green with silver marbling. Only variegated types typically turn pink.

**How do I make my variegated string of hearts pink?**
Give it bright light, some gentle direct sun if possible, and let the soil dry between waterings.

**Why did my string of hearts lose its pink color?**
Usually not enough light. Pink often fades in lower light and on older leaves.

**Is the purple underside of string of hearts normal?**
Yes, purple undersides are common on the standard variety.
