# Why Do My Geese Honk at Night? Alarm Calls, Predators and How to Quiet Them

Search this and you get page after page about **wild Canada geese** flying over at midnight. That's not your problem. Your problem is the flock in your own yard setting off at 2 a.m.

**Kept geese honk at night mostly because they heard or saw something.** Geese are living alarm systems: that's exactly why people have kept them around farmyards for centuries.

## The main reasons

**1. Something is out there**
A fox, raccoon, stray dog, owl or cat near the pen will set geese off. The honking is loud, repeated, and usually all the birds at once. This is the most common cause of a sudden 2 a.m. racket.

**2. They can't see**
Geese have poor night vision compared with their daytime eyesight. A noise they can't place is treated as a threat. A sudden motion-sensor light switching on does the same thing.

**3. A bird is separated**
A goose shut out of the house, or one that can't see the rest of the flock, will contact-call until it's answered. This sounds more like back-and-forth calling than a full alarm.

**4. Normal flock chatter**
Geese are not fully asleep all night. Low honks, murmurs and short exchanges when one bird shifts position are normal and usually brief.

**5. Breeding season**
In spring, ganders are far more reactive. The same noise that got no reaction in October gets a full alarm in March.

## How to tell which one it is

- **All birds, loud, repeated, sustained** → something is near the pen. Go look.
- **One bird calling, another answering** → separation. Check nobody is shut out or trapped.
- **A few soft honks, then quiet** → normal night chatter. Leave them.
- **Every night at the same time** → look for a predictable trigger: a car, a timer light, a neighbour's dog, livestock moving.

## What actually reduces it

- **Shut them in a secure house at dusk.** A goose that feels safe inside walls alarms less than one in open space.
- **Check the perimeter** for digging, gaps and tracks. If a predator keeps visiting, the honking won't stop until it does.
- **Turn off or re-aim motion lights** pointed at the pen.
- **Don't keep a single goose.** Lone geese call much more. Two or more settle better.
- **Keep the flock together at night**, rather than splitting birds between pens.

## What won't work

- Trying to train them out of it. Alarm calling is not a bad habit; it's the breed working as intended.
- Removing water and food at night to "calm" them. This doesn't reduce noise and creates other problems.
- Covering the house completely with no ventilation, hoping to muffle them.

If night noise is a neighbour issue, the realistic fix is a solid-walled house, sited away from property lines, with a secure run and no predator traffic, rather than quieter geese.

## When to worry

- Honking with **flapping, panic and feathers in the pen** in the morning → a predator got in.
- One bird calling constantly and **not eating** the next day → check it for injury.
- Alarm calling that continues **through the day** → something is still around.

## FAQ

**Do geese sleep at night?**
They rest in stretches but stay alert, and one or more birds often stays semi-awake. That's why they react to noises at any hour.

**Why do my geese honk at nothing?**
They usually heard something you didn't: an animal outside the fence, or a sound they couldn't place in the dark.

**Will geese stop honking at night as they get older?**
They get used to routine sounds, so a settled flock in a familiar place alarms less. Breeding season always raises it again.

**Do single geese honk more?**
Yes. A goose with no flock contact calls much more, day and night.
