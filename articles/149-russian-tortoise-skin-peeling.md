# Why Is My Russian Tortoise's Skin Peeling? Normal Shedding vs Problems

Snakes shed their skin in one piece. Tortoises don't. They shed in **small bits and flakes**, which can make a perfectly healthy Russian tortoise look a bit ragged around the neck and legs.

**Most skin peeling in Russian tortoises is normal.** Here's how to tell when it isn't.

*General information. See a reptile vet if you notice signs of infection or illness.*

## Normal shedding

**What it looks like:**
- Thin, papery **flakes** of skin on the neck, legs and head
- Coming off gradually over time
- Healthy, normal-looking skin underneath
- Tortoise is **active, eating, and alert**

Growing tortoises shed more often. Some keepers notice more peeling after soaks, when flakes loosen.

**Don't pull** flakes off. Let them come off naturally.

## When peeling may signal a problem

**Dehydration or low humidity**
Very dry, cracked, or constantly flaky skin can mean the tortoise isn't getting enough hydration or the enclosure is too dry.
**Try:** regular **shallow, lukewarm soaks**, fresh water always available, and check that humidity is in a suitable range for Russian tortoises (many care sources suggest a moderate range, with a more humid burrowing area).

**Fungal or bacterial skin infections**
**Signs:** discolored patches, raw or red skin, thick crusty areas, bad smell, sores.
**Action:** see a reptile vet.

**Mites or parasites**
**Signs:** tiny moving specks, irritation, rubbing.
**Action:** vet guidance and enclosure cleaning.

**Burns**
Heat lamps too close can burn skin or shell.
**Action:** check that basking lamps are at a safe height, and see a vet for burns.

## What about the shell?

Peeling of thin, clear **scute layers** on the shell can be normal in some tortoises. But be concerned about:
- **Soft spots**
- **Pitting**, discharge or a bad smell (possible shell rot)
- **Deep flaking** revealing raw areas

These need a vet.

## Support healthy skin

- **Regular soaks** in shallow, lukewarm water
- **Fresh water** available daily
- **Burrowable substrate** that holds some moisture in one area
- **UVB lighting** suitable for tortoises, replaced as recommended
- **Proper basking temperature** and a cooler side
- **Varied diet** of appropriate weeds and leafy greens

## FAQ

**Is it normal for tortoise skin to peel?**
Yes. Tortoises shed skin in small flakes, especially on the neck and legs.

**Why is my Russian tortoise's skin so dry?**
Low humidity or dehydration are common causes. Soaks and humidity adjustments can help.

**Should I remove peeling skin from my tortoise?**
No. Let it come off naturally.

**When is tortoise skin peeling a problem?**
When it comes with discolored or raw patches, sores, bad smell, parasites or other signs of illness.
