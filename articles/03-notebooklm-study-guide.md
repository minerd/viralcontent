# How to Use NotebookLM to Study Anything (Without It Doing the Studying for You)

NotebookLM is the AI study tool I'd recommend to most students, for one boring reason: it answers from your stuff. You upload your lecture notes, readings, or slides, and it answers questions based on those, with citations back to the exact spot in your sources.

That matters more than it sounds. A general chatbot will happily give you a confident answer that doesn't match what your professor taught. NotebookLM is much less likely to wander off, because it's anchored to what you gave it.

But there's a trap. It's so good at summarizing that you can end up "studying" by reading summaries, and then blank on the exam. So this guide is about using it to make you do the thinking.

## Step 1: Upload the right things (and not too much)

Make one notebook per course or per exam, not one giant notebook for everything.

Good sources to add:
- Your own lecture notes (even messy ones)
- The assigned readings as PDFs
- Slide decks
- Any past exam or practice questions you have

## Step 2: Get the map, not the answers

Your first question shouldn't be "summarize this." Try:

> "What are the 5–7 main concepts across these sources, and how do they connect to each other?"

This gives you the structure of the course. Write that structure down yourself, by hand if you can. That act of rewriting is where memory starts.

## Step 3: Make it quiz you

This is the part most people skip, and it's the part that works.

> "Ask me one question at a time about [topic]. Wait for my answer. Then tell me what I got wrong and cite the source."

Answer from memory. No peeking. When you get something wrong, click the citation and read the original passage, not just the correction.

## Step 4: Use the Audio Overview for review, not first contact

NotebookLM can generate a podcast-style Audio Overview of your sources. It's genuinely good for a walk or a commute.

But use it after you've studied the material, not instead of it. Listening to it cold feels productive and mostly isn't. Listening to it after you've been quizzed fills in the gaps.

## Step 5: Find what you don't understand

Before an exam, ask:

> "Based on these sources, what are the most common misunderstandings a student might have about [topic]? Explain each one."

Then check yourself honestly. If one of those "common misunderstandings" is what you actually believed, you just found your weak spot.

## What NotebookLM is bad at

It's not perfect, and pretending otherwise doesn't help you.

- It only knows what you upload. If a key reading is missing, it won't tell you.
- Summaries can flatten nuance. If your professor emphasized a debate, the summary might present one side as fact.
- Citations help, but they don't replace reading the source. Click them.

## A 45-minute study session that works

If you want a simple routine:

1. **5 min:** ask for the concept map, write it down
2. **25 min:** quiz mode, answer from memory
3. **10 min:** read the original passages for anything you got wrong
4. **5 min:** ask for common misunderstandings, check yourself

Audio Overview goes on the walk home.

## FAQ

**Is NotebookLM free?**
There's a free version that covers the basics most students need. Check Google's current plan page for limits, since they change.

**Is NotebookLM better than ChatGPT for studying?**
For studying your specific course material, usually yes, because it answers from your own sources with citations. For general explanations of a topic you have no notes on, a general chatbot can be more flexible.

**Can my teacher tell if I used NotebookLM?**
If you use it to quiz yourself, there's nothing to tell. You're the one answering. If you paste its summaries into an assignment, that's a different story, and it's usually against the rules.

**Does it work in other languages?**
Yes, it supports many languages for both sources and questions.
