# Why Does My Jumping Spider Keep Falling on Its Back? Molting, Weakness and Warning Signs

Seeing your jumping spider on its back is scary. Seeing it happen repeatedly is worse.

Sometimes it's completely normal. Sometimes it's a sign your spider is weak or unwell. Here's how to tell, and what to do.

**First rule: don't flip it over or poke it.** If your spider is molting, disturbing it can cause fatal complications.

## Possibility 1: It's molting (normal)

Spiders grow by shedding their exoskeleton. During a molt, many spiders lie still, sometimes on their back or at odd angles, often inside or near their silk "hammock."

**Signs it's a molt:**
- Your spider built or has been sitting in a **thick hammock** for days
- It **stopped eating** recently
- It's **very still**, legs tucked or twitching slightly
- Afterward, you'll find a **shed skin** (exuviae)

**What to do:** leave it completely alone. Don't open the enclosure unnecessarily, don't mist directly onto it, and remove any live prey that could bother it. Newly molted spiders are soft and fragile for a while; wait until it's moving normally before feeding.

## Possibility 2: It's weak and losing grip

If your spider isn't molting but keeps **falling from walls or the ceiling** and landing upside down, it may be weak.

**Common causes:**

**Dehydration**
Jumping spiders drink droplets. Too little moisture can leave them weak and clumsy.
*Fix:* lightly mist the **side of the enclosure** (not the spider) so droplets form, a few times a week, and ensure good ventilation so it doesn't get stuffy.

**A difficult or incomplete molt**
Stuck bits of old skin on the legs can make climbing hard.
*Fix:* keep humidity reasonable and avoid handling. Some spiders recover; badly stuck molts can be fatal.

**Old age**
Many jumping spiders live around a year or two, and **mature males** often have shorter lives. Older spiders get slower, clumsier and may fall more.

**Toxins in the air**
Spiders are very sensitive to **aerosols, bug sprays, air fresheners, strong cleaning products, scented candles and incense**. Exposure can cause twitching, loss of coordination and death.
*Fix:* move the enclosure away from any sprays or fumes and ventilate the room.

**Injury**
A fall from a height can injure legs or the body.
*Fix:* reduce high falls by adding plenty of climbable decor and keeping enclosures appropriately sized.

## Possibility 3: Dirty or slippery surfaces

Jumping spiders can climb glass using tiny hairs on their feet, but **very wet, dusty or greasy surfaces** can reduce their grip. Clean enclosure walls occasionally (without chemicals) and avoid over-misting.

## When it's serious

Be worried if your spider:
- Is on its back **and not molting** (no hammock, recently eating)
- **Twitches or curls its legs** tightly underneath
- Can't right itself or walk normally
- Stops responding to movement

Sadly, options are limited for sick spiders, and few vets treat them. The best tools are **prevention**: good humidity, ventilation, no toxins, and minimal disturbance during molts.

## FAQ

**Why is my jumping spider upside down?**
It may be molting, which is normal. If it's not molting, weakness from dehydration, age, injury or toxins are possible causes.

**Should I flip my jumping spider over?**
No. If it's molting, disturbing it can be fatal. Leave it alone.

**How do I know if my jumping spider is molting?**
Signs include building a thick hammock, stopping eating, and staying very still for a day or more, followed by a shed skin.

**What is toxic to jumping spiders?**
Bug sprays, aerosols, air fresheners, strong cleaners, and smoke from candles or incense can all harm them.
