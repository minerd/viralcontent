# Can You Take Hydrangea Cuttings in Winter? Yes: How to Root Hardwood Cuttings

Most guides tell you to take hydrangea cuttings in late spring or summer, from soft, green new growth. But if you're pruning in late fall or winter and don't want to waste those stems, you can try **hardwood cuttings**.

**Yes, hydrangeas can be propagated in winter.** Success rates tend to be lower than summer softwood cuttings, and it's slower, but it's easy, cheap, and a nice winter garden project.

## What are hardwood cuttings?

Hardwood cuttings come from **dormant, woody stems** of deciduous shrubs, taken after the leaves have fallen and before new growth starts. The stems have stored energy and can root slowly over winter and early spring.

## Which hydrangeas work?

Deciduous hydrangeas can be tried, including:
- **Bigleaf / mophead** (*Hydrangea macrophylla*)
- **Smooth hydrangea** (*H. arborescens*)
- **Panicle hydrangea** (*H. paniculata*)
- **Oakleaf hydrangea** (*H. quercifolia*)

Results vary by type and conditions, so take **more cuttings than you need**.

*Note: some hydrangea varieties are patented, which restricts propagating them for sale. Propagating for your own garden is generally fine.*

## How to take hardwood cuttings

**When:** after leaf drop, during dormancy, on a day when stems aren't frozen.

1. **Choose healthy stems** from this year's growth, about pencil thickness.
2. **Cut sections about 6–8 inches (15–20 cm) long**, each with at least a couple of sets of buds (nodes).
3. **Cut straight across just below a node** at the bottom, and **at an angle just above a node** at the top. The angle helps you remember which way is up.
4. **Dip the bottom end** in rooting hormone (optional, but it can improve success).

## Where to plant them

**Option 1: Pots**
- Fill a deep pot with a **free-draining mix** (for example, potting mix with lots of perlite or grit).
- Insert cuttings so about **two-thirds is below the surface**.
- Keep in a **sheltered, cool spot**: an unheated greenhouse, cold frame, garage with light, or against a protected wall.

**Option 2: A trench outdoors**
- In mild climates, cuttings can go into a **narrow trench** of loose, gritty soil in a sheltered spot.

**Care over winter**
- Keep soil **barely moist**, not soggy
- Protect from **hard freezes** and heavy frost heave
- Be patient

## What to expect

- **Roots form slowly** over winter and early spring
- **Buds swell and leaf out** in spring, but leaves alone don't mean roots. Wait until you see roots from the drainage holes or feel resistance when you tug gently
- **Pot up or plant out** rooted cuttings in late spring or once they're well established

## Easier alternative: layering

If you have space, **layering** is very reliable for hydrangeas: bend a low stem to the ground, wound it slightly, pin it into soil, and let it root while still attached to the parent plant. Separate it once roots form.

## FAQ

**Can you take hydrangea cuttings in winter?**
Yes, from dormant hardwood stems, though success rates are usually lower than summer softwood cuttings.

**Can hydrangea cuttings root in water in winter?**
Winter hardwood cuttings generally do better in a free-draining mix in a cool, sheltered spot.

**How long do hydrangea hardwood cuttings take to root?**
Typically over winter into spring. Wait for clear signs of roots before transplanting.

**Which hydrangeas can be propagated from hardwood cuttings?**
Many deciduous types, including bigleaf, smooth, panicle and oakleaf hydrangeas. Results vary.
