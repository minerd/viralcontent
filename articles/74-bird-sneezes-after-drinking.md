# Why Does My Bird Sneeze After Drinking Water? Usually Harmless, Sometimes Not

Your budgie, cockatiel or parrot takes a drink, lifts its head… and sneezes. Sometimes twice.

Most of the time, that's exactly what it looks like: **a bit of water went up the nose.** But since sneezing can also be a sign of illness in birds, it's worth knowing the difference.

*General information only. Birds hide illness well, so if in doubt, see an avian vet.*

## The usual reason: water in the nares

A bird's nostrils, called **nares**, sit just above the beak. When a bird dips its beak deep into water, especially in a deep dish, water can get into the nares. A sneeze is how they clear it.

This is more likely if:
- The water dish is **deep**
- Your bird **dunks its head or face**, which some birds love doing
- Your bird **bathes** in its drinking water
- Your bird drinks **quickly or messily**

**Harmless water sneezes look like:**
- One or two sneezes right after drinking
- Then back to normal
- Clean, dry nares
- No other symptoms

## Easy fixes

- **Use a shallower water dish** or a water bottle designed for birds (introduce it gradually and make sure your bird learns to use it before removing the dish).
- **Offer a separate bath dish** so your bird doesn't bathe in drinking water.
- **Keep water fresh and clean**, changing it at least daily. Birds often dunk food in water, which makes it dirty quickly.

## When sneezing isn't harmless

Occasional sneezes are normal for birds, a bit like people. But sneezing can also be an early sign of a **respiratory infection** or other illness.

**See an avian vet if you notice:**
- Sneezing **often**, not just after drinking
- **Discharge** from the nares, wet or crusty
- **Stained or matted feathers** above the beak
- **Swollen or red nares**
- **Tail bobbing** with each breath, or breathing with an open beak
- **Clicking or wheezing** sounds
- **Fluffed-up**, sleepy, or eating less
- **Changes in droppings**

## Things in the environment that cause sneezing

- **Dust**, including feather dust from species like cockatiels
- **Aerosols**: air fresheners, perfumes, cleaning sprays
- **Smoke** of any kind
- **Scented candles and plug-in fresheners**

Birds have very sensitive respiratory systems. Keep strong fumes away from them. In particular, **overheated non-stick cookware** can release fumes that are extremely dangerous to birds, so cook with good ventilation and keep birds out of the kitchen.

## A quick way to tell

| What you see | Likely cause |
|---|---|
| Sneeze right after a drink, then normal | Water in the nares |
| Sneezes when you clean or spray something | Irritant |
| Frequent sneezing + discharge or other symptoms | Possible illness, see a vet |

## FAQ

**Why does my bird sneeze after drinking?**
Usually because water got into its nostrils (nares), especially from a deep dish or dunking.

**Is it normal for birds to sneeze?**
Occasional sneezing is normal. Frequent sneezing or sneezing with discharge or other symptoms is not.

**Should I use a water bottle instead of a dish for my bird?**
Some owners use both. If you switch, introduce the bottle gradually and make sure your bird can drink from it.

**What are signs of a respiratory infection in birds?**
Nasal discharge, tail bobbing, open-mouth breathing, clicking sounds, fluffed feathers, and eating less.
