# Why Is My Corn Snake Turning Grey? (Usually It's About to Shed)

Your bright orange or red corn snake suddenly looks dull, washed out, maybe grey or milky. Its eyes look cloudy blue. Is it sick?

**Most likely not. It's getting ready to shed.** Here's how to recognize a normal pre-shed and when a color change is worth worrying about.

## The pre-shed ("blue") phase

Snakes grow by shedding their whole skin. Before a shed, fluid builds up between the old and new skin layers. That makes:
- The **body look dull, grey or faded**
- The **eyes turn cloudy blue or milky** (snakes have a clear scale over each eye, called a spectacle, that sheds too)
- The belly may look **pinkish**

**This phase usually lasts several days.** Then the eyes clear up again, the color seems to come back a bit, and within a few more days the snake sheds.

## Normal behavior during pre-shed

- **Hiding more**
- **Refusing food**
- **Being more defensive**, because it can't see well with cloudy eyes

**What to do:**
- **Don't handle** it more than necessary
- **Skip feeding** until after the shed (many keepers wait)
- **Make sure humidity is adequate**. A **humid hide** (a hide with damp sphagnum moss or paper towel) helps

## After the shed

A good shed usually comes off in **one piece**, like an inside-out sock, including the eye caps. The snake should look **brighter** afterward.

**Check for stuck shed**, especially:
- **Eye caps** (retained spectacles)
- **Tail tip**

If there's stuck shed, a humid hide or a short, lukewarm, shallow soak can help. Don't peel stuck eye caps yourself; ask a reptile vet.

## When grey isn't a pre-shed

Be more concerned if:
- The snake **stays grey** for a long time **without shedding**
- There are **patchy discolored areas**, especially on the belly (could be **scale rot** from damp, dirty substrate, or **burns** from a heat source)
- It's **lethargic**, losing weight, or has **bubbles or mucus** around the mouth or nose
- Sheds are **frequently stuck or in pieces**

These call for a husbandry check (temperatures, humidity, cleanliness) and a vet visit.

## Some corn snakes are just… grey

Corn snakes come in many color morphs. **Anerythristic** ("anery") corns, for example, lack red pigment and are naturally grey, black and white. Some snakes' colors also shift as they mature. If your snake was sold as a morph, look up how that morph changes with age.

## Tips for easy sheds

- Keep a **humid hide** available
- Provide proper **temperatures** with a warm side and a cool side
- Keep substrate **clean and dry** outside the humid hide
- Add **rough surfaces** (rocks, branches) to rub against

## FAQ

**Why does my corn snake look grey and dull?**
It's usually preparing to shed. The eyes often turn cloudy blue too.

**How long before a shed does a corn snake turn grey?**
Usually several days to about a week before the shed.

**Should I feed my corn snake when it's in blue?**
Many keepers wait until after the shed, since snakes often refuse food then.

**Why is my corn snake still grey after shedding?**
Check the morph and age first. If it's patchy, lethargic or shedding badly, check husbandry and see a vet.
