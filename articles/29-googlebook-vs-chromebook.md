# Googlebook vs Chromebook: What's Different and Who Should Buy One

For 15 years, if you wanted a Google laptop, you bought a Chromebook. Cheap, simple, lives in the browser, great for schools.

Now there's a second kind. Google calls it **Googlebook**, and it's a very different pitch. Pre-orders are open now, and they go on sale in the US on **October 4, 2026**.

Here's what's actually different, and whether you should care.

## What a Googlebook is

A Googlebook is a laptop built on the Android technology stack, with foundations from ChromeOS, and with Google's Gemini AI built into the core of the experience. Google describes the shift as "moving from an operating system to an intelligence system."

In plain terms: it's a premium, AI-first laptop that runs Android apps natively and works closely with your Android phone.

## The key differences

| | Chromebook | Googlebook |
|---|---|---|
| **Price** | Often under $400 | From $899 |
| **Built on** | ChromeOS | Android, with ChromeOS foundations |
| **Minimum RAM** | Varies, often 4–8GB | 16GB |
| **AI** | Gemini features added on | Gemini at the core, dedicated AI chip (NPU) |
| **Target buyer** | Schools, budget, basic use | Premium buyers, Android phone owners |
| **Updates** | Varies by model | 10 years promised |

## The features Google is pushing

**Magic Pointer.** Wiggle your cursor and Gemini offers suggestions based on whatever you're pointing at. Point at a date, get a calendar suggestion. Point at an image, get options for it.

**Custom widgets by voice.** Ask for a dashboard, and Gemini builds one, pulling from the web or your Gmail and Calendar.

**Phone continuity.** Open your Android phone's apps and files from the laptop, and continue what you were doing on your phone.

**Glowbar.** A light strip for notifications and Gemini animations. Mostly a style thing, but it'll make them recognizable.

All come with at least 16GB of RAM and 256GB of storage. Android Authority reports launch bundles include 12 months of Google AI Pro, plus YouTube Premium and Adobe Photoshop offers. International launch follows on October 5 in Canada, the UK, Ireland, France, Germany and Australia.

## Who should buy a Googlebook

**Good fit:**
- You have an Android phone and want your laptop to work with it the way iPhones work with Macs.
- You use Google's apps (Gmail, Docs, Drive) all day.
- You want AI features built in, not bolted on.
- You like the idea of 10 years of updates.

**Probably not for you:**
- **You're on a budget.** A regular Chromebook or a mid-range Windows laptop is much cheaper.
- **You need specific desktop software** like full video editing suites, certain games, or work tools that only run on Windows or Mac.
- **You're deep in the Apple world.** A MacBook will fit your iPhone better.

## Should you wait?

It's a brand-new platform. First-generation products often have rough edges, and app support for the laptop form factor will improve over time.

If you're excited about it and have an Android phone, the Acer at $899 is a reasonable way in. If your current laptop works fine, there's no harm in waiting for real-world reviews after October 4.

## What about Chromebooks?

Google hasn't made the future of ChromeOS the focus of this launch, and Chromebooks aren't going anywhere today. For now, think of it this way: Chromebook is the budget and education line, Googlebook is the premium line.
