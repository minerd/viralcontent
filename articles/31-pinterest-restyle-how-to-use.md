# Pinterest Restyle: How to Redesign Your Room With AI (and Get Better Results)

Every Pinterest user knows the feeling. You save 200 beautiful living rooms, then look up at your actual living room and have no idea how to get from here to there.

Pinterest's new **Restyle** feature is built for exactly that gap. You take a photo of your own room, tell the AI what you want, and it shows you your space redesigned. Then you can click on individual items to swap or remove them.

Here's how it works, how to get it, and how to get results you can actually use.

## What Restyle does

According to Pinterest, Restyle lets you:

- **Photograph your room** and use it as the starting point
- **Ask for specific changes**: new furniture, a paint color, different lighting, plants, wall art
- **Ask for a whole new style**: Pinterest mentions looks like "bohemian," "industrial," "whimsical" and "dopamine"
- **Click individual items** in the result to swap, erase or keep editing them
- **Pull from your saved boards**, so suggestions can reflect the Pins you've already collected

The goal, from Pinterest's side, is obvious: help you see an item in your real space, so you're more likely to buy it. From your side, it's a free way to test ideas before you spend money or pick up a paintbrush.

## How to get it

Restyle was announced on September 17, 2026, as an **early preview in beta** for users in the **US and Canada**. Pinterest says it's coming more broadly in **October 2026**.

## How to take a photo that works

AI redesign tools are only as good as the photo you give them. A few things make a big difference:

- **Shoot from a corner.** You'll capture more of the room and give the AI a sense of depth.
- **Use daylight.** Turn on the lights too if the room is dim. Dark photos give muddy results.
- **Hold the phone level** at about chest height. Tilted photos make furniture look warped.
- **Clear the clutter** on surfaces. The AI may try to "design around" a pile of mail.
- **Take a few angles.** One view of a room rarely tells the whole story.

## Prompts that give useful results

Vague prompts ("make it nicer") give generic magazine rooms. Specific prompts give ideas you can act on. Try these:

**Testing one change**
- "Change the wall color to a warm off-white. Keep everything else the same."
- "Replace the sofa with a green velvet sofa of similar size."
- "Add a large plant in the empty corner by the window."

**Testing a whole style**
- "Restyle this room in a Japandi style, but keep my existing bookshelf."
- "Make this feel cozy for winter: warmer lighting, layered rugs, throw blankets."
- "Dopamine decor, but only in the accessories. Keep walls and sofa neutral."

**Working with a budget**
- "Update this room by only changing the textiles: rug, curtains, pillows."
- "What's the smallest change that would make this room feel brighter?"

**Renters**
- "Redesign this room without painting or drilling into walls."

The "keep X the same" part is the trick. It stops the AI from redesigning things you can't or won't change.

## What to watch out for

- **Scale can be off.** AI images don't always get furniture dimensions right. Measure before you buy anything.
- **Colors on screen aren't paint chips.** Always test a real sample on your wall.
- **It's a sales tool too.** Suggestions may lean toward products you can buy. That's fine, but compare prices.
- **It's an early preview.** Expect some odd results. That's normal for a beta.

## Is it worth trying?

Yes, especially if you're stuck between a few ideas. Seeing your actual room in "industrial" versus "bohemian" is far more useful than scrolling other people's homes. Just treat the results as a mood board, not a blueprint.
