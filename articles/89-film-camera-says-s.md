# Why Does My Film Camera Say "S"? What It Means (and What to Do If It's Stuck)

You loaded film, closed the back, and the counter says **"S"** instead of a number. Or maybe there's an "S" on a dial on top of the camera. Either way, it's not immediately obvious what it means.

There are two very different "S" meanings on film cameras. Here's how to tell which one you're looking at.

## Meaning 1: "S" on the frame counter = Start

On many film cameras, the frame counter shows **S** (for **Start**) before you reach frame 1. It's the stretch of film at the beginning of the roll that was exposed to light while loading.

**On manual cameras:** after loading film and closing the back, you usually **advance and fire a couple of blank shots** until the counter moves from S to **1**. Those first frames are fogged anyway.

**On many automatic point-and-shoots:** the camera advances to frame 1 by itself when you close the back.

**On instant cameras (like many Instax models):** the counter may show **S** after loading a new pack. The first press ejects the dark protective slide, and then the counter shows the number of shots left.

## Meaning 2: "S" on the mode dial = Shutter priority

On cameras with exposure modes, **S** often means **shutter priority**. You pick the shutter speed, and the camera chooses the aperture. (Some brands call it **Tv**, time value.)

If you see S on a dial with options like P, A, M, that's what it is. It has nothing to do with film loading.

## "My camera is stuck on S"

If the frame counter stays on S and won't move to 1, the film probably isn't advancing. Common reasons:

**1. The film isn't loaded correctly**
The film leader may not have caught the take-up spool. Open the back **only if you're sure the film hasn't advanced** (or in darkness, or sacrificing the first few frames), and reload carefully.

**2. The back isn't fully closed**
Many cameras don't start advancing until the back is latched properly.

**3. Dead or weak batteries** (automatic cameras)
Replace them.

**4. No film loaded**
Some cameras stay on S or show a blinking signal when there's no film.

**5. Instant camera: the dark slide didn't eject**
Make sure the pack is inserted correctly, the batteries are good, and the camera is on.

**Manual camera check:** watch the **rewind knob** as you advance. If it turns, film is moving. If it doesn't, the film isn't being pulled through.

## FAQ

**What does S mean on a film camera counter?**
Usually "Start," the position before the first frame after loading film.

**What does S mean on a camera mode dial?**
Shutter priority mode, where you set the shutter speed and the camera sets the aperture.

**How do I get my film camera from S to 1?**
On manual cameras, advance and fire a couple of blank frames. Many automatic cameras advance to 1 on their own when you close the back.

**Why is my Instax on S?**
It usually means a new film pack is loaded. The first shot ejects the dark slide, then the counter shows shots remaining.
