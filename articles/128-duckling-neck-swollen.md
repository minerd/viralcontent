# Why Is My Duckling's Neck Swollen? Food Bulge, Air, or Something More

You pick up your duckling and notice a lump or swelling on its neck. Scary, especially with such a tiny bird.

**Often, it's food.** Ducklings eat fast and can pack food into their esophagus, which bulges visibly on the neck. But a swelling that doesn't go down, or feels like air, or comes with other symptoms needs attention.

*General information only. Contact a vet who treats poultry or waterfowl if you're unsure.*

## 1. Food bulge (usually normal)

Ducks don't have a true crop like chickens, but they can store food in an expandable esophagus. Ducklings that gobble their food, especially after being hungry, can have a noticeable bulge on the neck.

**Signs it's food:**
- Appears **right after eating**
- Feels **soft and doughy**, maybe a little lumpy
- **Goes down over the next few hours** as food moves along
- Duckling is **acting normally**: active, drinking, peeping

**What to do:** make sure water is always available, since ducklings need water to swallow food properly. Watch it go down.

## 2. Air under the skin

Sometimes a duckling can develop a **soft, puffy, air-filled swelling** under the skin. It can feel crackly or like a small balloon. This can happen after an injury affecting the air sacs or respiratory system.

**Signs:**
- Swelling that **feels like air**, soft and puffy
- Not related to eating
- May grow or move

**Action:** contact a vet. Don't try to puncture it yourself.

## 3. Impaction or blockage

If a duckling eats **bedding, long grass, string or other material**, it can get stuck.

**Signs:**
- Swelling that **doesn't go down** over several hours
- **Gaping**, neck stretching, trouble swallowing
- Not eating, drooling, lethargy

**Action:** remove the cause (for example, if they're eating bedding, switch to something they can't swallow easily or ensure feed is always available), and contact a vet.

## 4. Injury or infection

Pecking from other birds, rough handling, or cuts can lead to swelling, abscesses or infection.

**Signs:**
- Warm, firm or painful swelling
- Wounds, scabs, redness
- Swelling getting bigger over days

**Action:** vet visit.

## What to do right now

1. **Note when it appeared.** Right after eating? Or randomly?
2. **Feel gently.** Soft and doughy (food) or puffy and airy?
3. **Check water access.**
4. **Watch for a few hours.** A food bulge should shrink.
5. **Call a vet** if it doesn't go down, grows, feels like air, or the duckling seems unwell.

## FAQ

**Why does my duckling have a lump on its neck?**
Often it's food stored in the esophagus after eating. It should go down within hours.

**Do ducks have a crop?**
Ducks don't have a true crop like chickens, but food can bulge in their esophagus after eating.

**What does air under the skin look like in a duckling?**
A soft, puffy swelling that isn't related to eating. See a vet.

**When is a swollen neck in a duckling an emergency?**
If the duckling is gaping, struggling to breathe or swallow, not eating, or the swelling keeps growing.
