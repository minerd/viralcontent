# Can You Put Aquarium Snails in a Pond? It Depends on the Snail (and Your Winter)

Your aquarium has extra snails. You have a garden pond. Moving them outside seems like a perfect solution: free algae cleaners, and fewer snails indoors.

**Sometimes that works. Sometimes the snails die in the first frost. And sometimes they can escape and cause problems.** Here's how to decide.

## Question 1: Will they survive your winter?

Many popular aquarium snails are **tropical**. They need warm water year-round.

**Generally tropical (won't survive cold winters outdoors):**
- **Mystery snails** and other apple snails
- **Nerite snails**
- **Malaysian trumpet snails**
- **Rabbit snails**

In a warm-climate pond they may be fine; in a pond that gets cold or freezes, they're likely to die when temperatures drop.

**Often hardier:**
- **Ramshorn snails** and **bladder/pond snails** can tolerate a wider range of temperatures, and some can survive cold. That's also why they can multiply fast.

**Pond-specific species** such as **trapdoor snails** are commonly sold for outdoor ponds precisely because they cope with cooler conditions.

## Question 2: Could they escape and become invasive?

This is the part people overlook. Ponds aren't sealed. Snails and eggs can be carried out by:
- **Overflow** during heavy rain
- **Birds** and other wildlife
- **Plants** you move or share

Some aquarium snails, especially certain **apple snails**, are considered invasive in parts of the world and are **regulated or banned**. Releasing aquarium species into the environment is illegal in many places.

**Before you move snails outdoors:**
- Check your **local and regional rules** on aquatic species
- Consider whether your pond **connects** to streams, ditches or other water
- If in doubt, **don't**

## Question 3: Are they good for your pond?

Snails eat algae and decaying plant matter, but:
- Fast-breeding snails can **explode in number**
- Some eat **pond plants**
- They won't fix an algae problem caused by too many nutrients or too much sun

## Safer alternatives

- **Buy snails sold specifically for outdoor ponds** in your area
- **Give extra aquarium snails** to a fish store, club or other hobbyists
- **Keep tropical snails indoors** in a spare tank or tub

## If you do move them

- Choose species suited to your climate
- **Acclimate slowly**: float them in a bag or container of pond water, gradually mixing, so they adjust to temperature and water chemistry
- Make sure the pond has shelter, plants and good water quality
- Don't combine new snails with **copper-based treatments**, which are toxic to snails

## FAQ

**Can mystery snails live in a pond?**
Only where the water stays warm year-round. They're tropical and won't survive cold winters outdoors.

**Can aquarium snails survive winter in a pond?**
Tropical species usually can't. Some ramshorn and bladder snails are hardier, and pond species like trapdoor snails are commonly kept outdoors.

**Is it illegal to put aquarium snails in a pond?**
It depends on the species and where you live. Some apple snails are regulated or banned. Check local rules.

**Do snails help keep a pond clean?**
They eat algae and debris, but they won't solve algae caused by excess nutrients, and some breed very quickly.
