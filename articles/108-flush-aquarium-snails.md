# Can You Flush Aquarium Snails Down the Toilet? (Please Don't: Here's What to Do Instead)

If you have a planted aquarium, you've probably had a snail explosion. One day there are three tiny snails, then suddenly there are hundreds on the glass.

It's tempting to scoop them up and flush them. **Don't.** Here's why, and what to do instead.

## Why flushing is a bad idea

**1. Snails can survive**
Many aquarium snails are hardy. Being flushed doesn't reliably kill them, and it's a stressful, drawn-out way to go if it does.

**2. Invasive species risk**
Aquarium species released into local waterways can become invasive, outcompeting native species or carrying diseases and parasites. Some snails, like certain apple snails, are regulated or banned in parts of the world because of the damage they cause.

Wastewater treatment isn't designed to deal with living aquarium animals, and releasing any aquarium species into the environment, directly or indirectly, is illegal in many places.

**3. It's not a solution**
If your tank has a snail explosion, flushing a batch does nothing about the cause. They'll be back.

## What to do with extra snails

**Give them away or trade them**
- Many **local fish stores** take common snails, or know someone who wants them
- **Local aquarium clubs** and online groups
- Snails are popular as **food for pufferfish** and some loaches, so hobbyists may want them

**Move them to another tank or container**
A spare jar or tub with some plants can hold extra snails while you rehome them.

**Humane euthanasia**
If you need to cull snails, ask an **aquatic vet** or an experienced **local fish store** about humane methods. Opinions differ on the kindest approach, so get advice rather than improvising.

## Fix the cause: why you have so many snails

Snail explosions are almost always about **food**.

- **Overfeeding**: extra fish food is snail food. Feed less, and remove uneaten food.
- **Decaying plants**: trim and remove dead leaves.
- **Too much algae**: reduce light hours and nutrients.

**Other control methods:**
- **Manual removal**: pick them off, or use a snail trap (a lettuce leaf or a piece of blanched zucchini overnight, then remove it with snails attached)
- **Snail-eating tankmates**: some species eat snails, but research compatibility first, especially if you keep shrimp
- **Avoid chemical snail killers** unless you understand the risks: many are harmful to shrimp and other invertebrates

## Preventing hitchhikers

Most pest snails arrive on **new plants**. Quarantine plants in a separate container, rinse them well, and check for eggs (jelly-like clumps) before adding them to your tank.

## FAQ

**Can you flush aquarium snails?**
It's not recommended. Snails can survive, it isn't humane, and releasing aquarium species can harm local ecosystems.

**What do I do with unwanted aquarium snails?**
Give them to a local fish store, club or other hobbyists, or move them to a separate container while you find a home.

**How do I get rid of snails in my fish tank?**
Reduce feeding, remove decaying plant matter, pick snails out manually, and use a vegetable trap overnight.

**Where do aquarium snails come from?**
Usually as eggs or babies on new plants or decorations.
