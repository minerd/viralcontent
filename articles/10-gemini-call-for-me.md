# Gemini Can Now Make Phone Calls for You. Here's What I'd Let It Do (and What I Wouldn't)

If you hate phone calls, this might be the most exciting AI feature of the year.

Google is testing a feature called **Call for Me** with Pixel 11 owners in the US who pay for Gemini. You tell Gemini what you need, and it calls the business for you. It can navigate the phone menu, wait on hold, and handle things like reservations. You can follow along with a live transcript and jump in at any point.

Right now it's a limited test, so most people can't use it yet. But this kind of feature tends to spread, and it's worth thinking about now: what should you actually hand off to an AI on the phone?

Here's my take.

## What it's great for

### Waiting on hold

This is the killer use case. Nobody wants to spend 40 minutes listening to hold music to ask the cable company one question. If the AI waits and gets you when a human picks up, or just handles a simple question, that's pure time saved.

### Simple bookings

Restaurant reservations, haircuts, checking if a store has something in stock, asking about opening hours on a holiday. Low stakes, clear outcomes, easy to check afterwards.

### Calls you've been avoiding

Everybody has a "I need to call about that" task sitting on their list for three weeks. If the AI makes the call easy enough that you actually do it, that's a win, even if the call itself isn't hard.

## What I wouldn't let it do (yet)

### Anything involving money disputes

Contesting a charge, negotiating a bill, canceling a contract with a cancellation fee. The exact words matter, the other side is trained to push back, and you want to know exactly what was agreed. Do these yourself, or at least watch the transcript live.

### Medical stuff

Booking a routine appointment? Maybe. Describing symptoms, discussing results, or anything where a detail might get lost? No. Call yourself.

### Anything legal or official

Government offices, insurance claims, anything where what you said might end up in a file. If you'd want a record of your own words, use your own words.

### Calls where the relationship matters

Calling your landlord about a problem, your kid's school, a small business you're a regular at. Sending an AI to those calls can feel cold, and it might cost you goodwill you'll want later.

## The etiquette question

Here's the part nobody seems to be talking about: **how does the person on the other end feel?**

A restaurant host who picks up and hears an AI booking a table probably doesn't care much. But a small business owner who gets five AI calls an hour asking "are you open today?" when the hours are on their website might feel differently.

A few principles I think make sense:

- **The AI should say it's an AI.** Most people want to know who, or what, they're talking to. [ADD: check and note whether Google's feature announces itself at the start of the call]
- **Don't use it for things you could look up.** If the answer's on the website, read the website.
- **Be ready to take over.** If the call goes sideways or the person seems confused, jump in.

## Why this matters beyond phone calls

Call for Me is part of a bigger shift. This same month, OpenAI released a model that can fill forms and update records on its own, and Microsoft rebuilt Copilot around long-running agents. AI is moving from answering questions to taking actions.

Phone calls are just the most human version of that. And they raise the same question every agent feature does: **what's the worst thing that happens if it gets this wrong?** If the answer is "I get a 7:30 table instead of 7," go for it. If the answer is "I agreed to something I didn't mean to," do it yourself.

## FAQ

**Who can use Gemini Call for Me?**
At the time of writing, it's being tested with US Pixel 11 owners who have a paid Gemini subscription. Expect this to change as Google expands it.

**Can the business tell it's an AI?**
Usually, yes, and whether the AI discloses itself is an important detail to check before you use it.

**Is it legal for AI to make phone calls?**
Rules on AI calls and recording vary by country and state, and they're changing. Services like this generally have to follow local laws on consent and disclosure.

**Can I take over the call?**
According to Google, you can follow a live transcript and take over at any time.
