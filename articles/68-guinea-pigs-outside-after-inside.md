# Can You Put Guinea Pigs Outside After They've Lived Inside? How to Transition Safely

Maybe you're moving house, redoing a room, or you've built a lovely outdoor hutch and run. Can guinea pigs that have always lived inside move outdoors?

**Yes, in the right conditions, but never suddenly, and never heading into winter.** Guinea pigs are sensitive to temperature changes, drafts and damp. A pig used to a steady, heated home can't cope with a sudden drop in temperature.

*General information. Your vet can advise on your specific guinea pigs, especially if they're young, old or have health issues.*

## The golden rule: timing

**Only move indoor guinea pigs outside in late spring or early summer**, when temperatures are mild and settled. That gives them months to adjust before weather gets cold.

**Don't move them outside in fall or winter.** An indoor guinea pig hasn't grown the thicker coat or built the tolerance that an outdoor guinea pig develops gradually.

As a rule of thumb, many care guides recommend guinea pigs be kept roughly between **65 and 75°F (18–24°C)**. They also overheat easily, so **hot weather** is just as much a concern as cold.

## Step-by-step transition (over 2–3 weeks)

**Week 1: short daytime visits**
- On mild, dry days, put them in a secure outdoor run for **an hour or two**.
- Provide shade, a hidey house, hay and water.
- Bring them in well before evening cools down.

**Week 2: longer days**
- Gradually extend to **most of the day** in good weather.
- Keep bringing them in at night.
- Watch appetite, droppings and behavior.

**Week 3: first nights**
- If nights are consistently mild, start leaving them out overnight in their **fully set-up shelter**.
- Keep checking on them. If the weather turns cold or wet, bring them back in.

Go slower for young, old or more delicate guinea pigs.

## What the outdoor setup needs

- **Weatherproof, insulated hutch** raised off the ground
- **Sheltered location**, out of wind, direct sun and heavy rain
- **Deep bedding** and plenty of hay to burrow into
- **Predator-proof** mesh and locks (foxes, cats, rats, birds of prey)
- **Space to exercise**, a large attached run
- **Shade** in summer, and protection from extreme heat
- **Always at least two guinea pigs.** They're social and huddle for warmth.

## Signs they're not coping

Bring them back inside and watch closely if you see:
- **Eating less** or fewer droppings
- **Hunching**, puffed-up fur, shivering
- **Sneezing, crusty eyes or noisy breathing** (respiratory infections are a common risk in cold, damp conditions)
- **Lethargy**
- In heat: **panting, lying stretched out, drooling**

A guinea pig that stops eating needs a vet promptly.

## When it's better to keep them inside

- Your climate has **harsh winters or very hot summers**
- You can't provide a sheltered, insulated hutch
- Your guinea pigs are **very young, elderly, or have health conditions**
- You have **just one guinea pig**

Many owners use a mix: outdoor run time on nice days, indoor living the rest of the time. That often gives guinea pigs the best of both.

## FAQ

**Can indoor guinea pigs live outside?**
They can in suitable climates, with a proper shelter and a gradual transition during mild weather. They shouldn't be moved outdoors in fall or winter.

**How do I acclimate my guinea pig to outdoors?**
Over two to three weeks, start with short daytime visits in mild weather, then extend the time, and only then move to overnight stays.

**What temperature is too cold for guinea pigs outside?**
Many care guides recommend keeping guinea pigs around 65–75°F (18–24°C). Below that, they need extra protection or indoor housing.

**Can guinea pigs go outside in the fall?**
Supervised daytime run time on mild days is fine, but it's not a good time to move indoor guinea pigs outside permanently.
