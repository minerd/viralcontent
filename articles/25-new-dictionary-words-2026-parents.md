# Looksmaxxing, Crashout, Copium: The New Dictionary Words Your Kids Already Use

On September 15, 2026, Merriam-Webster added about 1,400 new words and definitions to its dictionary. Dictionary.com dropped thousands of its own on the same day.

If you have a teenager, you've probably heard half of them already, and nodded along without asking what they meant. Here's your cheat sheet.

## The internet-culture words

**Crashout**
Losing your temper or your composure in a big, dramatic way. "He had a total crashout when the Wi-Fi went down." Can also describe someone who's reckless or self-destructive.

**Copium**
A mix of "cope" and "opium." Used when someone is comforting themselves with a hopeful story that probably isn't true. "Saying the test didn't count is pure copium."

**FAFO**
Short for "f*** around and find out." Meaning: do something foolish and you'll face the consequences. Yes, it's in the dictionary now.

**Parasocial**
A one-sided relationship where you feel close to someone who doesn't know you exist, like a streamer, a celebrity, or increasingly, a chatbot.

**Rickroll**
Tricking someone into clicking a link that turns out to be Rick Astley's "Never Gonna Give You Up." Older than your kids, but it finally made the dictionary.

**IYKYK** (Dictionary.com)
"If you know, you know." An inside joke that only people in the group get.

**Engagement bait** and **reply guy** (Dictionary.com)
Engagement bait is a post designed to get reactions rather than say something. A reply guy is someone who replies to everything, especially to people who didn't ask.

## The appearance words (worth paying attention to)

**Looksmaxxing**
Trying to maximize your physical attractiveness. It can mean ordinary stuff like skincare, haircuts and working out. It can also mean extreme advice spreading online, from harsh diets to dangerous "hacks," especially aimed at teenage boys.

This is the one on this list worth a gentle conversation. Not "what's looksmaxxing?" but "what kind of looksmaxxing stuff shows up on your feed?" The answer will tell you a lot.

**Mogging** (not in this update, but everywhere)
Outdoing someone, often on looks. "He mogged everyone in the group photo." It comes from the same online world as looksmaxxing.

## The tech words

**Vibe coding**
Telling an AI what you want a program to do, in plain language, and letting it write the code. Your kid might be doing this for school projects.

**Compute**
Processing power and memory, used as a noun. "AI companies are fighting over compute."

**AGI**
Artificial general intelligence. A hypothetical AI that could learn and handle many different kinds of tasks, like a person. It doesn't exist yet, but people argue about it constantly.

**Meme coin**
A cryptocurrency based on an internet joke, like Dogecoin. Worth knowing about if your teen starts talking about "investing."

**Uncanny valley**
The creepy feeling when something artificial, like a robot or an AI video, looks almost human but not quite.

## The ones you'll probably use yourself

**Sunday scaries**
The dread you feel on Sunday night before the work or school week.

**California sober**
Avoiding alcohol and hard drugs while still using cannabis (and sometimes psychedelics).

**Cuffing season**
Colder months, when single people look for a short-term partner to get through winter with.

## Not in the dictionary (yet), but you'll hear them

- **6-7**: the most-searched slang of 2026. It doesn't really mean anything. That's the point. It's often said with a hand gesture, mainly to confuse adults. It's working.
- **Aura / aura farming**: someone's cool factor, and deliberately building it.
- **Clanker**: an insult for robots and AI, from Star Wars.

## Why dictionaries keep adding slang

People sometimes grumble that "FAFO" doesn't belong in a dictionary. But dictionaries don't decide which words are proper. They record the words people actually use. When enough people use a word, in writing, over time, it earns an entry.

As a Northeastern University linguist put it, technology gives us new things to talk about, but we mostly use the language tools we already have to talk about them. "Vibe coding" is just two old words stuck together in a new way.

## FAQ

**What new words were added to the dictionary in 2026?**
Merriam-Webster's September 2026 update includes looksmaxxing, crashout, copium, FAFO, parasocial, vibe coding, compute, AGI, meme coin and Rickroll, among roughly 1,400 additions.

**What does crashout mean?**
Losing control in a dramatic way, or behaving recklessly.

**What does looksmaxxing mean?**
Trying to maximize your attractiveness, ranging from normal grooming to extreme and sometimes harmful advice.

**What does 6-7 mean?**
Nothing specific. It's a viral phrase kids say, often to baffle adults.
