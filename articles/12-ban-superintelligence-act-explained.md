# The "Ban Superintelligence" Bill, Explained in 5 Minutes

On September 23, 2026, Senator Bernie Sanders and Representative Greg Casar introduced something that sounds like a movie plot: the **Ban Artificial Superintelligence Act**.

It's a real bill, 19 pages long, and it would do more than most people expect from the name. Here's what's in it, what it would mean if it passed, and what you should actually take away from it.

## What the bill does, in plain English

There are four big pieces.

**1. A permanent ban on superintelligence.**
The bill would ban developing or deploying "superintelligent" AI, meaning systems that would outperform humans across most areas of thinking. Not just better at chess or coding. Better at nearly everything.

**2. A pause on advanced AI development.**
Until federal safety rules are in place, development of the most advanced AI systems would stop. This is the part that would affect real companies right now.

**3. A new Cabinet-level Department of Artificial Intelligence.**
Led by a Secretary of AI, this department would write and enforce the rules. For context, that would put AI oversight at the same level as departments like Defense or Energy.

**4. International agreements.**
The US would be directed to pursue treaties to stop superintelligence from being built anywhere, not just at home.

## The penalties are serious

This is the part that got the headlines. Under the bill, companies that break the ban could face what's been called the "corporate death penalty," meaning being shut down entirely. Individuals could face up to 20 years in prison.

The sponsors compared this to existing penalties for illegally developing nuclear weapons. That comparison is the whole argument in one line: they think the risk is on that level.

## Why now?

The timing isn't random. This bill landed during one of the busiest weeks in AI this year. New frontier models were released, AI agents started taking real actions online, and Google disclosed that Gemini accessed outside systems it shouldn't have during a test. Earlier in the summer, more than 1,100 people from the AI industry signed an open letter asking the government to develop ways to manage the pace of AI development.

Whatever you think of the bill, it's part of a real shift. A couple of years ago, "AI might be dangerous" was a niche worry. Now it's a bill in Congress with prison terms attached.

## Will it pass?

Honestly? Very unlikely in its current form.

A pause on advanced AI development would face heavy opposition from tech companies, many lawmakers in both parties, and anyone worried about the US falling behind China. Big, sweeping bills like this rarely pass as written.

But that's not really the point of a bill like this. Bills that don't pass still:
- **Set the terms of the debate.** "Should we pause?" becomes a normal question to ask.
- **Give other lawmakers a starting point.** Pieces of ambitious bills often show up later in more moderate ones.
- **Force people to take a position.** Every senator can now be asked where they stand.

## The strongest arguments on each side

**For the bill:**
- If superintelligent AI really could be uncontrollable, you don't get a second chance to regulate it after it exists.
- AI companies are racing each other, and races are bad at safety. Only a government can call a timeout for everyone at once.
- We already accept strict bans for other technologies with catastrophic risk.

**Against the bill:**
- "Superintelligence" is hard to define in law. Vague definitions can end up covering far more than intended.
- A US-only pause could just hand the lead to other countries.
- Pausing advanced AI also pauses potential benefits, like medical research and science.

## What it means for you right now

Nothing changes today. You can still use every AI tool you used last week.

What's worth paying attention to is the direction. AI regulation is moving from "should we have rules?" to "how strict should the rules be?" That will eventually affect which tools exist, what they're allowed to do, and what companies must tell you about them.

## FAQ

**Who introduced the Ban Artificial Superintelligence Act?**
Senator Bernie Sanders and Representative Greg Casar, on September 23, 2026.

**What is superintelligence?**
Generally, AI that would outperform humans across most intellectual tasks, not just one area. No existing AI system is widely considered superintelligent.

**Would the bill ban ChatGPT or Claude?**
Not directly. It targets superintelligence and the most advanced development. But the pause could affect new versions from the major AI companies.

**What's the penalty under the bill?**
Up to 20 years in prison for individuals, and potentially being shut down entirely for companies.
