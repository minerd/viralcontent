# Why Are My Cherry Shrimp Dying After Molting? The White Ring of Death

If you lose shrimp again and again just after a molt — sometimes with a visible **white ring or gap around the body behind the head** — you're looking at a failed molt, not a disease.

**The usual cause is minerals and stability**, not something you can treat with medication.

## What's going on in a failed molt

A shrimp splits its old shell at the join behind the carapace and backs out. Two things stop that working:

- The new shell under the old one is **too weak or the old one too tough** to break cleanly
- The shrimp **can't get out** and dies partway, leaving the gap keepers call the **white ring of death**

Both trace back to water chemistry and the mineral supply over the preceding weeks.

## 1. GH — the main suspect

**GH (general hardness)** supplies calcium and magnesium, the raw material for a shell. Both extremes cause trouble:

- **GH too low** → weak new shell, shrimp struggles and dies in or after the molt
- **GH too high** → shells that are too hard to split, plus shrimp growing too fast

For neocaridina (cherry shrimp), keepers generally aim at a moderate GH — roughly **6–8 dGH** — with a stable **KH** of about 3–6 to hold pH steady. Measure it. Don't guess from a tap-water report.

Sources of minerals: a **remineralising salt** made for shrimp (used with RO water), crushed coral, cuttlebone, mineral stones, or a tap water that's already in range.

## 2. Swings, not values

Shrimp cope with a wide range of values; they do not cope with **changes** in them.

The classic killer is a **large water change** with water of a different temperature, GH or pH. Shrimp that look fine for a day or two then die mid-molt have often been triggered into molting by that change.

**Do instead:** small changes (10–20%), frequently, with pre-mixed, temperature-matched, remineralised water, added slowly (dripped in, or poured over a plant).

## 3. Ammonia, nitrite and nitrate

A shrimp just out of its shell is defenceless against poor water. Any detectable **ammonia or nitrite** is an emergency; shrimp tolerate far less than fish. Keep nitrate low with planting and modest feeding.

New tanks are the usual trap: shrimp added before the filter is fully cycled die in clusters, often at molt time.

## 4. Copper and other invisibles

Shrimp are extremely sensitive to **copper**. Check:
- Fish medications (many contain copper)
- Snail treatments and planaria/hydra products
- Plant fertilisers (micro mixes with copper, dosed heavily)
- Plants from a shop treated with pesticide — quarantine new plants
- Old copper plumbing, and any brass fittings

## 5. Diet

Shell building needs calcium and protein. A tank with nothing but algae wafers can run short. Feed sparingly but varied: shrimp food, blanched spinach or nettle, mulberry/catappa leaves, and a calcium source in the water.

## 6. Temperature too high

Warm water speeds metabolism and molt frequency, giving shrimp less time to build shell. Around **21–24°C (70–75°F)** suits neocaridina; the high 20s increases deaths.

## What to do this week

1. **Test** GH, KH, pH, ammonia, nitrite, nitrate — write down the numbers
2. Bring **GH into range slowly**, over days, never in one dose
3. Switch to **small, pre-mixed, matched water changes**
4. Remove any suspect **medication, fertiliser or decor**
5. **Feed less, more varied**, and add a leaf or cuttlebone
6. Stop adding new shrimp until the tank has run stable for weeks

## FAQ

**What is the white ring of death in shrimp?**
A visible gap behind the carapace where the shrimp got stuck mid-molt. It's a molting failure, usually from mineral or stability problems.

**What GH do cherry shrimp need?**
Roughly 6–8 dGH with KH around 3–6, kept stable, is a common target.

**Can I fix low GH with a big dose of mineral salt?**
No. Raise it gradually over several days — the swing itself can kill shrimp.

**Is it contagious?**
No. Multiple deaths at once point to water chemistry, a recent water change, or a toxin, not an infection.
