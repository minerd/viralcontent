# Can Dogs Get a Cold After a Bath? What Actually Happens (and How to Dry Them Right)

It's an old belief, for dogs and people: go outside with wet hair and you'll catch a cold.

The short answer for dogs is **no, a bath itself doesn't give them a cold.** Respiratory infections in dogs are caused by viruses and bacteria, often picked up from other dogs. Being wet doesn't create those germs.

But that's not the whole story. A dog that gets **properly chilled** after a bath can end up uncomfortable, and in some dogs, cold stress is a real concern. Here's what to know.

*General information only. If your dog is coughing, sneezing a lot, has nasal discharge or seems unwell, talk to your vet.*

## Why dogs shiver after a bath

Shivering after a bath is very common and usually harmless. Water evaporating from the coat cools the skin, and shivering is how the body warms back up. Some dogs also shiver because they're stressed about the bath itself.

It should stop once your dog is dry and warm. If shivering continues long after they're dry, or comes with other symptoms, call your vet.

## Which dogs are more at risk of getting too cold

- **Small and toy breeds**, which lose heat quickly
- **Short-coated or thin dogs** (think Greyhounds, Whippets, Chihuahuas)
- **Puppies**, who can't regulate body temperature as well
- **Senior dogs**
- **Dogs that are already unwell**

For these dogs, a wet coat in a cold house, or worse, outside in winter, deserves extra care.

## Signs your dog is too cold

- Shivering that doesn't settle once they're dry
- Hunched posture, tucked tail, seeking warm spots
- Lethargy or unusual weakness
- Cold ears, feet or body to the touch

Severe cold exposure can lead to hypothermia, which is an emergency. That's rare after an indoor bath, but more likely if a wet dog is let outside in cold weather.

## "But my dog started sneezing after the bath"

A few sneezes after a bath are usually just water or shampoo in the nose. If your dog develops ongoing **coughing, lots of sneezing, runny eyes or nose, or lethargy** over the following days, that's more likely an infection they picked up elsewhere (for example, from other dogs at the park or daycare), not the bath. Either way, that's a vet visit.

## How to bathe a dog safely in cold weather

1. **Warm the room first.** Bathe in a warm bathroom, not a cold garage or outdoors.
2. **Use lukewarm water.** Not hot, not cold. Test it on your wrist.
3. **Keep it quick.** Get in, wash, rinse thoroughly, get out.
4. **Towel dry hard.** Microfiber towels absorb far more water. Use two or three.
5. **Blow dry if your dog tolerates it.** Use a **low or cool setting**, keep the dryer moving, and hold it at a distance to avoid burns.
6. **Dry the undercoat.** Thick-coated dogs can feel dry on top and still be damp underneath.
7. **Keep them inside until fully dry.** No walks or backyard time with a wet coat in cold weather.
8. **Offer a warm spot** like a blanket or bed away from drafts.

## Alternatives when it's really cold

- **Spot-clean** muddy paws and bellies with a damp cloth
- **Brush thoroughly**, which removes a lot of dirt and loose hair
- **Waterless dog shampoo** or grooming wipes for touch-ups
- **Save full baths** for a warmer day if they aren't urgent

## FAQ

**Can dogs get sick from being wet and cold?**
Being wet doesn't cause infections, but getting chilled can stress the body, especially in small, young, old or short-coated dogs. Dry them well and keep them warm.

**Is it okay to let my dog outside after a bath?**
In cold weather, wait until your dog is completely dry, including the undercoat.

**Why is my dog shaking after a bath?**
Usually because they're cooling down or stressed. It should stop once they're dry and warm.

**How often should I bathe my dog in winter?**
Most dogs don't need frequent baths. Spot-cleaning and brushing can bridge the gap, and your vet or groomer can advise for your dog's coat type.
