# Why Is My Button Quail Growling? The Male Call That Sounds Like a Tiny Engine

Button quail (also called king quail or Chinese painted quail) are tiny, gentle birds. So when one of yours makes a low **growling** sound, a bit like a small engine or a gust of wind, it's confusing.

**The short answer: it's almost always a male calling.**

## What the growl is

Male button quail have a distinctive call. It often starts with a **low growling or rumbling sound** lasting a few seconds, followed by a series of **crows**, which are higher and louder.

Keepers describe the growl as:
- Wind blowing
- A tiny motor revving
- A low purr or buzz

Hens generally make **softer chirps and calls** and don't produce the same growl-and-crow pattern.

## Why males growl

**1. Calling for a mate**
Males call to attract females. A male without a hen may call much more.

**2. Announcing territory**
If a male can see or hear other button quail, he may call to say "this is my space."

**3. Breeding season**
Longer days and warm weather can increase calling. Many keepers notice males calming down as days shorten in fall.

**4. Excitement**
Sometimes males call when they're excited: new food, a new environment, or activity nearby.

## Using the growl to sex your quail

Hearing the growl-and-crow is one of the easier ways to identify a male. Combine it with:
- **Plumage** (in many color varieties, males have distinctive face and chest markings)
- **Behavior** (males often chase and court females)

## How to make a noisy male quieter

- **Give him hens.** A male with one or more hens often calls less. Many keepers house one male with several females.
- **Separate males** that can see each other, or block their view, to reduce territorial calling.
- **Keep nights dark.** Light at night can trigger calling. A dark, quiet room helps.
- **Stable routine** and not too much light for long hours.

## When a growl isn't a call

Rarely, rough breathing sounds can be mistaken for calling. See a vet familiar with birds if you notice:
- **Rattling or wheezing** with each breath
- **Discharge** from nostrils or eyes
- **Swelling** around the face
- **Lethargy** or not eating

## FAQ

**Why does my button quail make a growling sound?**
It's usually a male's call, often followed by crowing, used to attract mates or announce territory.

**Do female button quail growl?**
Generally not. The growl-and-crow call is typically a male behavior.

**How do I stop my male button quail from calling?**
House him with hens, keep him from seeing other males, and keep nights dark.

**Are button quail loud?**
Males can be surprisingly loud when calling, though they're quieter than many other quail and birds.
