# Human Atlas: A Free 3D Anatomy Explorer in Your Browser (No App, No Account)

If you've ever studied anatomy, you know the problem with textbooks: the body is 3D, and the page is flat. Good 3D anatomy apps exist, but the best ones usually come with a subscription.

**Human Atlas** is a new free alternative that's been climbing GitHub's trending charts this month, with nearly 4,000 stars since it was created in early September. It runs in your web browser. No download, no account.

## What it is

Human Atlas is an interactive 3D anatomy explorer. You can:

- **Orbit, zoom and click** on structures directly on the body
- **Toggle body systems** on and off, or use presets like skeleton-only or organs-only
- **Explode the model** from assembled anatomy into every individual piece
- **Search** by anatomical name
- **Isolate a structure** and read information about it
- Use it on **mobile**, with compact controls

## How detailed is it?

Quite. It includes:
- **2,234 individually selectable parts**
- **15 anatomical systems**
- **3,432 named concepts**

The anatomy data comes from **BodyParts3D 4.0**, a reference anatomy model of an adult male, licensed under Creative Commons (CC BY 4.0).

## How to try it

There's a live version at **human-atlas-seven.vercel.app**. Open it in any modern browser.

If you're technical and want to run or modify it yourself, the code is on GitHub under the MIT license. It's built with React and Three.js.

## Who it's great for

- **Students** in biology, nursing, physiotherapy, sports science or pre-med who want a free study tool
- **Teachers** who want something to put on a projector without buying licenses for a whole class
- **Artists** learning anatomy for drawing
- **Curious people** who want to know where their spleen actually is

## How it compares to paid anatomy apps

To be fair, big paid apps still offer things a new open-source project doesn't, such as:
- Detailed written lessons and quizzes
- Female anatomy models and variations
- Animations of muscles moving
- Clinical content and professional-grade references

Human Atlas is a **3D model explorer**, not a full course. For many students, that's exactly the missing piece: something free to rotate and click while reading their actual textbook.

## A quick study method using it

1. Read a section in your textbook or lecture notes (say, the bones of the arm).
2. Open Human Atlas and switch to the skeleton preset.
3. Find each bone, isolate it and rotate it. Look at where it connects.
4. Close the labels and try naming each bone from memory.
5. Use the search to check yourself.

Active recall with a 3D model beats reading labels on a diagram.
