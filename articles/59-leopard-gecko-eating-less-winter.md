# Do Leopard Geckos Eat Less in Winter? Normal Slowdown vs Real Problem

Your leopard gecko used to chase crickets like it was their job. Now it's colder out, and they're refusing meals, or taking two bites and walking away.

**Yes, many adult leopard geckos eat less in winter, and that can be completely normal.** But "can be normal" isn't the same as "always normal." Here's how to tell the difference.

*General care information, not veterinary advice. If you're worried, see a reptile vet.*

## Why leopard geckos slow down in winter

Leopard geckos come from dry regions of Afghanistan, Pakistan, northwest India and nearby areas, where winters get noticeably cooler. Even in captivity, many geckos respond to shorter days and slightly cooler rooms by slowing down: less activity, more hiding, smaller appetite.

Some keepers and breeders deliberately give adult geckos a cooler winter period. Others don't. Either way, a seasonal appetite dip in a healthy adult isn't unusual.

Males may also eat less during breeding season, even when they aren't paired with a female.

## What's normal

- A **healthy adult** eating less, or skipping some meals, for a few weeks
- **Weight staying mostly stable**
- A **plump tail** (leopard geckos store fat in their tails)
- Otherwise normal behavior: alert, clear eyes, normal shedding, normal poops when they do eat

## What's NOT normal

- **Steady weight loss** week after week
- A **tail getting noticeably thinner**
- **Juveniles** refusing food. Young geckos are growing and shouldn't fast for long.
- **Lethargy** beyond the usual winter laziness
- **Sunken eyes**, **stuck shed**, or **runny or no droppings** for a long time
- **Swelling**, **mouth problems**, or **regurgitation**

If you see any of these, don't blame the season. Book a reptile vet.

## Step 1: Weigh your gecko weekly

This is the single most useful thing you can do. Use a kitchen scale that measures in **grams**, weigh at the same time each week, and write it down.

A healthy adult in a winter slowdown usually holds its weight fairly steady. A gecko that keeps losing weight needs a vet, whatever the season.

## Step 2: Check your temperatures

Low temperatures are one of the most common reasons leopard geckos stop eating. They need warmth to digest food. If the enclosure gets colder in winter because your home is cooler, appetite drops.

Use a **digital thermometer with a probe** or a temperature gun, not a stick-on dial. Commonly recommended targets:
- **Warm side surface:** around 88–92°F (31–33°C)
- **Cool side:** around the low to mid 70s °F (21–24°C)
- **A modest night-time drop** is fine, but avoid long periods of cold

If your warm spot has drifted down as your house cooled, fixing the heat often fixes the appetite.

## Step 3: Keep offering food, without forcing it

- Offer food on your usual schedule, a little less often if they keep refusing
- Remove uneaten live insects so they don't stress or nibble your gecko
- Try a different feeder insect occasionally
- Keep fresh water available

Never force-feed without guidance from a vet.

## FAQ

**Is it normal for leopard geckos to not eat in winter?**
For healthy adults, eating less or skipping meals for a few weeks can be normal, as long as weight stays stable.

**How long can a leopard gecko go without eating?**
Healthy adults with good fat reserves can go some time without food, but weight loss or other symptoms mean it's time for a vet. Juveniles shouldn't go long without eating.

**Do leopard geckos brumate?**
Some adults naturally slow down in cooler months, and some keepers provide a cooler winter period. It's not required for pet geckos.

**Why is my leopard gecko not eating but acting normal?**
Often a seasonal slowdown or temperatures that are too low. Check your warm-side temperature and track weight weekly.
