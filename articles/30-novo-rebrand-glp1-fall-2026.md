# The Ozempic Maker Is Now Just "Novo." Here's Everything Else That Changed in Weight-Loss Drugs

If you haven't followed weight-loss drug news for a few months, a lot has changed. The company behind Ozempic has a new name, Medicare coverage has shifted, and you can now get GLP-1s as a pill.

Here's the catch-up, in plain English.

*This is general information, not medical advice. Talk to your doctor about whether any of these medications are right for you.*

## 1. Novo Nordisk is now "Novo"

On September 14, 2026, Novo Nordisk, the Danish company that makes Ozempic and Wegovy, announced it's rebranding as **Novo**. The legal company name stays Novo Nordisk, but "Novo" is what you'll see going forward.

CEO Mike Doustdar framed it around making healthcare "more responsive, relevant and easier to fit into everyday life." It comes with a push for speed and a sharper marketing strategy.

**Why now?** Competition. Rival Eli Lilly (maker of Mounjaro and Zepbound) has pulled ahead. UPI reports Lilly held more than 60% of the obesity drug market in the second quarter of 2026, compared with about 38% for Novo. A rebrand won't change that on its own, but it signals the company knows it's in a fight.

**Does anything change for patients?** No. Your medication, prescription and pharmacy don't change because of the new name.

## 2. Medicare now has a $50 GLP-1 program

This is the biggest change for many people.

Since **July 1, 2026**, a Medicare program called the **GLP-1 Bridge** lets eligible beneficiaries get certain GLP-1 weight-loss drugs for a **$50 copay** per prescription. It runs through **December 31, 2027**.

A few details that matter:
- The Part D deductible doesn't apply.
- The program runs separately from the normal Part D benefit.
- Eligibility has specific criteria. Reporting from CNBC describes it as including a BMI threshold plus a related health condition.
- The covered drugs have been reported to include Wegovy, the Zepbound KwikPen and the Foundayo pill.

**How to check if you qualify:** go to **Medicare.gov/glp1bridge** or call **1-800-MEDICARE**. Don't rely on social media posts about eligibility; check the official source.

## 3. GLP-1s now come as a pill

For years, the most effective GLP-1 weight-loss drugs were weekly injections. That's changing.

The FDA approved Eli Lilly's **orforglipron**, sold as **Foundayo**, on April 1, 2026, as a GLP-1 pill without the strict food rules of earlier oral versions. Novo has also launched an oral version of Wegovy.

For people who hate needles or found injections a barrier, that's a big deal. Pills and shots aren't identical, though, so the right choice is a conversation with your doctor.

## 4. What's coming next: CagriSema

Novo's next big bet is **CagriSema**, a weekly shot that combines semaglutide (the ingredient in Ozempic and Wegovy) with a second drug called cagrilintide. An FDA decision is expected in the fourth quarter of 2026.

Expect a lot of "CagriSema vs Zepbound" headlines when that decision lands.

## 5. The crackdown on compounded GLP-1s

In June, the FDA sent a wave of warning letters to companies over misleading claims about compounded GLP-1 drugs, the cheaper versions many telehealth companies sell.

If you're buying a compounded GLP-1 online, ask:
- Is the pharmacy licensed in your state?
- Does the company require a real consultation with a licensed provider?
- Are they claiming it's "the same as" a brand-name drug? (Compounded drugs are not FDA-approved.)

## FAQ

**Did Novo Nordisk change its name?**
It rebranded as "Novo" on September 14, 2026. The legal entity remains Novo Nordisk.

**Does Medicare cover Wegovy now?**
Through the Medicare GLP-1 Bridge, eligible beneficiaries can get certain GLP-1 drugs for a $50 copay between July 1, 2026 and December 31, 2027. Check eligibility at Medicare.gov/glp1bridge.

**Is there a GLP-1 pill for weight loss?**
Yes. Eli Lilly's Foundayo (orforglipron) was approved by the FDA in April 2026, and an oral version of Wegovy is also available.

**When will CagriSema be approved?**
An FDA decision is expected in the fourth quarter of 2026.
