# Why Is My White's Tree Frog Turning Purple? Normal Color Shifts vs Warning Signs

White's tree frogs (also called dumpy tree frogs) are known for their bright green color and sleepy smiles. So a frog that suddenly looks **purple, brownish or dull** can be alarming.

**Often, it's normal.** White's tree frogs can change color to a degree, like many tree frogs. But a color change combined with other symptoms can point to stress or illness.

*General information. See a vet experienced with amphibians if your frog shows signs of illness.*

## Normal reasons for color change

**Temperature**
Many frogs darken when cooler. A frog in a chilly room overnight may look browner or purplish, then brighten as it warms up.

**Resting and sleeping**
White's tree frogs are nocturnal. During the day, resting frogs can look darker than when they're active at night.

**Background and light**
Frogs can shift tone to blend with their surroundings. Dark backgrounds, dim lighting or brown branches can make them look darker.

**Humidity**
Dry conditions can stress frogs and dull their color.

**Before a shed**
Skin can look dull shortly before shedding. Frogs usually eat their shed skin, so you might not see it.

## Stress-related color change

A frog that stays dark, purple or brown for long periods may be stressed by:
- **Temperatures too low or too high** for the species
- **Humidity too low**
- **Too much handling** (amphibian skin is sensitive, and handling should be minimal)
- **Bright lights** or lack of hiding places
- **Tank mates** or a new environment
- **Water quality**: dirty water or chlorinated tap water in the soaking dish

## When to worry

Contact a vet if color change comes with:
- **Lethargy**, not moving, not climbing
- **Not eating**
- **Red or pink belly or legs** (can be linked to bacterial infection)
- **Patches, spots, lesions** or unusual skin texture
- **Excessive shedding** or skin that looks cloudy or peeling in patches
- **Bloating** or swelling
- **Sitting in water constantly**

Some amphibian diseases can spread and progress quickly, so early vet care matters.

## Quick husbandry checklist

- Correct **temperature gradient** for White's tree frogs, measured with a digital thermometer
- **Humidity** appropriate for the species, checked with a hygrometer
- **Dechlorinated water** in a shallow, clean dish, changed often
- **Hiding spots** and climbing branches
- **Minimal handling**; wash hands (no lotion or soap residue) if you must
- **Clean enclosure**, spot-cleaned regularly

## FAQ

**Is it normal for White's tree frogs to change color?**
Yes. They can shift between green, brown and purplish tones depending on temperature, light, surroundings and stress.

**Why is my tree frog brown during the day?**
Often because it's resting, cooler, or blending with its surroundings.

**When is a color change a sign of illness?**
If it comes with lethargy, not eating, red skin, lesions, excessive shedding or bloating.

**Can handling make my tree frog change color?**
Stress from handling can cause darker coloring. Keep handling to a minimum.
