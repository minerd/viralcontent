# Why Does My Quail Sound Like a Frog? A Guide to Quail Noises

If you keep quail, you've probably heard it: a strange, low, croaky sound that makes you look around for a frog. Then you realize it's coming from the quail pen.

Quail, especially **Coturnix (Japanese) quail**, make a much wider range of sounds than most people expect. A frog-like croak is usually one of them.

## Common quail sounds and what they mean

**The crow (males)**
Male Coturnix quail have a loud, distinctive call, often described as a burst of harsh notes. It's their version of a rooster's crow, used to announce themselves and attract hens. Young males often **practice** this call, and early attempts can sound gurgly, croaky or froggy.

**Soft trills and chirps (both, often hens)**
Contented quail make gentle chirps, trills and purring sounds while foraging or settling.

**Calling for others**
A quail separated from the flock may call repeatedly, a more urgent, repeated sound.

**Alarm calls**
Sharp, sudden sounds when startled, often followed by the whole flock freezing or flushing.

**Croaks and low grunts**
Many keepers describe a frog-like croaking from their quail. Often it's:
- A **young male** working up to his crow
- **Social chatter** or mild complaint (being crowded, disturbed)
- A **hen's** low call

## Using sounds to sex your quail

Sound is one of the easier ways to tell males from females once birds are several weeks old:
- **Crowing** is a strong sign of a male
- Hens tend to make softer calls
- Combine this with **feather patterns** (in many Coturnix varieties, males and females have different chest markings) and physical checks for a more reliable answer

## When a croak might be a health problem

A normal call happens occasionally and in context. Be concerned if the sound is actually coming with **breathing**:
- **Gurgling, rattling or clicking with each breath**
- **Open-mouth breathing** or gasping
- **Bubbles or discharge** at the nostrils or eyes
- **Swelling** around the face
- Sneezing, head shaking
- Fluffed up, lethargic, not eating

These can be signs of a **respiratory infection**, which can spread through a flock. Separate the bird and contact a vet who treats poultry or birds.

## How to tell the difference

| Sound | Likely meaning |
|---|---|
| Loud, repeated harsh call | Male crowing |
| Croaky, gurgly attempts in a young bird | Male practicing |
| Soft trills and chirps | Content |
| Sharp burst + flock freezes | Alarm |
| Croak/rattle with every breath | Possible respiratory issue |

## FAQ

**Why does my quail croak?**
Often it's a young male practicing his crow or normal social chatter. A croaky sound with every breath can signal illness.

**Do female quail make noise?**
Yes, usually softer chirps, trills and calls. Crowing is typically a male behavior.

**What does a male quail call sound like?**
A loud burst of harsh notes, often compared to a rooster's crow but shorter.

**Can quail get respiratory infections?**
Yes. Watch for gurgling breathing, discharge, swelling and lethargy, and contact a vet.
