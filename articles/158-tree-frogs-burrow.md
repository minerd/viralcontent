# Is It Normal for Tree Frogs to Burrow? Species Differences and Warning Signs

Tree frogs are built for climbing: sticky toe pads, long legs, a love of high perches. So finding yours buried in the substrate can be surprising.

**Occasional digging or hiding low isn't always a problem**, but frequent burrowing in a tree frog is often a sign to check humidity, temperature and stress.

## Why tree frogs might burrow

**1. Looking for moisture**
Amphibians breathe and absorb water partly through their skin. If the enclosure is too dry, a frog may dig into damp substrate to stay moist.

**2. Escaping heat**
Too warm, especially under a lamp, and a frog may head down and dig in to cool off.

**3. Hiding from stress**
New enclosures, handling, pets, bright lights, or not enough hiding spots up high can push frogs to hide low.

**4. Seasonal behavior**
Some tree frog species, like gray tree frogs in the wild, spend winter tucked under leaf litter or in sheltered spots. Pet frogs kept at cooler temperatures may show more hiding behavior.

**5. Shedding**
Some frogs hide during a shed.

## By species

**White's tree frogs**
Usually rest on branches, leaves or walls during the day. Occasional time on the ground is common, but frequent burying suggests checking humidity and temperatures.

**American green tree frogs**
Climbers that rest on leaves and glass. Burrowing is uncommon and worth a husbandry check.

**Gray tree frogs**
May hide under bark and leaf litter more often, especially when cooler.

## Husbandry checklist

- **Humidity** appropriate for your species, measured with a hygrometer
- **Temperature gradient** with no overheated spots
- **Dense foliage and hides up high**, not just on the ground
- **Clean, dechlorinated water** in a shallow dish
- **Moist, clean substrate** that isn't waterlogged
- **Minimal handling**

## When to see a vet

- **Not eating** and losing weight
- **Lethargy** or weak climbing
- **Skin changes**: redness, patches, excessive shedding
- **Bloating**
- **Staying buried constantly** even after fixing the setup

## FAQ

**Why is my tree frog burying itself?**
Often because it's too dry, too warm, or stressed. Some species naturally hide under leaf litter.

**Is it normal for White's tree frogs to be on the ground?**
Occasionally, yes. Frequent burrowing is worth a husbandry check.

**Do tree frogs hibernate?**
Some wild species overwinter in sheltered spots. Pet frogs are usually kept at stable temperatures.

**What humidity do tree frogs need?**
It varies by species. Follow a species-specific care guide and measure with a hygrometer.
