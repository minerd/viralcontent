# Why Does My Eclectus Grind His Beak? The Bedtime Sound Explained

Search this and you mostly find pages about **cockatiels** and **parakeets**. The behaviour is the same across parrots, but eclectus owners tend to notice it more, because an eclectus settling for the night is otherwise so quiet.

**Beak grinding is a contentment sound.** It is not the parrot equivalent of a person grinding their teeth from stress.

## What it is

The bird slides the lower mandible side to side against the upper, producing a soft, dry, raspy, crackling sound. You'll usually hear it:

- In the **evening**, as the light drops
- When the bird is **fluffed, on one leg**, eyes half closed
- After a **head scratch** or a quiet period with you nearby
- Just before it falls asleep

It often comes with other settling behaviours: a long body shake, a tail fan, a final preen, beak tucked toward the shoulder.

## Why they do it

Two things are going on at once:

1. **Comfort and winding down.** A relaxed bird grinds; a frightened or stressed one doesn't. It's one of the more reliable "this bird feels safe" signals you get.
2. **Beak maintenance.** The inner surface of the upper beak is ridged, and grinding keeps the lower beak shaped and smooth, alongside chewing and beak wiping.

So when your eclectus grinds in the dark on its sleeping perch, you're hearing the bird equivalent of a satisfied sigh plus a quick file of the nails.

## Eclectus-specific notes

- Eclectus are **routine-driven** and often need a long night (around 12 hours of dark, quiet sleep). Regular evening grinding usually means the sleep routine is working.
- They are also **undemonstrative** birds compared with cockatoos. Grinding, soft chirps and relaxed body posture are often the clearest affection signals you'll get.
- A newly rehomed eclectus that **starts** grinding after a few weeks is a good sign: it's settling.

## Beak sounds that are NOT contented grinding

Don't lump every beak noise together:

| Sound | Likely meaning |
|---|---|
| Soft raspy grinding at bedtime | Contentment, normal |
| **Loud beak clicking** while posturing, eyes pinned | Warning or excitement — back off |
| **Clicking/grinding plus head shaking** | Check for food, debris or discomfort in the mouth |
| **Clicking with laboured breathing**, tail bobbing, discharge | Possible respiratory problem — avian vet |
| **Grinding all day**, bird otherwise dull and quiet | Not normal; get it checked |

## When to see an avian vet

Grinding on its own is not a symptom. See a vet if you notice:

- **Overgrown, flaking or misshapen beak**, or uneven wear
- Grinding with **appetite loss**, weight loss or puffed, sleepy daytime posture
- **Breathing sounds**, nasal discharge, or tail bobbing
- **Sudden change** in a long-standing habit, in either direction

## FAQ

**Is beak grinding a sign of stress in parrots?**
No. Unlike human teeth grinding, it's associated with comfort and the wind-down to sleep.

**Why does my eclectus only grind at night?**
Because it's a pre-sleep behaviour. Most parrots do it in the last few minutes before dozing off.

**Do I need to trim a beak that grinds a lot?**
No. Grinding is part of how a healthy beak stays in shape. A misshapen or overgrown beak needs a vet, not a trim at home.

**My eclectus never grinds. Is something wrong?**
Not necessarily — some birds are quieter about it. Look at the whole picture: weight, appetite, droppings, activity and sleep.
