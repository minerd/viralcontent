# Is Jimothy the Raccoon Healthy? Short Spine Syndrome, Explained

In July 2026, a Seattle resident named Kiana Hall posted a video of an unusual raccoon in her Ballard neighborhood. Round. Compact. Almost no neck. In the video, she says what everyone watching was thinking: "What am I looking at?"

She named him **Jimothy**, because, as she put it, "He just looked like a Jimothy to me." The internet fell in love.

Since then, Jimothy has inspired tattoos, a mural, bobbleheads, rookie cards, an honorary University of Washington degree, and a Seattle City Council proclamation declaring it "Jimothy Summer."

But the most common question people search is simpler: **is he okay?**

## What's different about Jimothy

Animal experts believe Jimothy has a **spinal deformity, most likely short spine syndrome**. That's what gives him his rounded, compact, almost cartoonish body shape.

## What is short spine syndrome?

Short spine syndrome is a rare condition where the vertebrae (the bones of the spine) are compressed or fused, so the spine is much shorter than normal. The result is an animal that looks squashed: short back, little or no visible neck, and a body that seems to sit directly on the legs.

It has been documented in several animals, including dogs, and it's usually present from birth. It's not contagious, and it's not something the animal "caught."

How much it affects an animal varies. Some have mobility problems or pain. Others adapt remarkably well and live fairly normal lives.

## So, is Jimothy healthy?

Good news: according to experts, Jimothy is **"likely healthy for his species and appears to have no issues with mobility or pain."**

The **Washington Department of Fish and Wildlife** said he appears healthy without any human intervention. He moves around, finds food and behaves like a normal raccoon.

Part of the credit goes to his mother. Experts say her care is what helped him survive when he was young, since an animal that looks different can be more vulnerable early in life.

## Please don't go looking for him

This is the part officials keep repeating, and it's important.

**Don't approach Jimothy, don't feed him, and don't try to get close for a photo.** Wildlife officials warn that wild raccoons can carry bacteria and parasites that can spread to humans. And animals that get used to people can become bold around them, which usually ends badly for the animal.

Loving Jimothy means leaving him alone. Enjoy the videos, the mural and the merch instead.

## Why Jimothy went viral

A few reasons:
- **He looks like a cartoon.** People genuinely couldn't believe he was real at first.
- **He's an underdog.** An animal born different who's thriving anyway is a story people love.
- **Local pride.** Seattle adopted him fast, from murals to a city proclamation.
- **It's wholesome.** In a year of heavy news, a round raccoon living his best life is a break.

## FAQ

Experts believe he has a spinal deformity, likely short spine syndrome, which gives him a short, rounded body.

**Is Jimothy the raccoon healthy?**
Experts say he is likely healthy for his species and shows no signs of mobility problems or pain.

**Where does Jimothy the raccoon live?**
In the Ballard neighborhood of Seattle.

**Can I visit or feed Jimothy?**
No. Officials urge people not to approach or feed him. Wild raccoons can carry diseases, and human contact can harm wildlife.
