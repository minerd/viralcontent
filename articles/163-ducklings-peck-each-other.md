# Is It Normal for Ducklings to Peck Each Other? When It's Play and When to Step In

Ducklings explore the world with their bills, and that includes each other. A bit of pecking at siblings' feathers, feet and bills is common.

**Some pecking is normal.** But pecking can escalate into feather pulling and injuries, so it's worth knowing when to step in.

## Normal pecking

- **Curiosity**: ducklings test everything, including their brooder mates
- **Food on each other**: crumbs stuck to feathers or bills get pecked off
- **Pecking order**: young ducks sort out their social ranks with mild pecking and shoving
- **Preening help**: ducklings may nibble at each other's down

Normal pecking is **brief and mild**, without bleeding or bald patches.

## Problem pecking

Step in if you see:
- **Feathers or down being pulled out**
- **Bald patches** or scabs
- **Blood**: birds are attracted to red, and a small wound can turn into repeated attacks
- **One duckling being targeted** constantly
- Ducklings **hiding** or not getting to food and water

## Common causes of excessive pecking

- **Overcrowding**: ducklings grow fast, and brooders get cramped quickly
- **Boredom**
- **Heat**: an overheated brooder makes birds irritable
- **Diet**: a poor diet, including low protein or niacin, is often linked to feather pecking in poultry
- **Bright lighting**
- **Size differences**: mixing very different sizes or ages

## What to do

1. **Separate injured ducklings** until they heal. Clean wounds and keep them covered or treated so red isn't visible.
2. **Give more space.** Upgrade the brooder as they grow.
3. **Add enrichment**: chopped greens to forage, safe objects to peck, supervised shallow water time.
4. **Check brooder temperature** and reduce heat as they grow.
5. **Use a proper duckling or waterfowl feed** with adequate niacin.
6. **Reduce bright light**; some keepers use a red heat lamp or lower-intensity lighting.
7. **Reintroduce slowly**, under supervision, once healed.

## FAQ

**Why are my ducklings pecking each other?**
Usually curiosity, food on each other's feathers, or sorting out pecking order. Crowding, boredom and heat can make it worse.

**Is it normal for ducklings to pull each other's feathers?**
Mild nibbling happens, but pulling out down or feathers, bald patches or blood are signs to step in.

**How do I stop ducklings from pecking each other?**
Give more space, add enrichment, check temperature and diet, and separate injured birds.

**Why do ducklings peck at red?**
Birds are drawn to red, so wounds can attract more pecking. Cover or separate injured ducklings.
