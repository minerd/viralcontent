# Why Are My Superworms Turning Black and Dying? 6 Causes and Fixes

Superworms are a popular feeder for bearded dragons, leopard geckos and other reptiles. They're also frustrating when a batch starts **turning black and dying**, often one after another.

When a superworm dies, it darkens quickly. So black superworms usually mean dead or dying ones. Here's what causes die-offs and how to prevent them.

## 1. The fridge (a very common mistake)

Many people store **mealworms** in the fridge to slow them down. **Superworms are different.** Cold temperatures can kill them.

**Fix:** keep superworms at **room temperature**, commonly in the range of the low 70s to low 80s °F (low to high 20s °C). Never refrigerate them.

## 2. Overheating in crowded tubs

Superworms packed tightly in deep bedding can generate heat, making the inside of the container much warmer than the room.

**Fix:**
- Keep bedding **shallow**
- **Don't overcrowd**; spread worms across larger or multiple containers
- Keep them away from heat sources and sunny windows

## 3. Too much moisture and mold

Wet food pieces, condensation, and poor airflow create mold and bacteria, which can kill worms quickly.

**Fix:**
- Offer **small pieces** of moisture food (like carrot or potato) and replace them often
- Remove moldy or mushy food immediately
- Use a container with **good ventilation**
- Change bedding if it gets damp or smelly

## 4. Too little moisture

Superworms need some moisture from their food.

**Fix:** offer fresh veggie slices regularly for hydration.

## 5. Old age and pupation

Superworms eventually try to **pupate**. Isolated superworms can pupate into beetles. Pupae naturally change color as they develop, but **dead pupae** turn dark and shriveled, often because the larva didn't have enough moisture reserves before pupating.

## 6. Contamination or chemicals

Pesticides, cleaning products, or contaminated food can kill feeders.

**Fix:** use clean containers and food, and keep them away from sprays.

## Quick care checklist

- [ ] Room temperature, **never the fridge**
- [ ] Shallow bedding, not overcrowded
- [ ] Good ventilation
- [ ] Fresh veggie slices for moisture, replaced often
- [ ] Remove dead worms and mold right away

## Is it safe to feed black superworms?

**No.** Remove dead or darkening superworms right away and don't feed them to your reptile.

## FAQ

**Why are my superworms turning black?**
They're usually dead or dying. Common causes include cold, overheating, moisture and mold.

**Can superworms go in the fridge?**
No. Unlike mealworms, cold can kill superworms. Keep them at room temperature.

**How do I keep superworms alive longer?**
Keep them at room temperature in shallow bedding with good ventilation and fresh moisture food.

**Why are my superworm pupae turning black?**
Dead pupae turn black, often because the larvae didn't have enough moisture before pupating.
