# Set Up a Family Safe Word Tonight: The 10-Minute Fix for AI Voice Scams

Here's the scenario. Your phone rings. It's your kid, or your mom, crying. They've been in an accident, or arrested, or they're stuck somewhere. They need money now, and please, please don't tell anyone.

It sounds exactly like them. Because it was built from their voice.

Voice cloning tools can now copy a voice from a few seconds of audio, and that audio is easy to find: a TikTok, an Instagram story, a voicemail greeting. Banks and credit unions have been warning customers about this all year. One widely reported case involved an Arizona mother who paid $50,000 to scammers using a clone of her daughter's voice.

The good news is that the best defense costs nothing and takes ten minutes. It's a word.

## What a family safe word is

A safe word is a word or short phrase that only your family knows. If someone calls in an "emergency" asking for money or help, you ask for the word. A real family member knows it. A scammer with a cloned voice doesn't.

That's it. No app, no subscription. The clone can copy how your daughter sounds. It can't copy something she's never said online.

## How to pick a good one

Most people's first idea is bad, so here's what to avoid and what to aim for.

**Don't use:**
- Pet names, street names, birthdays, school names. These are exactly the things people post about.
- Anything that's ever been in a caption, bio, or comment.
- Something so obvious that a scammer might guess it under pressure ("love you", "family").

**Do use:**
- Two random words that don't belong together. "Purple toaster." "Wednesday canoe."
- Something from an inside joke that never made it online.
- A word that's easy to remember when you're panicking. Simple beats clever.

Tip: say it out loud once. If it's awkward to say while stressed, pick something else.

## The 10-minute setup

1. **Pick the word** with one other family member. Don't do it in a group chat that might get hacked later. A phone call or in person is best.
2. **Tell everyone who might call in an emergency.** Parents, kids, grandparents, a partner. Include the grandparents especially. They're the most targeted.
3. **Agree on the rule:** "If anyone calls asking for money or urgent help, we ask for the word. No word, we hang up and call back."
4. **Practice once.** Seriously. Call your mom and ask for the word. It feels silly. It's also the only way the habit sticks.
5. **Don't write it anywhere online.** Not in notes that sync to a shared account, not in a text you might screenshot.

## Copy-paste message for your family chat

You can send this as is. It explains the idea without revealing the word itself:

> Hey everyone, quick one. There are scams now where people use AI to copy someone's voice and call family members pretending to be in trouble. To be safe, I want us to have a family safe word. I'll call each of you to tell you what it is (not putting it in writing). If anyone ever calls asking for money or urgent help, even if it sounds exactly like one of us, ask for the word. No word = hang up and call that person back on their normal number. Takes 2 min, promise.

## What to do if you get the call

Even with a safe word, you'll want a plan, because the call is designed to make you panic.

- **Slow down.** Urgency is the whole trick. A real emergency survives a two-minute callback.
- **Ask for the word.** If they dodge ("I don't have time for that!"), that's your answer.
- **Hang up and call back** on the number you already have saved. Not a number they give you.
- **Watch for the payment request.** Gift cards, crypto, wire transfers, and payment apps are the classic tell. Real police and real hospitals don't ask for gift cards.
- **Tell someone.** Scammers push secrecy ("don't tell Dad") because a second person breaks the spell.

## "Would my family really fall for this?"

Probably not on a normal day. But the call won't come on a normal day. It'll come at 2 a.m., or when you're driving, and the voice will be crying.

Smart people get caught by this, not because they're gullible, but because the scam targets the exact moment you stop thinking. A safe word works because it gives your brain one simple thing to do when everything else is shouting at you.

## Bonus: make yourself harder to clone

You don't have to delete your social media. But a few small changes help:

- Set your accounts to private if you post a lot of video of yourself talking.
- Replace a personal voicemail greeting with the default robot one.
- Be careful with random "is this you in this video?" calls where someone keeps you talking. Some scammers are collecting audio.

## FAQ

**How much audio does a scammer need to clone a voice?**
Some tools claim to need only a few seconds. More audio makes a better clone, but even a short clip can be enough to fool someone on a bad phone line.

**Can scammers also fake video calls?**
Yes, video deepfakes exist too, though they're harder to pull off live. The same rule works: ask for the word, then call back.

**Should businesses use safe words too?**
Many do. If your job involves approving payments, ask your company about a verification step for any urgent request that comes by phone or video.

**What if someone forgets the word?**
Then you hang up and call them back. The callback is the real protection. The word just makes it faster.
