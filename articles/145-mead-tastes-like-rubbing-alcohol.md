# Why Does My Mead Taste Like Rubbing Alcohol? Fusels, Heat and How to Fix It

You waited weeks, maybe months, for your first mead. You take a sip, and it tastes **hot**, harsh, like nail polish remover or rubbing alcohol. Not the smooth honey wine you imagined.

This is one of the most common complaints from new mead makers. Here's what causes it and what you can do.

## What's causing the harsh taste

The usual culprit is **fusel alcohols** (sometimes called higher alcohols). Yeast produces mostly ethanol, the alcohol you want. But when yeast is **stressed**, it produces more fusel alcohols, which taste hot, solvent-like and harsh.

**Common causes of yeast stress:**

**1. Fermenting too warm**
This is the big one. Fermentation creates its own heat, so the liquid can be several degrees warmer than the room. Fermenting at the top of (or above) your yeast's recommended temperature range tends to produce more fusels.

**2. Not enough nutrients**
Honey is low in the nutrients yeast needs. Without added yeast nutrients, yeast struggles.

**3. Too much sugar, too fast**
Very high-gravity meads (lots of honey) put yeast under strain.

**4. It's just young**
Young meads, especially strong ones, often taste "hot" before aging.

## Can you fix it?

**Time helps, but it isn't magic.** Many mead makers find that harsh, young meads **mellow significantly with aging**, often over months. Some of the "hot" character can soften as the mead ages.

**Things to try:**
- **Age it longer**, in a cool, stable place
- **Back-sweeten** (after stabilizing, if you know how) to balance harshness
- **Blend** with a smoother mead

Some very fusel-heavy meads never become great. Consider it a lesson for the next batch.

## How to prevent it next time

- **Control fermentation temperature.** Ferment at the lower end of your yeast's recommended range. Keep the fermenter away from heaters, and consider a water bath to buffer temperature swings.
- **Use yeast nutrients.** Many mead makers add nutrients in stages during the first days of fermentation, following a nutrient schedule.
- **Rehydrate yeast properly** according to the packet.
- **Pitch enough healthy yeast.**
- **Degas** gently in the early days of fermentation, which some makers find reduces stress.
- **Don't rush.** Give mead time to finish and age.

## Is it safe?

A hot, harsh-tasting mead is a quality issue, not a spoilage one. That said, fusel-heavy drinks are often reported to feel harsher, and like any alcohol, drink responsibly.

## FAQ

**Why does my homemade mead taste like alcohol?**
Often because of fusel alcohols produced by stressed yeast, usually from warm fermentation or lack of nutrients.

**Will my mead get better with age?**
Often, yes. Young meads can mellow significantly over months.

**What temperature should mead ferment at?**
Within your yeast's recommended range, ideally toward the lower end. Check your yeast's specifications.

**Do I need yeast nutrient for mead?**
Most mead makers use yeast nutrients because honey lacks the nutrients yeast needs.
