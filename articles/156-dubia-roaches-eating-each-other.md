# Why Are My Dubia Roaches Eating Each Other? Causes and Fixes for Your Colony

Dubia roaches are popular feeders because they're easy to keep and breed. So finding roaches with chewed wings, missing legs, or half-eaten bodies can be unsettling.

Some nibbling happens in any roach colony. But noticeable cannibalism usually means the colony is **missing something**.

## Why dubia roaches eat each other

**1. Not enough protein**
The most common cause. Roaches that don't get enough protein may look for it elsewhere, including in their colony mates.

**2. Not enough moisture**
Dehydrated roaches may chew on softer roaches for moisture.

**3. Dead roaches**
Roaches will scavenge dead colony members. That's normal clean-up behavior, not necessarily a sign of a problem, but lots of dead roaches is its own warning sign.

**4. Freshly molted roaches**
Right after molting, roaches are **white and soft**. They're vulnerable, and hungry colony members may nibble them.

**5. Overcrowding**
Too many roaches in too little space increases competition and stress.

**6. Wing nibbling**
Adult males have wings, and chewed wings are very common in colonies, sometimes from other roaches.

## How to fix it

**Improve the diet**
- Feed a **quality roach chow** or a balanced dry diet with adequate protein
- Supplement with fresh vegetables and fruits
- Some keepers add small amounts of higher-protein foods, following reputable guidance (avoid anything moldy or spoiled)

**Improve hydration**
- Offer **water crystals** or gel, or moist fruit and vegetables regularly
- Don't use open water dishes, which roaches can drown in

**Give them space**
- Add more **egg crate** vertical space
- Split large colonies into more bins
- Separate breeding adults from feeders if needed

**Clean regularly**
- Remove dead roaches and old food
- Keep frass (droppings) from building up too deep
- Watch for mold and mites

**Keep them warm enough**
Dubia roaches breed and stay active best when warm. Cold, stressed colonies are more prone to problems.

## FAQ

**Is it normal for dubia roaches to eat each other?**
Some scavenging of dead roaches is normal. Noticeable cannibalism usually means low protein, low moisture, or overcrowding.

**Why are my dubia roaches' wings chewed?**
Wing damage is common in colonies and can come from other roaches, especially when crowded or protein-hungry.

**What should I feed dubia roaches?**
A quality roach chow or balanced dry diet plus fresh fruits and vegetables for moisture.

**How do I give dubia roaches water?**
Use water crystals or gel, or moist produce. Avoid open dishes.
