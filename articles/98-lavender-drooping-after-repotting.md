# Why Is My Lavender Drooping After Repotting? (It's Usually Too Wet)

You moved your lavender into a nice new pot with fresh potting soil. Now the stems are flopping, leaves look limp, and it seems worse than before.

With most plants, drooping after repotting means "transplant shock, give it time." With lavender, there's usually a second, bigger issue: **the new soil is too wet and too rich.**

## Why lavender is different

Lavender comes from dry, sunny, rocky Mediterranean hillsides. It wants:
- **Fast drainage**
- **Lean soil** (not rich compost)
- **Lots of sun**
- **Soil that dries out** between waterings

Standard potting mix is designed to *hold* moisture. That's great for many houseplants, and a problem for lavender.

## The likely causes

**1. Soil staying wet (most common)**
Moisture-retentive potting soil, a pot without drainage, or a much bigger pot all keep roots wet. Lavender roots start to suffer quickly, and the plant droops.

**Clues:** soil still damp days after watering, drooping plus yellowing or graying leaves, soft stems at the base.

**2. Transplant shock**
Repotting disturbs roots. Some drooping for a few days is normal.

**Clues:** soil is drying properly, drooping started right after repotting, no rot signs.

**3. Not enough light**
If repotting came with a move indoors or into shade, lavender will sulk.

**4. Too much fertilizer or compost**
Rich soil and feeding can cause weak, floppy growth.

## What to do now

1. **Check the soil** a couple of inches down. If wet, **don't water**.
2. **Make sure the pot drains.** If it doesn't have holes, repot or drill holes.
3. **Move it to full sun.** At least 6+ hours of direct sun if outdoors; the sunniest window if indoors (indoor lavender often struggles without very bright light).
4. **Don't fertilize.**
5. **If it's very wet and getting worse,** gently unpot and check the roots. Healthy roots are firm and light-colored; rotten ones are dark and mushy. Trim rot and repot into a gritty mix (below).

## The right mix and pot for lavender

- **Soil:** a gritty, fast-draining mix. For example, a cactus/succulent mix, or regular potting soil mixed with a generous amount of **perlite, coarse sand or fine gravel**.
- **Pot:** only slightly larger than the root ball, with **drainage holes**. **Terracotta** is a great choice because it breathes and helps soil dry out.
- **Watering:** deeply, then let the top few inches dry out before watering again. In cool months, much less often.

## Will it recover?

If the problem was mostly transplant shock or wet soil caught early, lavender often perks up within one to a few weeks once conditions improve. Badly rotted plants may not recover, but cuttings from healthy stems can often be rooted to start again.

## FAQ

**Why is my lavender drooping after repotting?**
Most often because the new soil stays too wet or is too rich. Transplant shock and low light are other causes.

**Should I water lavender after repotting?**
Water once to settle the soil, then let it dry out before watering again.

**What soil is best for potted lavender?**
A gritty, fast-draining mix, such as cactus mix or potting soil mixed with perlite or coarse sand.

**Can drooping lavender recover?**
Often yes, if the soil dries out and it gets plenty of sun. Severe root rot is harder to fix.
