# Why Are My Feeder Hornworms Turning Green? (For Reptile Keepers)

If you search this question, you'll mostly find gardening articles about hornworms destroying tomato plants. That's not your situation. You bought **feeder hornworms** for your bearded dragon, chameleon or gecko, and now they're changing color.

Here's what's usually going on.

## Why feeder hornworms are blue-green to begin with

Feeder hornworms are usually raised on a **special chow** rather than tomato leaves. This often gives them a **bright blue-green or turquoise** color.

Hornworms that eat plants from the nightshade family (like tomato or tobacco) take in plant pigments that make them a more typical **green**.

## Why your hornworms are changing color

**1. Diet**
If your hornworms are eating something other than their usual chow, or the chow has changed, their color can shift.

**2. Approaching pupation**
As hornworms reach full size and get ready to pupate, their color can change. Keepers report hornworms turning a brighter or different green, or darker along the back, and becoming restless ("wandering") as they look for a place to burrow. Growth slows, and they stop eating.

**3. Normal variation**
Color can vary between batches and suppliers.

**4. Dying or sick hornworms**
Hornworms that turn **black, brown, mushy or deflated** are dead or dying. Remove them right away. Don't feed them to your reptile.

## Should you still feed them?

- **Healthy, active hornworms** that changed color from diet or growth are generally still used as feeders.
- **Hornworms preparing to pupate** are often fed off quickly by keepers, since they won't get bigger and will soon stop being useful as feeders.
- **Dark, discolored, limp or smelly** hornworms should be thrown away.

## Important: don't feed wild hornworms

**Wild hornworms from tomato or tobacco plants are not safe feeders.** They feed on plants that contain toxic compounds, and they may carry pesticides or parasites. Only feed hornworms raised specifically as feeders.

## Tips for keeping feeder hornworms healthy

- **Keep them at room temperature**, away from heat sources (they grow very fast when warm)
- **Keep the cup's chow clean** and remove frass (droppings)
- **Don't overcrowd**
- **Feed them off in a timely way**; they grow quickly

## FAQ

**Why are my hornworms turning green instead of blue?**
Diet changes or approaching pupation can shift their color.

**Can I feed my reptile hornworms that changed color?**
Healthy, active hornworms usually are. Discard dark, limp or smelly ones.

**Can I feed my reptile hornworms from my garden?**
No. Wild hornworms eat toxic plants and may carry pesticides or parasites.

**Why are my hornworms turning black?**
Usually they're dead or dying. Remove them immediately.
