# Why Is My Gargoyle Gecko Hanging Upside Down? (And the Floppy Tail Risk)

Gargoyle geckos are arboreal: in the wild they live in trees and shrubs, climbing and clinging to branches. So finding yours hanging upside down from the ceiling or clinging head-down on the glass is, in itself, pretty normal.

But there's one thing keepers watch for with this behavior: **floppy tail syndrome**.

## Why gargoyle geckos hang upside down

- **Climbing and exploring**: they're built for it
- **Resting**: many geckos rest in odd positions
- **Hunting**: waiting for insects to walk past
- **Seeking a temperature**: higher areas of the enclosure are often warmer
- **Feeling secure**: up high, near cover

## What is floppy tail syndrome?

Floppy tail syndrome (FTS) is a condition seen in captive geckos, especially **crested geckos**, and also reported in gargoyle geckos. The tail flops over the body at an unusual angle when the gecko is resting head-down on a vertical surface, rather than hanging down naturally.

It's commonly associated with geckos spending a lot of time **resting head-down on smooth glass**, which puts strain on the pelvis and tail base over time. In severe cases, it has been linked to pelvic or spine deformities.

**What it looks like:** when the gecko rests head-down on the glass, its tail flops **over its back or to the side** instead of hanging down.

## How to reduce the risk

The main idea: give your gecko **plenty of better places to rest** than bare vertical glass.

- **Add lots of branches**, cork bark and vines, especially **horizontal and diagonal** perches
- **Dense foliage** (live or artificial plants) to hide and rest in
- **Cover part of the glass** with a textured background, so there's less smooth glass to hang on
- **Resting spots near the top** where the gecko can lie horizontally

## When to worry

See a reptile vet if you notice:
- The tail **consistently flops over** the body when the gecko rests head-down
- **Kinks or bends** in the tail base or spine
- **Difficulty walking**, dragging legs, or weakness
- Soft or rubbery jaw or limbs (signs that can be linked to **metabolic bone disease**)

Metabolic bone disease is related to calcium, vitamin D3 and lighting. Make sure your gecko's diet and supplements follow a reputable care guide.

## Is my gecko happy hanging upside down?

If your gargoyle gecko is:
- **Active at night**
- **Eating well**
- **Pooping normally**
- Moving around the enclosure, not stuck in one spot

…then hanging upside down is likely just normal gecko behavior. Just make sure the enclosure gives it lots of alternatives to glass.

## FAQ

**Is it normal for gargoyle geckos to hang upside down?**
Yes. They're climbing geckos, and hanging upside down is common.

**What is floppy tail syndrome?**
A condition where a gecko's tail flops over its body when resting head-down, often linked to spending long periods on smooth vertical glass.

**How do I prevent floppy tail syndrome?**
Provide plenty of horizontal and diagonal perches, dense foliage, and textured backgrounds so the gecko rests less on bare glass.

**Can floppy tail syndrome be fixed?**
Changing the enclosure can help prevent it from getting worse. See a reptile vet if you're concerned.
