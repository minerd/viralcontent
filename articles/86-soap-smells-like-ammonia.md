# Why Does My Soap Smell Like Ammonia? (Milk Soap Makers, Read This)

You unmold your beautiful goat milk soap, lean in for a sniff, and get… ammonia. Sharp, a bit like cleaning spray. Not what you were going for.

If you made **milk soap**, this is common, and in most cases it **fades as the soap cures**. Here's what's going on.

## Why milk soap can smell like ammonia

When sodium hydroxide (lye) meets milk, it reacts with more than just the fats. Milk contains **proteins**, and when strong alkali breaks down proteins, it can release **ammonia**. Milk also contains **sugars**, which can scorch in the heat of the reaction, adding a burnt or "off" smell and a tan or orange color.

The ammonia smell tends to be strongest:
- **Right after mixing** lye into milk
- **During the first days** after unmolding
- In soaps with **lots of milk**
- When the batch got **hot**

## Does the ammonia smell go away?

Usually, yes. Most soap makers find the smell fades during the normal **cure time** (commonly around **4–6 weeks**) as the soap dries and finishes saponifying. Let bars cure somewhere with good airflow, spaced apart, out of direct sun.

If the smell is still strong after a full cure, see the other causes below.

## How to prevent it next time

**1. Freeze the milk.**
Freezing milk into cubes or slush before adding lye keeps temperatures down, which reduces scorching and smell. Add lye slowly while stirring.

**2. Add lye slowly and keep it cool.**
Work in stages, and set your container in an ice bath if needed.

**3. Try the split method.**
Some makers dissolve lye in part of the water, then add milk at trace or blend it into the oils instead of mixing lye directly into all the milk.

**4. Soap cooler.**
Avoid heating the batch. Some milk soap makers skip insulating molds, or pop freshly poured milk soap into the fridge or freezer for a day to prevent overheating (gel phase), which can reduce browning and smell.

**5. Use less milk.**
Replace part of the liquid with water if the smell bothers you.

## Other reasons soap can smell off

- **Rancid oils** (dreaded orange spots, old oils): smells stale, crayon-like or like old cooking oil, not ammonia
- **Fragrance issues:** some fragrance oils morph or react with lye
- **Contamination:** unclean equipment, spoiled milk

## Safety notes

- Always follow proper lye safety: goggles, gloves, ventilation
- Mix lye solutions in a **well-ventilated area**. The fumes, especially with milk, aren't pleasant
- Don't use soap before it's fully cured and tested

## Store-bought soap that smells like ammonia?

That's unusual. It may be old, stored in heat or damp, or the product may have gone off. If a bar smells strongly of ammonia and it's causing irritation, stop using it.

## FAQ

**Why does my goat milk soap smell like ammonia?**
Lye can break down milk proteins, releasing ammonia. It's common in milk soaps and usually fades during cure.

**Will the ammonia smell go away in soap?**
In most cases it fades over the curing period of several weeks.

**How do I stop milk soap from smelling like ammonia?**
Freeze the milk, add lye slowly, keep the batch cool, and consider using less milk.

**Is soap that smells like ammonia safe?**
Properly made, fully cured milk soap with a fading ammonia smell is typically fine. Test and cure fully before use.
