# Why Is My Eclectus Regurgitating? Bonding, Hormones and When It's Vomiting

Your eclectus bobs its head, pumps its neck, and brings up a neat little pile of warm food — for you, a toy, a mirror, or the corner of its cage.

**That's regurgitation, and it's courtship behaviour.** In the wild it's how a parrot feeds a mate or chicks. Directed at you, it means the bird has put you in the mate category.

It is normal. It can also become a problem if it happens constantly.

## Regurgitation vs vomiting — learn the difference

This is the single most useful thing to know, because one is behaviour and one is a medical emergency.

**Regurgitation (behaviour):**
- Deliberate **head bobbing and neck pumping**, often stretching up
- Food comes up **whole or barely changed**, in a tidy pile
- Bird looks **engaged and excited**, then carries on normally
- Aimed at a **target**: you, a toy, a mirror, a cage corner

**Vomiting (get a vet):**
- **Head flicking side to side**, flinging material
- **Mucus-covered, partly digested** food, often sticking to head feathers and cage bars
- Bird looks **unwell**: fluffed, quiet, eyes closing, not eating
- Often with **soiled vent**, weight loss, or crop problems

If it looks like the right-hand column, stop reading and call an avian vet.

## Why eclectus in particular

Male eclectus are well known for **hypersexual behaviour** in captivity: regurgitating, displaying, pacing, masturbating on a favourite object or person. Several things in a normal pet home push hormones up:

- **Long days.** Eclectus need a long, dark night. Keeping a bird up under household lights past a long evening mimics breeding-season daylight.
- **Rich, high-fat food.** Lots of nuts, seed, warm soft food and sugary fruit reads as "abundant season — breed".
- **Nest-like spaces.** Cardboard boxes, tent huts, cupboards, under furniture, dark drawers, bedding.
- **The wrong kind of petting.** Stroking the back, under the wings, around the tail and vent is sexual contact to a parrot. Head and neck only.
- **One favourite human** doing all the handling.

## How to damp it down

1. **12 hours of dark, quiet sleep.** Cover or move to a quiet room at a consistent time.
2. **Pet head and neck only.** Nowhere else, however much the bird leans in.
3. **Remove nest sites:** huts, tents, boxes, fabric caves; block access to dark cupboards and furniture gaps.
4. **Cut back rich food.** Pellets plus vegetables, limited fruit, nuts as occasional treats; no warm mushy food on demand.
5. **Take away the courted object** if it's a specific mirror, bell or soft toy.
6. **Spread handling** across more people in the household.
7. **Add foraging and exercise.** A bored, under-stimulated bird redirects energy into hormonal behaviour.

Changes take weeks, not days. The aim is to lower the overall hormonal signal, not to punish the bird — never scold or spray a bird for regurgitating.

## When regurgitation does need a vet

- It happens **constantly**, with weight loss or appetite change
- **Crop looks swollen**, or food sits in it without emptying
- Material is **sour-smelling, mucousy or discoloured**
- The bird **loses condition** or looks unwell at any point
- You **can't tell** regurgitation from vomiting — that question is worth a consultation on its own

## FAQ

**Why does my eclectus regurgitate on me?**
It sees you as a mate and is offering food. It's affectionate behaviour driven by hormones.

**Should I let my parrot regurgitate for me?**
Don't encourage it. Calmly put the bird down, end the session, and work on the sleep, diet and petting changes above.

**Is regurgitating dangerous for the bird?**
Occasional regurgitation isn't. Chronic hormonal behaviour is linked to real health problems, which is why damping it down matters.

**Do female eclectus do it too?**
Yes, but it's males that are best known for it, often together with other hypersexual behaviour.
