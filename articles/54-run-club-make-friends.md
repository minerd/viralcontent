# How to Actually Make Friends at Run Club (A 4-Week Plan for Awkward People)

Run clubs are having a moment. According to Google, searches for "run club" and "how to start running" hit all-time highs in 2026, and **"how to make friends at run club"** became a breakout search this summer, along with "how to find local run clubs."

That second search tells you something. A lot of people aren't joining run clubs for the running. They're joining because making friends as an adult is hard, and run clubs look like an easy way in.

They can be. But showing up and running in silence for five weeks, then going home, is also very easy. Here's a plan that gets you from "stranger" to "people who save me a spot" in about a month.

## Step 0: Pick the right club

Not every run club is built for making friends. Look for:

- **A "no one left behind" or all-paces policy.** Clubs that regroup or have multiple pace groups are much easier for newcomers.
- **A post-run hangout.** Coffee, breakfast or a drink afterwards. This is where friendships actually happen.
- **Same day, same time, every week.** Consistency is everything.
- **Shorter routes.** A 5K club gives you more talking time per mile than a marathon training group.

Where to find them: local running stores (many host free weekly runs), community Instagram accounts, Strava clubs, Meetup, and simply searching "run club near me."

## Week 1: Just show up

Your only goal in week one is to arrive, run, and not leave immediately.

- Get there **5–10 minutes early.** That's when people chat. Arriving right on time means you only see backs.
- Tell the organizer it's your first time. They'll often introduce you to someone.
- **Run next to someone at your pace** and ask one question: "Have you been coming here long?"
- Stay for the post-run coffee, even if it's just ten minutes.

That's it. Don't try to make a best friend on day one.

## Week 2: Learn three names

People remember whoever remembers them.

- Say hi to anyone you spoke to last week, **by name.**
- Learn two new names. Write them in your phone afterwards if you need to (everyone does this).
- Good low-pressure questions: "Are you training for anything?" "Where do you usually run during the week?" "What's the best post-run food around here?"

## Week 3: Become a regular

This is where it starts to click.

- By now, some people will recognize you. Sit with them at coffee.
- **Follow the club's social account** and the people you've met. Comment on the group photo.
- Offer something small: "I'm doing an easy run on Thursday too if anyone wants to join."

## Week 4: Make one plan outside the club

Friendships need at least one moment outside the regular setting.

- Suggest a **second run**: "Want to do the Saturday long run together?"
- Or suggest a **race**: signing up for a local 5K together is a great shared goal.
- Or keep it simple: "A few of us should grab food after next week's run."

One "yes" is all you need to start.

## "But I'm slow"

This is the number one fear, and it's mostly unfounded.

- Most social run clubs have people of all speeds, and many have a designated slower group.
- **Tell people you're new and slow.** Runners are generally delighted to help newcomers.
- If a club is all fast people and nobody waits, it's the wrong club, not the wrong you. Try another.
- **Walking clubs** hit a 10-year high in 2026, according to Google, and they're even better for conversation.

## "But I'm shy"

Running is actually perfect for shy people:
- **You don't have to make eye contact.** You're both looking at the road.
- **There's built-in small talk.** Pace, route, weather, the hill you just suffered up together.
- **Silence is normal.** Nobody expects you to talk the whole time.

## FAQ

**How do you make friends at a run club?**
Go every week at the same time, arrive early, stay for the post-run social, learn names, and after a few weeks invite someone to a run or event outside the club.

**Are run clubs good for beginners?**
Many are, especially those with multiple pace groups or a "no one left behind" policy.

**How do I find a run club near me?**
Check local running stores, Strava clubs, Meetup, Instagram, and search "run club near me."

**What if I'm too slow for run club?**
Look for clubs with pace groups or walking options. Telling people you're new usually gets you support, not judgment.
