# Why Is My Hognose Snake Twitching? Dramatic Acting vs a Real Problem

Hognose snakes are the drama queens of the snake world. When threatened, they hiss, flatten their necks, and sometimes put on a full death scene: writhing, twitching, rolling onto their backs with their mouths open and tongues hanging out.

So a twitching hognose is often just… being a hognose. But twitching can also be a sign of something serious. Here's how to tell.

*General care information. For persistent or unusual twitching, see an exotic-animal vet who treats reptiles.*

## Normal: the death-feigning display

When scared, hognoses may:
- **Hiss loudly** and flatten their head and neck like a cobra
- **Fake strike** with a closed mouth
- **Writhe and twitch**, then roll onto their back
- **Play dead**, mouth open, tongue out, sometimes releasing a smelly musk

If you flip them right side up, they often roll back over, because in their minds dead snakes lie belly-up.

**Clues it's a display:**
- It happened after a **clear trigger**: being picked up, a sudden movement, a new environment
- The snake **recovers** after a few minutes left alone
- Normal behavior otherwise: eating, exploring, burrowing

**What to do:** step back and give it space. Frequent triggering is stressful, so handle calmly and less often if your snake is jumpy.

## Worrying: neurological signs

Be concerned if twitching:
- Happens **without a trigger**, or while the snake is resting
- Looks like **tremors, head wobbling, or "stargazing"** (head held up, staring)
- Comes with **trouble righting itself**, loss of coordination, or corkscrewing movements
- Continues or keeps coming back

**Possible causes include:**

**Toxin exposure**
- **Mite treatments** or pesticides used incorrectly
- **Cedar or strong aromatic bedding** (commonly advised against for reptiles)
- **Aerosols, cleaning chemicals** or fumes near the enclosure

**Temperature problems**
Very high or very low temperatures can cause neurological signs. Check your warm and cool spots with a digital probe thermometer.

**Infections or injury**
Various infections or head injuries can cause neurological symptoms.

**Nutrition problems**
Diet-related deficiencies can occur in snakes fed a limited diet.

**What to do:**
1. **Remove any possible toxins**: change bedding if aromatic, stop any recent treatment, ventilate the room.
2. **Check temperatures.**
3. **Take a video** of the twitching to show your vet.
4. **Book a reptile vet visit** promptly.

## Other twitchy behaviors that are usually fine

- **Tail twitching or rattling**: a nervous or defensive behavior in many snakes
- **Twitching before a strike at food**: excitement
- **Small twitches during a shed**

## FAQ

**Why does my hognose snake twitch and roll over?**
Usually because it's performing a death-feigning display after being startled. This is normal hognose behavior.

**Is it normal for hognose snakes to play dead?**
Yes. It's one of their best-known defensive behaviors.

**When is hognose twitching a problem?**
If it happens without a trigger, includes tremors, stargazing or loss of coordination, or keeps recurring.

**Can mite spray cause twitching in snakes?**
Toxins, including misused pesticides and mite treatments, can cause neurological signs. Stop exposure and see a vet.
