# Can You Soak Orchids in Water Overnight? When It Helps and When It Causes Rot

Soaking is a popular way to water orchids, especially the common moth orchid (*Phalaenopsis*). So if a short soak is good, is an overnight soak better?

**For routine watering: no.** A short soak is enough, and leaving an orchid in water overnight can lead to soggy roots and rot. But there are a couple of situations where a longer soak can help.

## How routine soaking works

Most orchids sold in shops grow in **bark** or **sphagnum moss**. In nature, many orchids grow on trees, with roots that get drenched by rain and then dry quickly in the air.

A typical soak:
1. Set the pot (with drainage) in a bowl of room-temperature water.
2. Let it soak about **10–15 minutes**.
3. **Drain completely.** Never let the pot sit in standing water afterward.

That's enough for bark to absorb water and roots to turn green.

## Why overnight is too long (usually)

- **Roots need air.** Orchid roots suffocate and start to rot if they stay wet too long.
- **Sphagnum moss holds a lot of water.** Orchids in moss can stay soggy for days after a long soak.
- **Crown rot risk.** Water sitting where the leaves meet (the crown) can cause rot.

## When a longer soak can help

**1. Severely dehydrated orchids**
If an orchid's leaves are **wrinkled and limp**, roots are **silvery and papery** (not rotten), and the bark is bone dry, a longer soak (for example, **an hour or two**) can help rehydrate it. Some growers go longer for very dry bark that repels water. Keep an eye on it and drain thoroughly afterward.

**2. Brand-new, very dry bark**
Fresh, dry bark can be water-repellent. Soaking the bark **before** potting is common practice.

**Even then,** overnight in water isn't necessary for most orchids and increases rot risk.

## How to tell if your orchid needs water

- **Roots:** silvery = dry, green = hydrated
- **Pot weight:** light = dry, heavy = wet
- **Potting mix:** check an inch down with a finger or a wooden skewer
- **Moss** stays wet much longer than bark

Many moth orchids need watering roughly **once a week** in bark, less often in moss, but conditions vary.

## Signs you've overwatered

- **Mushy, brown or black roots**
- **Yellowing lower leaves**
- A **sour smell** from the pot
- **Crown** turning soft and dark

If you see these, unpot, trim rotten roots, and repot in fresh, airy bark.

## FAQ

**Can you soak an orchid overnight?**
It's not recommended for routine watering. A 10–15 minute soak is usually enough, and long soaks can cause root rot.

**How long should I soak my orchid?**
About 10–15 minutes for routine watering, then drain completely.

**How do I rehydrate a dehydrated orchid?**
A longer soak of an hour or two can help, as long as roots aren't rotten. Drain fully afterward.

**Should orchids in moss be soaked?**
Moss holds water longer, so soak briefly and water less often than bark-potted orchids.
