# Why Is My Pet Pigeon Attacking Me? Territory, Hormones and Imprinting

Pigeons have a gentle reputation, so it's surprising when a pet pigeon starts pecking your hands, slapping you with its wings, or charging at your feet.

It's almost never random. Pigeon aggression usually comes down to **territory, hormones, or how the bird sees you**.

## What pigeon "attacks" look like

- **Pecking** hands or feet
- **Wing slapping** (a sharp smack with one wing)
- **Puffing up**, cooing loudly, and **turning in circles**
- **Charging** or chasing
- **Growling-like** coos when approached

## Reason 1: Protecting territory or a nest

Pigeons can be very protective of their **space**, especially a favorite perch, corner, or nest spot. If your hand goes near that area, you may get pecked or slapped.

**Signs:** attacks happen **in one place**, like near a nest box, a shelf, or inside the cage.

**What to do:** respect the space, avoid reaching into it without need, and consider removing nest-like areas if the bird isn't meant to breed.

## Reason 2: Breeding hormones

During breeding periods, pigeons (especially males) can become more territorial and pushy. Females may guard eggs.

**Signs:** lots of cooing, bowing, puffing up, dragging tail feathers, nest building, laying eggs.

**What to do:** reduce nesting triggers: remove nest material and dark nest-like spots, and keep a regular daylight schedule.

## Reason 3: Imprinting on humans

Pigeons **hand-raised by humans** can imprint on people. They may see you as **another pigeon**: sometimes a mate, sometimes a rival. A male imprinted pigeon may court you one minute and attack you the next if he sees you as competition.

**Signs:** the pigeon strongly prefers humans to other pigeons, courts you (cooing, bowing, trying to feed you), and attacks when you don't respond "correctly."

**What to do:** consistent, calm handling; avoid encouraging courtship behaviors; many keepers find that a pigeon companion helps an imprinted bird.

## Reason 4: Fear

A pigeon that feels cornered or grabbed may lash out.

**What to do:** approach slowly, let the bird step onto your hand rather than grabbing, and build trust with treats.

## Reason 5: Pain or illness

A sudden personality change can mean a bird is unwell or hurting.

**Watch for:** fluffed feathers, less eating, changes in droppings, limping, lethargy. See an avian vet if you notice those.

## How to handle an aggressive pigeon

- **Don't punish or hit back.** It increases fear or rivalry.
- **Stay calm and still**, or calmly move away.
- **Wear a long sleeve** or use a towel if you must handle during a hormonal phase.
- **Reward calm behavior** with treats.
- **Give them their own space** and respect it.

## FAQ

**Why does my pigeon peck me?**
Often because it's protecting its territory or nest, feeling hormonal, seeing you as a rival, or feeling scared.

**Why does my pigeon slap me with its wing?**
Wing slapping is a defensive and territorial behavior, often used by pigeons guarding their space.

**Do pet pigeons imprint on humans?**
Yes, hand-raised pigeons can imprint on people and may treat them as mates or rivals.

**How do I stop my pigeon from attacking me?**
Reduce nesting triggers, respect its territory, avoid encouraging courtship, and build trust calmly.
