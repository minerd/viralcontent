# Are You a "Meat Proxy"? The New AI Slang Everyone's Using at Work

You ask a coworker a question on Slack. Ten seconds later, they reply with four perfectly formatted paragraphs, bullet points, and a closing line that says "I hope this helps!"

They didn't write it. They pasted your question into a chatbot and pasted the answer back to you. They may not have even read it.

There's now a word for that person. They're a **meat proxy**.

AI has been creating new slang all year, and a lot of it is about the awkward ways people use AI around each other. Here's the latest vocabulary and what it actually means.

## Meat proxy

**Meaning:** A person who passes along AI-generated text or code without reading, understanding, or checking it. The human is just the delivery system.

**Where it came from:** German software engineer Niklas Gruhn wrote about it in an August 3, 2026 blog post, describing coworkers who kept copying AI responses straight into Slack. The post took off on Hacker News, where it drew more than 700 comments from people with the same complaint.

His fix was simple: before you share AI output, "read it, understand it, and validate it."

**Use it like:** "I asked about the deadline and got three paragraphs about project management best practices. Total meat proxy move."

## Slop grenade

**Meaning:** Dropping a huge block of AI-generated text into a conversation and leaving everyone else to deal with it.

"Slop" has been internet shorthand for low-effort AI content for a while. The grenade is the delivery: it lands, and now it's your problem.

**Use it like:** "He slop-grenaded the group chat with a 1,200-word 'summary' of a 10-minute meeting."

## Tokenmaxxing

**Meaning:** Using as many AI tokens (basically, as much AI usage) as possible and treating that as proof you're productive.

It started as a workplace meme on X earlier in 2026, around the idea that some companies track how much employees use AI. The pushback came quickly. IBM published a piece arguing tokenmaxxing is dead and pitching "valuemaxxing" instead: measure what the AI produced, not how much you used it.

It's part of the bigger "-maxxing" trend, as in looksmaxxing and fibermaxxing.

## Agent washing

**Meaning:** Calling a regular chatbot or basic automation an "AI agent" to make it sound more advanced.

Now that AI agents (tools that take actions like booking, buying or filling forms) are the hot thing, everything is suddenly an agent. If the "agent" just answers questions in a chat window, it's probably been agent-washed.

It's the AI version of "greenwashing."

## Clanker

**Meaning:** An insult for AI, robots, or technology in general. It comes from Star Wars, where clone troopers use it for battle droids.

It's on Mental Floss's list of the top Gen Alpha slang terms of 2026. You'll hear it used jokingly ("thanks, clanker") and less jokingly, when people are fed up with AI in everything.

## FOBO

**Meaning:** Fear Of Becoming Obsolete. The worry that AI will make your skills, or your whole job, irrelevant.

It's been showing up in business press all year, including Fortune and Forbes. It's not just a feeling, either. A KPMG survey found about 4 in 10 workers fear losing their job to AI.

## The words that made it into the dictionary

On September 15, 2026, Merriam-Webster added about 1,400 new words and definitions. The AI-related ones include:

- **Vibe coding:** telling an AI in everyday language what you want and letting it write the code
- **Compute:** processing power and memory, treated as a resource
- **AGI:** artificial general intelligence, a hypothetical AI that can learn across many areas
- **Uncanny valley:** the creepy feeling when something artificial looks almost, but not quite, human

Dictionary.com's update the same day added **model collapse** (when AI trained on AI-made content gets worse) and **context window** (how much text an AI can handle at once).

## Why so much new slang?

Most of these words aren't really about technology. They're about manners.

We're all still working out the etiquette of AI: when it's okay to use it, when it's lazy, when it's rude. New slang is how people signal "this is annoying" without writing a formal policy. "Meat proxy" does in two words what an HR memo would need a page for.

And honestly, the best way to avoid being called one is Gruhn's advice. Read it before you send it.
