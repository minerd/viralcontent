# Why Is My Ginger Bug Soda Not Carbonating? 7 Causes and Fixes

You made your ginger bug, mixed your soda, bottled it, waited… and it's flat. No fizz, no pop, just sweet ginger water.

Carbonation happens in the bottle during a **second fermentation**, when yeast eats sugar and makes carbon dioxide that has nowhere to go. If there's no fizz, one of the pieces is missing. Here's a checklist.

## 1. The bug isn't active enough

**Check:** does your ginger bug bubble within a few hours of feeding? Does it fizz when you stir it?

**Fix:** if it's sluggish, feed it daily with sugar and fresh ginger for a few more days before making soda. Only use it when it's **visibly bubbly**, ideally a few hours after feeding when it's most active.

## 2. Not enough bug in the mix

**Fix:** a common starting point is roughly **¼ cup of active ginger bug liquid per liter (quart)** of sweetened base. If it's been flat, try a little more.

## 3. Not enough sugar

The yeast needs food to make bubbles. If your soda base isn't sweet enough, or you used a sugar substitute, there's nothing to ferment.

**Fix:** use real sugar (or honey, fruit juice, maple). Artificial sweeteners and stevia don't feed yeast. You can add a teaspoon of sugar per bottle at bottling time if it's been flat.

## 4. The bottles aren't sealing

Carbon dioxide escapes if the seal isn't tight.

**Fix:** use **swing-top (flip-top) bottles** designed for pressure, or bottles made for carbonated drinks. Check the rubber gaskets. Mason jars and decorative bottles often don't hold pressure well.

## 5. Too much or too little headspace

**Fix:** leave about **an inch (2–3 cm) of space** at the top. Too much air space dilutes pressure; too little is risky.

## 6. It's too cold

Yeast slows way down in cool rooms.

**Fix:** keep bottles at **warm room temperature** during the second fermentation, somewhere around the low to mid 70s °F (roughly 21–24°C) works well for many people. In a cold kitchen, it may just need more days.

## 7. Not enough time (or opened too soon)

**Fix:** second fermentation typically takes **1–3 days** at room temperature, longer when cool. Resist opening early, since every opening releases pressure.

## Safety: don't over-carbonate

This is important. Homemade fermented sodas can build **a lot of pressure**, and glass bottles can **burst**.

- Use bottles designed for pressure
- **"Burp"** a bottle carefully once a day to check fizz levels (or use one plastic bottle as a tester: when it's firm, the batch is ready)
- Once it's fizzy, **move the bottles to the fridge**, which slows fermentation dramatically
- Open over a sink, pointed away from faces

## Quick checklist

- [ ] Bug bubbly and fed a few hours ago
- [ ] Enough bug in the mix
- [ ] Real sugar in the base
- [ ] Pressure-rated, sealed bottles
- [ ] About an inch of headspace
- [ ] Warm room
- [ ] 1–3 days (or more if cool)

## FAQ

**Why is my ginger bug soda flat?**
Usually a weak bug, not enough sugar, poor seals, a cold room, or not enough time.

**How long does it take ginger bug soda to carbonate?**
Often 1–3 days at room temperature, longer in cooler rooms.

**Can I use coconut sugar for a ginger bug?**
Many people use less-refined sugars, but plain white sugar tends to give the most reliable, active bug. If you use coconut sugar, watch activity closely.

**Can ginger bug soda explode?**
Yes. Pressure can build enough to burst bottles. Use pressure-rated bottles, check them daily, and refrigerate once fizzy.
