# Why Does My Goat's Face Look Swollen? Bottle Jaw, Abscess and Other Causes

You walk out to the pen and one of your goats looks… puffy. Swollen under the jaw, lumpy on the cheek, or puffy all over the face.

Facial swelling in goats has several possible causes, and a few of them are serious. This guide helps you figure out what you might be looking at so you can talk to your vet with good information.

*This is general information and not a substitute for a veterinarian. Several causes below need prompt professional care.*

## Emergency signs: call a vet now

- **Trouble breathing** or noisy breathing
- Swelling that's **spreading fast**
- **Pale gums or eyelids**
- Goat is **weak, down, or not eating**
- Swelling after a **snake bite** or suspected bite

## 1. Bottle jaw (soft swelling under the jaw)

**What it looks like:** a soft, squishy, fluid-filled swelling under the jaw, sometimes bigger in the morning and smaller later in the day.

**What it usually means:** bottle jaw is often a sign of **anemia and low blood protein**, commonly linked to heavy loads of blood-sucking stomach worms such as the **barber pole worm**.

**Check:** look at the inside of the lower eyelid. Healthy color is pink-red. **Pale pink or white** suggests anemia. (Many goat keepers use the FAMACHA eyelid color system to monitor this.)

**Action:** this needs attention soon. A vet can confirm the cause, often with a fecal test, and recommend treatment. Parasite resistance to dewormers is common, so guidance matters.

## 2. Abscess (firm lump)

**What it looks like:** a firm, often round lump on the jaw, cheek or neck, sometimes near a lymph node. It may later soften and burst.

**Why it matters:** some abscesses in goats are caused by **caseous lymphadenitis (CL)**, a contagious bacterial disease. The pus can spread infection to other goats.

**Action:** **isolate the goat** and **don't lance it yourself** without veterinary advice. A vet can test and advise on safe handling.

## 3. Tooth or mouth problems

**What it looks like:** swelling on one side of the face or jaw, possibly with drooling, dropping food, bad smell, or reluctance to eat.

**Action:** vet exam. Tooth root abscesses and mouth injuries need treatment.

## 4. Bites, stings and injuries

**What it looks like:** sudden swelling, often on the nose or face, sometimes with a visible mark. Goats are curious and poke their noses into things.

**Action:** snake bites are an emergency. For stings or minor injuries, call your vet for advice, especially if swelling is large or near the airway.

## 5. Allergic reactions or plant irritation

**What it looks like:** sudden swelling of the face, lips or muzzle, sometimes hives.

**Action:** contact a vet, especially if breathing is affected.

## What to note before calling

- **Where** the swelling is (under jaw, one cheek, whole face)
- **Soft or firm**, warm or not
- **When** it started, and whether it changes during the day
- **Eyelid color**
- Appetite, cud chewing, energy, temperature if you can take it

## FAQ

**What is bottle jaw in goats?**
A soft swelling under the jaw, often linked to anemia and low protein, commonly caused by heavy parasite loads such as barber pole worm.

**Is a lump on my goat's jaw contagious?**
It can be. Some abscesses are caused by caseous lymphadenitis, which is contagious. Isolate the goat and consult a vet.

**How do I check my goat for anemia?**
Look at the inside of the lower eyelid. Pale pink or white suggests anemia.

**When is goat face swelling an emergency?**
If there's trouble breathing, rapid spreading, weakness, pale gums, or a suspected snake bite.
