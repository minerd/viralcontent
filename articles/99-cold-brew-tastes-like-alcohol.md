# Why Does My Cold Brew Taste Like Alcohol? (It May Be Fermenting)

Cold brew should taste smooth, chocolatey, low in acidity. If yours tastes **boozy, winey, fizzy or "fermented,"** something went a bit wrong along the way.

The most likely explanation: **it started to ferment.**

## How coffee can ferment

Brewed coffee contains dissolved sugars and other compounds that wild yeasts and bacteria can feed on. Give them **time and warmth**, and they'll start fermenting, producing alcohol-like, sour and funky flavors.

That's why the details of how you steep and store cold brew matter.

## Common causes

**1. Steeping too long at room temperature**
Many recipes steep cold brew on the counter. That's usually fine for a normal steep, but leave it much longer (especially in a warm kitchen), and fermentation can start.

**2. Storing it too long**
Cold brew concentrate doesn't last forever, even in the fridge. Older concentrate can develop sour, fermented flavors.

**3. Dirty equipment**
Coffee oils and residue left in jars, filters or cold brew makers can harbor microbes and add stale, fermented or rancid flavors to the next batch.

**4. Stale or old beans**
Old, oxidized coffee can taste flat, sour or strange in cold brew. It's not the same as fermenting, but it can add off flavors.

**5. Beans with a "boozy" flavor profile**
Some coffees, especially certain naturally processed or specially fermented coffees, genuinely taste winey or fruity. If the flavor is pleasant and matches the bag's tasting notes, it may be intentional.

## Is it safe to drink?

If cold brew tastes **clearly fermented, sour, fizzy, or off**, or shows **mold, film or cloudiness**, throw it out. It's not worth the risk or the bad cup.

## How to fix it

- **Steep in the fridge** rather than on the counter, especially in warm weather. It takes a bit longer but is much less likely to ferment.
- **Keep steep times reasonable.** Many recipes use roughly **12–24 hours**. Follow your recipe, and taste to find your sweet spot.
- **Filter thoroughly.** Remove all grounds and fine sediment.
- **Store concentrate in a clean, sealed container in the fridge** and use it within the time your recipe recommends (often about a week or two for concentrate). When in doubt, make smaller batches.
- **Clean everything well** between batches, including filters, mesh and gaskets.
- **Use fresh beans**, ideally used within a few weeks of roasting.

## FAQ

**Why does my cold brew taste fermented?**
Usually because it steeped too long at room temperature, was stored too long, or the equipment wasn't fully clean.

**Can cold brew coffee ferment?**
Yes. Given time and warmth, microbes can ferment compounds in brewed coffee.

**Should I steep cold brew in the fridge or on the counter?**
Both are common, but fridge steeping reduces the risk of fermentation, especially in warm kitchens.

**How long does cold brew concentrate last?**
It varies by recipe and storage, but many sources suggest about one to two weeks in the fridge. Discard it if it tastes off.
