# Why Do My Millipedes Keep Dying? 8 Common Mistakes in Millipede Care

Millipedes are popular, low-maintenance invertebrate pets. But "low-maintenance" doesn't mean "no-maintenance," and when millipedes keep dying, it's usually because one part of their setup isn't right.

Here are the most common reasons, in rough order of how often they cause problems.

## 1. Moisture is wrong

Millipedes need **moist substrate**, but not waterlogged.
- **Too dry:** they dehydrate, struggle to molt, and may die
- **Too wet:** stagnant, soggy substrate can grow harmful bacteria and mold

**Aim for:** substrate that's **damp like a wrung-out sponge**, often with a moisture gradient (one side a bit wetter, one side a bit drier) so they can choose.

## 2. Substrate is too shallow or too poor

Millipedes **burrow**, especially to **molt**. They also eat their substrate.

**Aim for:**
- **Deep substrate**, deep enough for them to fully bury themselves, often several times their body length for larger species
- A mix of **organic soil, decaying hardwood leaf litter and rotting hardwood** (commonly used by keepers)

## 3. Not enough food

Many millipedes mainly eat **decaying leaves and rotting wood**. Vegetables and fruit are treats, not the whole diet.

**Aim for:** a constant supply of **leaf litter** (like oak or beech) and **rotting wood** on and in the substrate.

## 4. No calcium

Millipedes need calcium for their exoskeletons.

**Aim for:** a calcium source such as **cuttlebone**, crushed eggshells or other calcium additions commonly recommended by keepers.

## 5. They were disturbed while molting

This is a big one. Millipedes molt **underground**, inside a small chamber. If you dig them up during a molt, they're soft and vulnerable, and it can be fatal.

**Aim for:** **don't dig them up** when they go underground for a while. Millipedes may disappear for weeks. That's normal.

## 6. Temperature swings or heat

Most commonly kept millipedes do well at typical room temperatures. **Overheating** (heat mats, direct sun, hot rooms) is dangerous.

## 7. Chemicals and contamination

Millipedes are sensitive to:
- **Pesticides and bug sprays**
- **Soil or leaves from treated areas**
- **Chlorinated water** in some cases
- **Scented products** and fumes

**Aim for:** collect leaves from **untreated** areas, and consider baking or freezing collected leaf litter to reduce pests.

## 8. They were old or stressed on arrival

Wild-caught or long-shipped millipedes may already be **stressed, old or weak**. Some deaths shortly after purchase have nothing to do with your care.

## Quick checklist

- [ ] Damp but not soggy substrate, with a gradient
- [ ] Deep enough to fully burrow
- [ ] Plenty of leaf litter and rotting hardwood
- [ ] Calcium source available
- [ ] Not digging them up during molts
- [ ] Room temperature, no heat mats or direct sun
- [ ] No pesticides, sprays or treated materials
- [ ] Good but gentle ventilation

## FAQ

**Why are my millipedes dying?**
Common causes are wrong moisture, shallow or poor substrate, lack of food or calcium, disturbance during molts, heat, and chemical exposure.

**How deep should millipede substrate be?**
Deep enough for them to completely bury themselves to molt, which for larger species can be quite deep.

**What do pet millipedes eat?**
Mainly decaying leaves and rotting hardwood, with occasional vegetables and a calcium source.

**Why is my millipede not moving?**
It may be preparing to molt, especially if it's underground. Avoid disturbing it.
