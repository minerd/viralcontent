# Do Guinea Pigs Eat Less in Winter? What's Normal and What's an Emergency

Short answer: **a healthy guinea pig should not eat noticeably less in winter.** If anything, guinea pigs kept somewhere cool often eat *more*, because they burn extra energy to stay warm.

That's the opposite of what some people expect, and it matters. A guinea pig that's eating less is one of the most important warning signs you can spot.

*This is general care information, not veterinary advice. If your guinea pig has stopped eating, contact an exotic-animal vet.*

## Why guinea pigs don't "slow down" for winter

Some animals, like tortoises and some reptiles, naturally slow down and eat less in cold months. Guinea pigs don't. They don't hibernate, and their digestive system is built to run **constantly**.

Guinea pigs need a near-continuous flow of food, mainly **hay**, to keep their gut moving. When they eat less, their gut slows down, and that can spiral quickly into a dangerous condition often called **GI stasis**.

That's why the usual advice from vets and rescues is blunt: a guinea pig that has stopped eating, or is eating much less, for even a short period needs attention.

## Indoor vs outdoor guinea pigs

**Indoor guinea pigs** in a heated home shouldn't show much change at all. Appetite should stay steady year-round.

**Outdoor or cold-room guinea pigs** may eat **more hay** in winter. Chewing and digesting hay actually helps them generate body heat. This is why cold-weather care guides stress unlimited hay and extra bedding.

## Signs that are NOT normal

Watch for:
- **Leaving hay untouched**, or much less eaten than usual
- **Ignoring favorite vegetables** or treats
- **Fewer, smaller, or misshapen droppings** (droppings are one of your best health clues)
- **Hunching**, puffed-up fur, sitting still in a corner
- **Weight loss**
- **Teeth grinding** in a tense way, or drooling
- **Sneezing, crusty eyes, or noisy breathing**, since respiratory infections are more common when guinea pigs get cold

Any of these with reduced eating is a reason to call a vet, not wait and see.

## The best tool you have: a kitchen scale

Weigh your guinea pigs **once a week**, at the same time of day, and write it down. Healthy adults stay fairly stable. A steady drop over a couple of weeks is often the first sign something's wrong, before you'd notice anything else.

## Keeping guinea pigs eating well in winter

- **Unlimited hay, always.** Top it up more often in winter. Hay should make up most of their diet.
- **Keep them warm enough.** Guinea pigs are sensitive to cold and to drafts. Many care guides suggest keeping them roughly between 65 and 75°F (18–24°C). If your guinea pigs live outdoors and it's getting colder than that, consider bringing them inside or into a protected, insulated space.
- **Extra bedding** and a hidey house where they can snuggle.
- **Fresh water that isn't frozen.** Check bottles in cold weather; a frozen spout means no water, and guinea pigs that don't drink often eat less too.
- **Vitamin C.** Guinea pigs can't make their own, so they need it from fresh vegetables and guinea-pig pellets year-round.
- **Keep them moving.** Floor time or a bigger run helps appetite and digestion.

## FAQ

**Do guinea pigs eat less in winter?**
Healthy guinea pigs shouldn't eat much less. Guinea pigs kept in cooler places may eat more hay to stay warm. Reduced eating is a warning sign.

**How long can a guinea pig go without eating?**
Not long. Because their digestion needs constant food, a guinea pig that stops eating should be seen by a vet promptly.

**Do guinea pigs need more food in winter?**
Outdoor or cooler-kept guinea pigs may need more hay. Always keep unlimited hay available.

**What temperature is too cold for guinea pigs?**
Many care guides recommend roughly 65–75°F (18–24°C). Below that, provide extra warmth and protection, or bring them indoors.
