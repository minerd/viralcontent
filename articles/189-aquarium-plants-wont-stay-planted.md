# Aquarium Plants Won't Stay in the Substrate? Why They Float Up and How to Anchor Them

You plant a stem, turn around, and it's drifting at the surface. This is one of the most common first-month frustrations in a planted tank, and it's almost always mechanical rather than a plant-health problem.

## Why they won't stay down

**1. No root system yet**
Freshly bought stems and tissue-culture plants often have almost no roots. Until they grow some, nothing is holding them but friction. Give it **two to four weeks**: plants that stay put start to anchor themselves.

**2. Substrate too shallow**
Gravel 2 cm deep cannot hold a stem. Aim for roughly **5–7 cm (2–3 inches)** where you plan to plant, sloping shallower at the front if you like the look.

**3. Substrate too coarse**
Large, round, polished gravel is the worst offender: the grains roll apart and nothing grips. **Fine gravel (1–3 mm), sand, or a proper aquasoil** holds stems far better.

**4. Buoyancy**
Stems are full of gas-filled tissue; some plants genuinely float. Trimmed tops, in particular, will rise until they root.

**5. Flow**
A filter outlet or powerhead aimed along the substrate will undermine and lift plants. Point it across the surface or diffuse it with a spray bar.

**6. Livestock**
Loaches, corydoras, geophagus, goldfish, cichlids, crayfish and large snails dig, uproot, and sometimes eat plants. If you keep diggers, plant accordingly rather than fighting it.

## Planting technique that works

- **Strip the lower leaves** off the bottom 2–3 cm of each stem. Buried leaves rot and loosen the stem.
- **Plant deep** — push the bare stem well in, not just the tip.
- **Plant stems individually**, a couple of centimetres apart, rather than as a bundle. Bundles trap gas, rot in the middle and float as a clump.
- **Angle the stem slightly** rather than straight down; it grips better.
- Use **planting tweezers** (long, curved). This is the single biggest practical upgrade; fingers disturb far more substrate.
- Lower the **water level** while planting, if that's easy — less buoyancy, better visibility.

## Anchoring tricks

| Method | Best for |
|---|---|
| **Plant weights / lead-free strips** wrapped at the base | Stems and bunches, temporary |
| Tying to a **small inert stone** pushed into the substrate | Anything that keeps lifting |
| **Rock or wood** placed against the base | Crowns and rosettes |
| **Cyanoacrylate gel glue** onto wood/rock | Anubias, bucephalandra, moss, java fern — these should **not** be buried |
| **Mesh or stainless pins/staples** | Carpeting plants, mosses |
| Leaving in the **pot/basket** and burying the pot | Large rosette plants, temporary layouts |

## Match the method to the plant

- **Stem plants** (rotala, hygrophila, bacopa): plant deep, individually, in fine substrate.
- **Rosette plants** (cryptocoryne, vallisneria, amazon sword): bury the roots, keep the crown just above the substrate.
- **Rhizome plants** (anubias, java fern, bucephalandra): never bury the rhizome — tie or glue to hardscape.
- **Bulbs** (aponogeton, crinum): sit the bulb partly exposed; burying it fully rots it.
- **Carpeting plants**: split into small portions, press into fine substrate, keep flow gentle until rooted.
- **Floating-by-nature plants** (hornwort, elodea trimmings): let them float, or expect regular replanting.

## Still won't hold?

- Add **depth**, not more weight
- Switch the planting area to **finer substrate** or a layer of sand on top
- **Re-aim the filter** and reduce flow at substrate level for a few weeks
- Re-check for **digging livestock**, especially at night
- Accept a **hardscape-attached layout** (ferns, anubias, moss on wood) if you keep diggers

## FAQ

**How deep should substrate be for aquarium plants?**
About 5–7 cm (2–3 inches) in the planted areas; much less and roots have nothing to hold.

**Can you plant aquarium plants in gravel?**
Fine gravel works. Large, smooth gravel is the usual reason plants won't stay down.

**How long until plants root properly?**
Typically two to four weeks for healthy stems, longer in low light and no fertiliser.

**Should I glue or bury anubias and java fern?**
Glue or tie them to rock or wood — burying the rhizome causes it to rot.
