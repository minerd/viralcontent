# Why Is My Fire Cider Cloudy? (Normal) and When to Toss It (Not Normal)

Fire cider is a folk tonic made by steeping pungent ingredients like horseradish, ginger, garlic, onion, hot peppers and citrus in apple cider vinegar, often sweetened with honey afterward.

If yours looks **cloudy, murky or has sediment**, that's usually completely normal. Here's why, and the few things that aren't.

## Why fire cider gets cloudy

**1. Raw apple cider vinegar**
Many people use **raw, unfiltered ACV "with the mother."** It's naturally cloudy, and it can grow more haze or strands over time.

**2. Starches and particles from the roots**
Horseradish, ginger, garlic and onion release **starches, fibers and tiny particles** into the vinegar. That makes it hazy and can form a layer of sediment at the bottom.

**3. Honey**
Adding honey, especially raw honey, makes the liquid cloudier.

**4. Citrus**
Citrus pulp and oils add cloudiness.

**5. Grating vs chopping**
Grated ingredients release more particles than chunky ones.

## What's the "mother"?

A **vinegar mother** is a gelatinous, jelly-like disc or strands that can form in raw vinegar. It's made of cellulose and the bacteria that make vinegar. It can look strange but is a normal part of raw vinegar.

You can strain it out or leave it; many people strain fire cider through cheesecloth before bottling.

## When cloudy isn't okay

**Throw it out if you see:**
- **Fuzzy mold** on the surface, especially on ingredients poking above the liquid
- **Colored spots** (green, black, pink) on top or on the solids
- **A rotten or putrid smell** instead of sharp, spicy vinegar

Most spoilage in fire cider happens when **ingredients stick up above the vinegar**, where mold can grow.

## How to avoid problems

- Keep all ingredients **fully submerged** under the vinegar
- If using a **metal lid**, put parchment or wax paper under it, because vinegar can corrode metal
- **Shake daily** during infusion
- **Strain** after the steeping period (many recipes use a few weeks)
- Store in a **cool, dark place**, or refrigerate if your recipe suggests

## Is fire cider supposed to be clear?

No. Clear fire cider is unusual. A hazy, slightly murky, golden-orange liquid with some sediment is typical. Shake before using.

## FAQ

**Is cloudy fire cider normal?**
Yes. Raw vinegar, root starches, citrus and honey all make it cloudy.

**What is the slimy thing in my fire cider?**
Often a vinegar "mother," a harmless jelly-like disc that forms in raw vinegar.

**How do I know if fire cider has gone bad?**
Fuzzy mold, colored spots, or a rotten smell mean it should be discarded.

**Should I strain fire cider?**
Many people strain it through cheesecloth after steeping, which also reduces sediment.
