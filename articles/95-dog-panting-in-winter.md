# Why Is My Dog Panting So Much in Winter? 7 Reasons When It Isn't Hot

Panting is how dogs cool down, so it makes sense in summer. But in winter? When it's cold outside and your dog is lying on the couch, panting away?

There are some very ordinary winter explanations, and a few that deserve a vet visit. Here's how to think it through.

*General information only. If your dog is panting heavily at rest, seems distressed, or has other symptoms, contact your vet.*

## 1. It's warmer indoors than you think

Central heating, radiators, fireplaces and wood stoves can make a room very warm, especially near the floor where a dog lies. A dog curled up next to a heat source can overheat without leaving the spot.

**Try:** move the dog's bed away from heaters, and check if panting stops.

## 2. A coat or sweater that's too warm

Dog coats are great outdoors for small or short-haired dogs. Indoors, they can cause overheating fast.

**Try:** take coats off as soon as you come inside.

## 3. Excitement and activity

Winter walks, playing in snow, visitors over the holidays. Excitement and exercise cause panting in any season.

**Normal if:** it settles down within a reasonable time after activity.

## 4. Stress and anxiety

Holiday decorations, guests, fireworks around New Year, changes in routine, less time outside. All can cause stress panting.

**Clues:** pacing, whining, hiding, lip licking, yawning, tucked tail.

## 5. Pain

Dogs often pant when they're in pain. Cold weather can make **arthritis** and joint stiffness worse, especially in older dogs.

**Clues:** stiffness, reluctance to climb stairs or jump, limping, panting while resting, restlessness at night.

## 6. Weight gain

Less exercise and more treats in winter can add pounds. Overweight dogs pant more with less effort.

## 7. Medical conditions

Some health problems cause panting regardless of temperature, including:
- **Heart or lung disease**
- **Cushing's disease** (common in middle-aged and older dogs, often with increased thirst, urination and appetite)
- **Fever or infection**
- **Side effects of certain medications**, such as steroids

## When to call the vet

- Heavy panting **at rest** in a cool room
- Panting that's **new and unexplained**
- Panting with **coughing, weakness, pale or blue-tinged gums**, collapse, or restlessness
- Panting with **increased thirst, urination or appetite**
- Signs of **pain**
- Any sudden change in an older dog

**Emergency:** labored breathing, blue or gray gums, collapse, or a swollen belly. Go to an emergency vet.

## Quick check at home

1. Move your dog to a cooler room, away from heat.
2. Remove any coat.
3. Let them rest 20–30 minutes.
4. If panting continues at rest, note breathing rate and other symptoms, and call your vet.

## FAQ

**Why is my dog panting when it's cold?**
Often because indoor heating or a coat is making them warm, or from excitement or stress. Pain and medical conditions are other possibilities.

**Is it normal for dogs to pant in winter?**
After activity or near a heater, yes. Heavy panting at rest in a cool room is not normal.

**Why does my old dog pant at night in winter?**
Pain from arthritis, anxiety, or medical conditions like Cushing's disease or heart problems can cause night panting. See your vet.

**Can dogs overheat indoors in winter?**
Yes, especially near radiators, fireplaces or while wearing a coat indoors.
