# Can You Drink Mead After 2 Weeks? Young Mead, Safety and Taste

You started your first batch of mead two weeks ago. It's bubbling, it smells like honey and alcohol, and you're curious. Can you drink it?

**Technically, you can taste it.** But at two weeks, most mead is still **fermenting**, and it's usually **cloudy, yeasty, sweet or harsh**. For most recipes, it isn't ready yet.

## What two-week-old mead is like

At around two weeks, a typical mead:
- Is often **still fermenting** (bubbles in the airlock)
- Is **cloudy** with yeast
- May taste **very sweet** (sugar not finished) or **hot and harsh** (young alcohol)
- Can have **yeasty or sulfur** notes

A small taste won't hurt you, but it's not the finished experience.

## The big risk: bottling too early

The most important safety issue isn't drinking it, it's **bottling it too early**.

If you bottle mead that's still fermenting, the yeast keeps producing carbon dioxide inside sealed bottles. Pressure can build until bottles **burst** ("bottle bombs"), which can cause serious injury.

**How to know fermentation is finished:**
- Use a **hydrometer** to take **specific gravity readings**
- Take readings a few days apart
- If the reading is **stable** over several days, fermentation has likely finished

Bubbles in the airlock alone aren't a reliable indicator.

## How long does mead take?

It varies a lot:
- **Short meads and hydromels** (lower alcohol) can be ready in weeks
- **Standard meads** often take months to finish and clear
- **Strong or complex meads** can benefit from much longer aging

Aging often makes a big difference: harsh flavors mellow, and honey and fruit character comes through.

## If you want mead sooner

- Try a **short mead or hydromel** recipe, designed to be drinkable earlier
- Use **proper yeast nutrients** and good temperature control, which help produce cleaner, faster fermentations
- Be patient with clearing and aging

## FAQ

**Can I drink mead after one or two weeks?**
You can taste it, but it's usually still fermenting and not at its best.

**Is young mead safe to drink?**
Tasting young mead is generally fine, but don't bottle it until fermentation is complete.

**How do I know when my mead is done fermenting?**
Use a hydrometer and look for stable gravity readings over several days.

**What is a bottle bomb?**
A bottle that bursts because fermentation continued inside it and built up pressure.
