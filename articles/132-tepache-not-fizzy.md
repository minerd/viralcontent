# Why Is My Tepache Not Fizzy? How to Get Bubbly Tepache (Safely)

Tepache can be lightly fizzy, like a natural pineapple soda. But plenty of first batches come out flat: tasty, but still.

Usually, nothing is wrong. You just need to know **where fizz comes from**.

## Where the bubbles come from

Wild yeasts on pineapple peels eat sugar and produce **carbon dioxide**. In an **open or loosely covered jar**, most of that gas escapes. So a first ferment in a jar is often only slightly bubbly.

Real fizz comes from a **second fermentation** in a **sealed bottle**, where the gas has nowhere to go.

## Common reasons tepache stays flat

**1. You only did the first ferment**
Open-jar fermentation rarely builds much fizz.

**2. It's too cold**
Yeast slows down in a cool kitchen. Fermentation still happens, just much more slowly.

**3. The peels were scrubbed too well**
The natural yeast lives on the peel. Rinsing off dirt is good; scrubbing hard or using hot water can remove too much.

**4. Chlorinated water**
Heavily chlorinated tap water may slow down wild yeast. Filtered or dechlorinated water can help.

**5. Not enough sugar left**
If the first ferment went long, the yeast may have eaten most of the sugar.

**6. It fermented too long**
Over-fermented tepache can get sour and even vinegary, with less sweetness to power a second ferment.

## How to make fizzy tepache

1. **First ferment:** make tepache as usual in a jar, loosely covered, at room temperature. Often 2–3 days, tasting daily.
2. **Strain** out the peels and spices.
3. **Bottle** in **swing-top bottles** or bottles designed for carbonated drinks. Leave about an inch of headspace.
4. Optionally add a **small amount of sugar** or a splash of pineapple juice per bottle.
5. **Second ferment** at room temperature for about **12–48 hours**.
6. **Check pressure daily**: carefully "burp" a bottle, or use one plastic bottle as a tester. When it's firm, it's ready.
7. **Refrigerate** to slow fermentation and keep the fizz.

## Safety: pressure can build fast

Pineapple is sugary and wild ferments can be vigorous. **Glass bottles can burst.**
- Use bottles rated for pressure
- Check them daily
- **Refrigerate once carbonated**
- Open over a sink, pointed away from your face

## FAQ

**Why isn't my tepache fizzy?**
Usually because it hasn't had a sealed second fermentation. Cold temperatures, scrubbed peels and low sugar also reduce fizz.

**How do I make tepache carbonated?**
Strain it, bottle it in pressure-safe bottles, let it ferment 12–48 hours at room temperature, then refrigerate.

**Can tepache explode?**
Yes, pressure can build enough to burst bottles. Check bottles daily and refrigerate once fizzy.

**Can I use white sugar in tepache?**
Yes. Piloncillo is traditional and adds caramel flavor, but white sugar works for fermentation.
