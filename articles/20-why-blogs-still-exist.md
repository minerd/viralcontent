# Google Answers Everything Now. So Why Do Blogs Still Exist?

Here's an awkward thing to write on a blog: most people who search for things don't need blogs anymore.

Ask Google a question now and there's a good chance an AI summary answers it before you see a single link. Studies from SEO firms like Ahrefs and Seer Interactive put the drop in clicks at roughly 58–61% on searches where these AI Overviews appear. And by September 2026, almost all of Google's "People also ask" answers were AI-generated.

For a lot of websites, that's been brutal. If your site explained "what is a Roth IRA" or "how many cups in a quart," Google is now giving that answer away without sending you anyone.

And honestly? For those questions, that's probably fine. Nobody needed a 2,000-word blog post with a recipe-style life story to find out how many cups are in a quart.

But it raises a real question. If AI can answer everything, what's left for people who write?

## What AI summaries are actually good at

Let's be fair to them. AI summaries are great at:
- Facts that are the same no matter who you ask
- Definitions
- Simple steps that everyone agrees on
- Combining information that's already all over the web

That's a lot of what the internet used to be. A big chunk of blogging for the last fifteen years was, bluntly, rewriting what other websites said in slightly different words to rank on Google. AI does that better, faster, and for free. That part of blogging deserved to go.

## What they can't do

Here's what's interesting. The same research that shows the traffic drop also shows what's holding up. The content still getting clicks tends to be:

**Things someone actually tried.** "I used these three tools for a month" can't be summarized from other sources, because the experience only exists in one place.

**Opinions with a spine.** An AI summary is designed to be balanced. "It depends on your needs" is its native language. A person who says "don't buy this, buy that, here's why" is offering something the summary can't.

**Decisions.** Comparisons, "should I," "which one." People facing a real decision want to see the reasoning, not a paragraph.

**Original information.** A small survey, a price you tracked, a test you ran, a photo you took. New information doesn't exist until someone creates it. AI can only summarize after a human goes first.

**A voice you trust.** This is underrated. People follow writers, not just topics. If you've read someone for a year and they've been right, their recommendation is worth more than an anonymous summary.

## The irony

There's a twist in all this. AI summaries are built from what people write. If humans stop writing original things (testing, reviewing, arguing, reporting) the summaries have nothing new to summarize. They start summarizing summaries.

So the stuff AI can't replace is also the stuff AI needs. The web still needs people to go first.

## What this means for writers

If you write online, the lesson is uncomfortable but clear: **stop writing things a summary can replace.**

Before you write anything, ask: *could Google's AI summary answer this in three sentences?* If yes, don't bother, or find the angle it can't cover. Your experience. Your opinion. Your test. Your data.

It's harder than writing another "10 Tips for X" post. It's also the only kind of writing that's worth anything now, which is maybe how it should have been all along.

## What this means for readers

If you read online, the lesson is simpler: **seek out people, not just answers.**

For facts, the summary is fine. For decisions, go find someone who's actually done the thing and will tell you what they really think. Follow them. Subscribe to their newsletter. That's the part of the internet worth keeping, and it only survives if people read it.

## So, why do blogs still exist?

Because an answer isn't the same as advice, a summary isn't the same as experience, and "it depends" isn't the same as someone telling you what they'd do.

The internet is losing a lot of pages it won't miss. The ones that are left are the ones that were worth writing in the first place.

## FAQ

**Are AI Overviews killing blogs?**
They've heavily cut traffic to blogs that answered simple, factual questions. Blogs built on first-hand experience, strong opinions, comparisons, and original information are holding up much better.

**How much traffic do AI Overviews take?**
Studies from firms including Ahrefs and Seer Interactive have measured click-through drops of roughly 58–61% on searches where AI Overviews appear.

**Is blogging still worth it in 2026?**
Yes, if you write things a summary can't replace. Generic, rewritten content is much less worth doing than it used to be.

**How do I get my blog cited in AI Overviews?**
Clear structure, direct answers near the top of sections, original information, and strong credibility all help, though there's no guaranteed method.
