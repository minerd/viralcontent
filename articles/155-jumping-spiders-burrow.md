# Is It Normal for Jumping Spiders to Burrow? What Digging and Hiding Can Mean

Most pet jumping spiders spend their time climbing, hunting, and resting in little silk "hammocks" near the top of their enclosure. So when yours disappears into the substrate or seems to be digging, it's natural to worry.

**It depends a lot on the species and the situation.** Here's what to consider.

## Most jumping spiders don't really burrow

Popular pet species like the regal jumping spider (*Phidippus regius*) and bold jumping spider (*Phidippus audax*) usually build **silk retreats up high**, often in a corner near the lid. That's why many keepers use enclosures that open from the bottom or front, so they don't destroy the hammock when opening the lid.

## Some species naturally stay low

Some jumping spiders live mainly on the ground, among leaf litter and stones, and may tuck themselves under debris or into the substrate. If you have a less common or wild-caught species, look up its natural habitat.

## Reasons a jumping spider might go into the substrate

**1. Preparing to molt**
Spiders molt in a sheltered spot. Some will hide low or build a retreat in an unusual place. Pre-molt spiders often stop eating and become less active.

**2. Laying an egg sac (females)**
Mature females may produce egg sacs, even without mating (those eggs won't be fertile). They often guard them in a silk retreat.

**3. Too hot or too bright**
A spider may hide low to escape heat or bright light from a lamp directly above.

**4. Stress**
A new enclosure, frequent disturbance, or vibrations can make a spider hide more.

**5. The enclosure has no good hammock spots**
If there's nowhere suitable up high, a spider may build a retreat lower down.

## When to worry

- **Not eating for a long time** without signs of molting, plus weight loss (shrinking abdomen)
- **Struggling to climb**, falling repeatedly
- **Curling legs** underneath, twitching
- **Exposure to sprays or fumes** (bug spray, air freshener, incense), which are dangerous to spiders

## How to help

- Provide **anchor points up high**: twigs, fake leaves, corners
- Use an enclosure that opens from the **front or bottom**
- Avoid **direct heat** and very bright lamps
- Mist lightly on the walls for drinking droplets, with good ventilation
- **Don't dig your spider out** if it might be molting

## FAQ

**Do jumping spiders burrow?**
Most common pet species build silk hammocks up high. Some ground-dwelling species stay low among debris.

**Why is my jumping spider hiding in the substrate?**
It may be preparing to molt, laying an egg sac, avoiding heat or light, or feeling stressed.

**Should I dig up my jumping spider?**
No, especially if it might be molting. Disturbing a molt can be fatal.

**Why isn't my jumping spider building a web up high?**
It may not have good anchor points, or the enclosure setup doesn't suit it.
