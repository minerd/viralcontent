# The AI Subscription Audit: You're Probably Paying for the Same Thing 3 Times

Here's how it usually goes. You sign up for ChatGPT Plus because everyone has it. Then Claude gets good at something you need, so you add that. Your phone offers you a Gemini trial. Work gives you Copilot. You try Perplexity for research, an image tool for a project, a voice tool for a video, and a note-taker for meetings.

A year later, you're paying for six AI subscriptions and using two of them.

AI tools have become the new streaming services: each one is "only" a monthly fee, and together they're a real bill. And unlike Netflix vs Disney+, most general AI chatbots now overlap heavily. They all write, summarize, analyze files, search the web, and make images to some degree.

So let's audit.

## Step 1: List everything (5 minutes)

Open your bank or card statement and search for these names from the last three months:

- OpenAI / ChatGPT
- Anthropic / Claude
- Google One / Google AI / Gemini
- Microsoft 365 / Copilot
- Perplexity
- Midjourney
- ElevenLabs
- Any note-taker, "AI writer", or AI plugin you tried once

Also check your **App Store and Google Play subscriptions**. That's where forgotten trials hide.

Write each one down with its monthly cost. Annual plans: divide by 12.

## Step 2: Write down what you *actually* used it for (5 minutes)

Not what you signed up for. What you did with it in the last month. Be honest.

A lot of people find something like this:

| Tool | What I actually used it for |
|---|---|
| Chatbot #1 | Emails, quick questions |
| Chatbot #2 | Long documents, sometimes |
| Chatbot #3 (trial) | Nothing, forgot about it |
| Image tool | Two images for one presentation |
| Note-taker | Used it for three meetings, then stopped |

If a column says "nothing" or "once," you already know what to do.

## Step 3: Find the overlap (5 minutes)

Here's the uncomfortable part. If you pay for two or more general chatbots, ask: **what can one do that the other genuinely can't, for the things I actually use?**

Not "which is better on benchmarks." What you use.

For most people, the honest answer is one of these:
- **"Nothing important."** Keep your favorite, cancel the rest.
- **"One is better for writing/coding, the other for X."** Fair. But check if the free tier of one covers X.
- **"I like switching between them."** Also fair. Just know what it costs you per year.

The same goes for images. All the big chatbots now generate images to some degree. If you're paying for a dedicated image tool and making two images a month, the one inside your chatbot is probably good enough.

## Step 4: Check what you already get for free (5 minutes)

This is where people save the most. A lot of AI features come bundled with things you already pay for:

- **Your phone.** Recent flagship phones often come with a trial or built-in AI features.
- **Your work.** If your company provides an AI tool, use it for work stuff (following company rules), and don't pay personally for the same thing.
- **Your storage plan.** Some cloud storage plans now include AI features.
- **Free tiers.** The free versions of most major chatbots are genuinely capable for everyday tasks. The paid tier mostly buys higher limits and the newest models.
- **Student discounts.** Several AI companies offer them. Always check before paying full price.

## Step 5: Decide, then set a reminder

Cancel what you don't use. For anything you're unsure about, cancel anyway and see if you miss it in two weeks. You can always come back, and many services will even offer you a discount when you do.

Then put a reminder in your calendar for three months from now: "AI subscription audit." These tools change fast. What's worth paying for today might be free, or worse, in a few months.

## A simple rule of thumb

If you're not a developer, designer, or someone whose job is heavy writing or research: **one paid chatbot is almost always enough.** Use the free tiers of the others for the occasional comparison.

If you *are* a heavy user, paying for two can make sense, especially if one is much better at your specific work. But three or more general chatbots is usually habit, not need.

## FAQ

**Which AI subscription is worth paying for?**
The one you open every day. Seriously. Pick based on what you actually use, not reviews. Try the free tiers for a week first.

**Is ChatGPT Plus worth it if I use the free version a lot?**
If you regularly hit the free limits or need the newest models, probably yes. If you use it a few times a week, the free version is likely fine.

**Are AI subscriptions tax deductible?**
Sometimes, if you use them for work and you're self-employed. Check with an accountant for your country.

**How do I cancel an AI subscription bought through my phone?**
Through your phone's app store subscription settings, not the app itself. Deleting the app does not cancel the subscription.
