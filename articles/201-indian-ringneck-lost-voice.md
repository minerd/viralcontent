# Why Is My Indian Ringneck Losing Its Voice? Silence, Squeaks and Respiratory Illness

An Indian ringneck that was talking and screaming last week now sounds **hoarse, squeaky, or opens its beak with almost nothing coming out**.

**Take this seriously.** In parrots, a changed or lost voice is one of the recognised early signs of a respiratory tract infection — and birds hide illness until they can't.

## Check for illness first

Birds vocalise using the **syrinx**, deep in the chest where the windpipe splits. Anything that inflames or obstructs the airway changes the voice. Look for:

- **Breathing noises**: clicking, wheezing, gurgling
- **Tail bobbing** at rest (the whole tail pumping with each breath)
- **Discharge** from nostrils or eyes; crusty or blocked nares
- **Open-mouth breathing**, or breathing with the beak slightly open after mild activity
- **Fluffed posture**, sleeping during the day, sitting low on the perch
- **Appetite or weight loss** — weigh the bird; a gram scale is the best early-warning tool you can own
- **Changed droppings**

Any of these, with the voice change, means an **avian vet now**. Respiratory infections in parrots can be fatal and respond much better early. Also on the differential list: **vitamin A deficiency** (common in seed-heavy diets, and it damages the lining of the airway), aspergillosis (fungal, often from mouldy food or bedding), and air-sac mites.

## Environmental causes that are also real

**Airborne irritants** damage a parrot's airway quickly. Remove, permanently:
- Non-stick cookware fumes (**PTFE/Teflon is lethal to birds**)
- Cigarette, vape, incense and candle smoke
- Air fresheners, scented sprays, plug-ins, essential oils
- Aerosols, strong cleaners, fresh paint
- Dusty bedding or a dusty room

**Dry air** (winter heating) also dries the airway. Baths, misting, and a humidifier can help — but dry air alone shouldn't make a bird genuinely lose its voice.

## The harmless explanations

Once illness is ruled out:

- **Molting.** Many birds are quieter and scruffier while molting; energy goes into feathers.
- **Hormonal season.** Ringnecks change behaviour markedly in breeding season, in both directions — some go silent, some get very loud.
- **A quiet house.** Parrots match their environment. If the home has gone quiet (someone moved out, a routine changed, the radio stopped), the bird may simply stop calling.
- **Young birds pausing.** Juvenile ringnecks experiment with sounds, then go through quiet phases before talking properly. A young bird that has gone quiet for days or weeks is often just between stages.
- **Stress or a change**: a new cage spot, new pet, rearranged room, loss of a companion. Ringnecks are sensitive to change.
- **Over-use.** A bird that screamed for hours can sound rough the next day, like a person after a concert.

## What to do in order

1. **Look and listen**: breathing noise, tail bob, nares, droppings, posture
2. **Weigh** the bird, and keep weighing it
3. **Remove airborne irritants** from the whole home, not just the bird's room
4. **Check the diet**: a pellet base plus vegetables; vitamin A sources (dark leafy greens, carrot, sweet potato, squash). A seed-only diet is a cause, not a detail
5. **Check for mould** in feed, seed, bedding and anything damp near the cage
6. **Vet** if there is any physical sign, or if the voice doesn't come back within a week or two with everything else normal

## FAQ

**Can a parrot lose its voice like a person with a cold?**
Not quite — birds don't get human colds, and a voice change usually means airway inflammation from infection, irritants or deficiency. It isn't something to wait out.

**My ringneck squeaks instead of talking. Is that illness?**
It can be. If breathing, posture, appetite and droppings are all normal and the bird is young or molting, it's more likely a phase.

**How long should I wait before seeing a vet?**
Immediately if there's any breathing noise, discharge, tail bobbing or appetite change. Otherwise, a week or two of a persistently changed voice is enough reason.

**Does a quiet ringneck mean it's unhappy?**
Not necessarily — but a bird that was vocal and has gone silent is worth investigating medically before assuming it's mood.
