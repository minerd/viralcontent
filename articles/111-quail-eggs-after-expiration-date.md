# Can You Eat Quail Eggs After the Expiration Date? How to Tell If They're Still Good

Quail eggs are small, pretty, and usually bought for a specific recipe. Which means there's often a carton sitting in the fridge past the date on the label.

**Often, refrigerated quail eggs are still fine for a while after the date.** But "often" isn't "always," so check them before you use them.

*General food safety information. When in doubt, throw it out.*

## What the date on the carton means

Dates on egg cartons are commonly **"best by," "sell by," or "use by"** dates, and they vary by country and producer. Many are about **quality**, not a hard safety cutoff.

Eggs that have been **kept refrigerated consistently** often remain usable for a period after the date, though quality (freshness, how well whites hold their shape) slowly declines.

## Where they were stored matters

- **Kept in the fridge the whole time:** best chance of still being good
- **Left out for long periods**, or moved in and out of the fridge: higher risk, use caution
- **Unwashed farm eggs** vs **washed store eggs**: storage guidance differs by country, but once eggs are refrigerated, keep them refrigerated

## How to tell if quail eggs are still good

**1. The float test**
Place the egg in a bowl of water:
- **Sinks and lies flat:** fresh
- **Sinks but stands up:** older, usually fine to use, best cooked thoroughly
- **Floats:** old, with a large air cell. Many people discard floaters

The float test shows **age**, not safety for certain, so always follow it with a smell check.

**2. The smell test (most important)**
Crack each egg into a separate small bowl before using. A **bad egg smells unmistakably bad**, sulfurous and rotten. If it smells off, toss it.

**3. Look**
Discard eggs with:
- **Cracked shells** (bacteria can get in)
- **Slimy or powdery** shells
- **Unusual colors** inside (pink, green, black)

## Cook them thoroughly

Especially with older eggs, **cook until whites and yolks are firm**. Quail eggs cook quickly:
- **Soft-boiled:** about 2–2.5 minutes
- **Hard-boiled:** about 3.5–4 minutes

Avoid raw or runny older eggs, and be extra careful if serving children, older adults, pregnant people or anyone with a weakened immune system.

## Storing quail eggs

- Keep them **in the fridge**, in their carton, not in the door (the door gets the most temperature swings)
- **Pointy end down** helps keep the yolk centered
- **Hard-boiled quail eggs:** refrigerate and eat within about a week

## FAQ

**Can you eat quail eggs after the expiration date?**
Often, if they've been kept refrigerated. Check them with a float test and a smell test first.

**How long do quail eggs last in the fridge?**
They often keep for several weeks when refrigerated consistently, but check each egg before use.

**How do you know if a quail egg is bad?**
A rotten smell when cracked, discolored insides, or cracked or slimy shells. Floating eggs are old and often discarded.

**Can you eat quail eggs raw?**
Raw eggs carry a risk of foodborne illness, especially older eggs. Cooking thoroughly is safest.
