# Why Are My Goslings Limping? Niacin, Injury and Infection

A gosling that was fine last week is now favouring one leg, sitting down a lot, or walking with bowed legs. Geese grow faster than almost any farm bird, and their legs carry that growth — which is why leg problems show up early and get worse quickly.

**Start with diet.** In young waterfowl, limping that isn't an obvious injury is most often nutritional.

## 1. Niacin (vitamin B3) shortage — the usual cause

Goslings and ducklings need **much more niacin** than chicks. Feeding plain chick starter, which is formulated for chickens, is the single most common reason for weak, bowed or wobbly legs in waterfowl.

What it looks like:
- Reluctance to walk, sitting back on the hocks
- Legs **bowing outward** over days or weeks
- Poor weight gain
- Usually **several birds** affected, not just one

What to do:
- Use a **waterfowl starter/grower** if you can get one
- Otherwise, supplement: **brewer's yeast** mixed into the feed is the standard fix keepers use (roughly a tablespoon or two per cup of feed), or niacin added to water per your vet's advice
- Don't use niacin products meant as human flush-free supplements without advice — plain niacin/nicotinic acid is what's wanted
- Caught early, bowed legs often improve markedly. Caught late, the bone deformity can be permanent

Related: **thiamine and other B vitamins** can be short in birds fed a lot of bread, grain or kitchen scraps instead of a balanced feed.

## 2. Growth outrunning the legs

Geese put on weight extremely fast. Too much high-energy feed, too little grazing space, and slippery flooring combine into strain injuries and splay-leg. Goslings need **room to walk and graze every day** — grass is their natural feed and the exercise is part of raising sound legs.

Slick surfaces (plastic tubs, newspaper, smooth boards) cause slips that become long-term limps. Use a **non-slip floor** with clean straw, towels or rubber matting.

## 3. Injury

- **Trampling** by the flock or by adult birds
- A caught leg, a fall, or a slip on wet flooring
- Being picked up by the legs (never do this)

Signs: it happened suddenly, **one bird only**, often no swelling or heat. Confine the bird somewhere clean and quiet with easy access to feed and water, and most simple strains improve in days.

## 4. Infected foot or joint

A swollen leg or foot that feels **hot** — sometimes starting from a tiny cut or thorn — points to a bacterial infection, including staph infections that attack the tendon sheaths. These affect young geese more than adults, take a long time to heal, and need a **vet**: antibiotics are the treatment, not time.

**Bumblefoot** (a dark scab on the pad with swelling) is the other classic foot problem; it also needs treatment rather than waiting.

## 5. Parasites

Young geese on pasture are particularly vulnerable to **gizzard worm**, which drains condition and shows up as weakness and lameness. Worms generally cause poor growth plus lameness in a group. Worming programmes for waterfowl differ from chickens — ask a vet rather than guessing a dose.

## 6. Botulism and other emergencies

A gosling that is **suddenly weak in legs, neck and wings**, often around stagnant water in hot weather, may have botulism. This is an emergency: fresh water, shade, and immediate vet advice.

## Quick triage

| Clue | Likely cause |
|---|---|
| Several birds, bowing legs, slow growth | Niacin/B shortage — fix the feed now |
| One bird, sudden, no swelling | Injury or strain — confine and rest |
| Hot, swollen leg or foot | Infection — vet |
| Dark scab under the foot | Bumblefoot — treatment needed |
| Whole group poor, pasture-raised | Worms — vet advice |
| Weak legs, neck and wings together | Possible botulism — emergency |

## FAQ

**Can goslings have chick starter?**
Not as their only feed. Chicken feed is low in niacin for waterfowl; use a waterfowl feed or supplement with brewer's yeast.

**Will bowed legs in a gosling correct themselves?**
Often partly, if the diet is corrected early. Advanced deformity usually doesn't fully straighten.

**Should I splint a gosling's leg?**
Only on veterinary advice. Splints are used for specific conditions like slipped tendon or splay leg, and the wrong splint makes things worse.

**How much space do goslings need?**
Daily access to grazing and a dry, non-slip floor. Confinement on slick bedding is itself a cause of leg problems.
