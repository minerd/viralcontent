# Siri Can't Set Timers on Apple Watch After watchOS 27? Try These Fixes

Setting a timer is probably the single most common thing people ask Siri on Apple Watch. So it's especially annoying when, after updating to watchOS 27, you raise your wrist, say "set a timer for ten minutes," and Siri replies with something like:

> "The Clock app is not on the device."

The Clock app is right there. It works fine when you tap it. Siri just can't seem to find it.

You're not alone. Owners have been reporting this on Apple's own support forums since watchOS 27 came out in mid-September. Here are the fixes people say have worked, from easiest to most drastic.

## Fix 1: Turn Siri off and back on (try this first)

This is the fix the original poster on Apple's forum confirmed worked for them.

1. On your **iPhone**, open **Settings > Siri**.
2. Turn off Siri (on some setups this is under **Talk & Type to Siri**).
3. Turn it back on.
4. **Restart your iPhone.**
5. **Restart your Apple Watch** (hold the side button, then tap the power icon).

Then try "set a timer for one minute" again.

In their words: "Turning off Siri and turning it back on solved my problem."

## Fix 2: Switch from Siri AI (Beta) back to regular Siri

Several users noticed the problem only appeared after they turned on the new **Siri AI (Beta)** that arrived with iOS 27. When they switched back to the standard Siri, timers worked again.

One user put it this way: after they "backed off to 'regular' Siri from Siri AI (Beta)," watch Siri timer commands started working.

To switch back, look in **Settings > Siri** on your iPhone for the Siri AI (Beta) option and turn it off. [ADD: exact path/label once confirmed with screenshot]

You lose the new Siri features for now, but you get a working timer. You can switch Siri AI back on after Apple releases a fix.

## Fix 3: Check for updates

Apple has already shipped **watchOS 27.0.1** with a fix for a separate restart bug, and more point updates are likely. On your iPhone, open the **Watch app > General > Software Update**, and install anything available. Do the same for iOS in **Settings > General > Software Update**.

## Fix 4: Unpair and re-pair (last resort)

Some users erased and set up their watch as new, and it worked. Others found it didn't help. Since it's a lot of effort, only try it if the fixes above fail.

Before you do: make sure your iPhone is backed up. Unpairing through the Watch app creates a backup of your watch automatically.

## While you wait: quick workarounds

- **Use the Timer app directly.** Open it and tap a preset. It's two taps instead of a voice command.
- **Use the Smart Stack or a complication.** Add a Timer complication to your watch face for one-tap access.
- **Ask your iPhone instead** if it's nearby.

## Why is this happening?

Apple hasn't commented on this specific bug. The pattern in user reports points to the handoff between the new Siri AI and the Watch's apps. Brand-new assistant systems often break small, reliable things at first. This is one of those.

## FAQ

**Why does Siri say the Clock app is not on my Apple Watch?**
Users report this error after updating to watchOS 27, often when Siri AI (Beta) is turned on. It appears to be a software bug.

**How do I fix Siri timers on Apple Watch?**
Turn Siri off and on on your iPhone, then restart both devices. If that doesn't work, switch from Siri AI (Beta) back to standard Siri.

**Will Apple fix it?**
Apple hasn't publicly acknowledged this specific bug yet, but watchOS point updates are already rolling out. Keep your devices updated.

**Can I still set timers without Siri?**
Yes. Open the Timer app directly or add a Timer complication to your watch face.
