# Rubber Plant Drooping After Repotting? What's Normal and How to Help It Recover

You gave your rubber plant (*Ficus elastica*) a bigger, nicer pot. A few days later, it's sulking: leaves drooping, maybe a yellow leaf or two falling off.

Don't panic. **Some drooping after repotting is very common**, and most rubber plants bounce back. But there's one mistake that turns a temporary sulk into a real problem.

## Why rubber plants droop after repotting

**1. Transplant shock (normal)**
Repotting disturbs roots, even when you're careful. Tiny root hairs that absorb water get damaged, so for a little while the plant can't take up water as well. Leaves droop to reduce water loss.

**Clues:** drooping starts within days of repotting, soil is appropriately moist, plant otherwise looks healthy.

**2. Too much water in a bigger pot (the common mistake)**
A bigger pot holds more soil, and more soil holds more water. A root system sized for the old pot can't use all that moisture, so the soil stays wet for days. Roots start to suffocate, and the plant droops, which looks exactly like thirst. So people water more. That's how root rot starts.

**Clues:** soil still wet days after watering, yellowing lower leaves, a musty smell, soft stems at the base.

**3. Too dry**
Less common, but new potting mix can be dry and hydrophobic, and water may run straight through or around the root ball.

**Clues:** soil pulling away from the pot edges, very light pot, crispy leaves.

**4. A change in location**
Repotting often means moving the plant. Rubber plants, like many figs, can react to changes in light and temperature by drooping or dropping leaves.

## What to do this week

1. **Check the soil with your finger**, a couple of inches deep. Don't water on a schedule.
2. **If it's wet:** don't water. Let the top few inches dry out before watering again.
3. **If it's bone dry:** water slowly and thoroughly until water drains out the bottom.
4. **Put it back** in its usual bright, indirect light spot, away from drafts and heaters.
5. **Don't fertilize** for several weeks after repotting. Roots need to recover first.
6. **Be patient.** Recovery from transplant shock can take a week or a few weeks.

## Signs of root rot

If drooping gets worse and you see:
- Several leaves yellowing and dropping
- Soil that stays soggy
- A sour or rotten smell
- Soft, dark stem at the soil line

…unpot it and look at the roots. Healthy roots are firm and light-colored. Rotten roots are dark, mushy and may smell.

**If you find rot:** trim the rotten roots with clean scissors, repot into fresh, dry-ish, well-draining mix in a pot that's **only slightly bigger** than the root ball, and water sparingly until it recovers.

## How to repot next time

- Go **only 1–2 inches (2–5 cm) larger** in pot diameter
- Use a pot with **drainage holes**
- Use a chunky, **well-draining mix**
- Repot in **spring or early summer** when the plant is actively growing
- Water once after repotting, then **wait for the soil to dry partly** before watering again

## FAQ

**Is it normal for a rubber plant to droop after repotting?**
Yes, some drooping from transplant shock is common and usually temporary.

**How long does transplant shock last in a rubber plant?**
Often from about a week to a few weeks, depending on how much the roots were disturbed.

**Should I water my rubber plant after repotting?**
Water once, then let the soil partly dry before watering again. Overwatering in a bigger pot is a common cause of problems.

**Why is my rubber plant dropping leaves after repotting?**
Transplant shock, overwatering, or a change in light or location are the usual causes.
