# Why Are My Turkey Poults Fighting? Pecking Order, Crowding and When to Separate

Search this and most results talk about **adult toms** squaring up in spring, or wild turkey flock hierarchy. Poults in a brooder are a different situation.

**Some sparring is normal from the first week.** Constant fighting, bloody heads or picked-bare necks are not: those are brooder-management problems.

## Normal sparring vs real trouble

**Normal:**
- Short chest-bumps and jump-ups, then both walk away
- Grabbing at each other's beaks or snoods briefly
- Chasing that stops within a few seconds
- No blood, no bird hiding in a corner all day

**Not normal:**
- One bird pinned and pecked repeatedly
- **Blood** on heads, necks or vents (blood attracts more pecking, fast)
- A poult that won't come to feed or water
- Feathers missing around the neck and tail

## The usual causes of real fighting

**1. Crowding**
This is the number one cause. Poults grow fast and brooder space that was fine at day three is tight by week two. Plan to expand the space or split the group as they grow.

**2. Too few feeders and drinkers**
Fighting often happens *at the feeder*. If birds have to compete for a spot, the dominant ones guard it. Add feeder length and a second drinker rather than refilling the same one.

**3. Brooder too hot**
Overheated poults get restless and irritable, pile at the edges and pick. Watch behaviour, not just the thermometer: birds spread evenly away from the heat source is right; birds panting with wings out is too hot; birds piled directly under it is too cold.

**4. Light that's too bright, for too long**
Very bright, 24-hour light increases picking. Dim it once they've found feed and water.

**5. Boredom**
Bare brooder floors give poults nothing to do but each other. Add perches at a safe height, a hanging cabbage or green leaves, and clean litter to scratch in.

**6. Diet**
Poults need a higher protein starter than chicks (commonly 26–28% for the first weeks). Under-fed or under-protein birds pick more.

## What to do right now if there's blood

1. **Remove the injured bird** to a clean, warm, separate space with its own feed and water.
2. **Clean the wound** and keep it clean; a vet can advise on treatment for anything deep or around the eyes.
3. **Don't return it** until the wound is fully healed and no longer red — poults target red.
4. **Fix the cause** meanwhile (space, feeders, heat, light), or the next bird becomes the target.

## Mixing poults with chicks

A common cause of one-sided fighting is mixing species or ages. Chicks move faster and peck differently; poults are slower to learn feed and water. If you brood them together, expect to watch closely, give much more space, and be ready to split them.

## When it's a tom thing

From several months old, young toms start displaying and sparring properly. At that point the fixes are different: more space, separating toms you don't need, and not keeping too many males with too few hens.

## FAQ

**Is it normal for turkey poults to peck each other?**
Light sparring and beak-grabbing is normal. Drawing blood, or one bird repeatedly attacking another, is not.

**At what age do turkey poults start fighting?**
Dominance behaviour shows up in the first weeks, even in day-old poults, and gets more serious as they mature.

**How much brooder space do turkey poults need?**
More than chicks, and increasing weekly. If they can't all rest away from the heat source without touching, it's too tight.

**Should I separate a bullied poult?**
Yes, if it's injured or being kept from food and water. Keep it separate until the wound has healed.
