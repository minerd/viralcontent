# How to Fix Kimchi With Too Much Garlic: 6 Ways to Save the Batch

You tasted your kimchi and it's… aggressive. Hot, raw, garlicky, the kind of garlic burn that stays with you. Maybe you misread the recipe, maybe your garlic cloves were huge, maybe you just love garlic a bit too much.

Good news: **garlicky kimchi is very fixable**, and in many cases time alone will help. Here's what to do.

## 1. Let it ferment longer

This is the easiest fix. Raw garlic's harsh bite **mellows as kimchi ferments**. Fresh kimchi often tastes much more garlicky than kimchi that's been fermenting for a week or two.

**Try:** leave it at room temperature for another day or two (checking and pressing it down under the brine), then move it to the fridge and taste it every few days. Many people are surprised how much it changes.

## 2. Dilute the batch

If it's really overpowering, **add more of everything except garlic**:
- Salt and add more **napa cabbage**, or
- Add **radish** (Korean radish/mu), cut into cubes or matchsticks, salted briefly
- Add some **green onion** or **carrot**
- Adjust **chili flakes, fish sauce and a little sugar** to match

Mix it through the existing kimchi so the new vegetables pick up the seasoning and ferment together.

## 3. Balance it with sweetness

A little sweetness takes the edge off harsh garlic:
- A spoon of **sugar**, or
- A bit of **grated Asian pear or apple** blended into some of the seasoning paste

Mix into the kimchi and let it rest.

## 4. Cook with it

Cooking transforms garlic from sharp to sweet and mellow. Garlicky kimchi is perfect for:
- **Kimchi fried rice**
- **Kimchi jjigae** (kimchi stew)
- **Kimchi pancakes** (kimchijeon)
- **Kimchi fried noodles**
- **Kimchi grilled cheese**

Well-fermented, garlicky kimchi often makes *better* stews than mild kimchi.

## 5. Serve it with rich or mild foods

Eat it alongside things that soften strong flavors: plain rice, eggs, tofu, fatty pork, noodles. The garlic becomes an accent instead of the headline.

## 6. Use it as a flavor base

Chop it finely and use small amounts as a seasoning: in dumpling filling, mixed into mayo for sandwiches, in dressings or marinades.

## About blue or green garlic

Sometimes garlic in kimchi turns **blue or green**. It looks strange, but it's a harmless natural reaction between compounds in garlic and the acidic environment. It doesn't mean your kimchi has gone bad.

## Next time

- **Weigh your garlic** instead of counting cloves (cloves vary a lot in size)
- Start with **less garlic**; you can always add more to a finished batch
- Some people **blend garlic with ginger, pear and onion** in the paste for a rounder flavor

## FAQ

**Can you fix kimchi with too much garlic?**
Yes. Let it ferment longer, dilute with more vegetables, add a little sweetness, or cook with it.

**Does garlic in kimchi mellow over time?**
Yes, garlic's raw bite softens as kimchi ferments.

**Why did the garlic in my kimchi turn blue?**
It's a harmless natural reaction in acidic conditions and doesn't mean the kimchi is spoiled.

**What can I make with very garlicky kimchi?**
Kimchi fried rice, kimchi stew, kimchi pancakes and other cooked dishes, where garlic turns mellow and sweet.
