# Can You Use Wood Stove Pellets for Cat Litter? Yes, With 3 Important Rules

Here's a money-saving trick a lot of cat owners swear by: the same **wood pellets** sold for pellet stoves can be used as cat litter. A large bag often costs a fraction of what pine litter does.

It works, but you need to choose the right pellets and set up the box correctly.

## How wood pellet litter works

Wood pellets are compressed sawdust. When your cat pees on them, the pellets **absorb the liquid and break down into sawdust**. The wood also helps absorb odor.

Unlike clumping clay litter, you don't scoop clumps. Instead you:
- **Scoop solids** daily
- **Sift or stir** so the sawdust falls to the bottom (or use a sifting litter box system)
- **Replace the sawdust** and top up with fresh pellets

## Rule 1: Only plain wood, no additives

This is the most important part. Use pellets that are:
- **100% wood**
- **No additives, binders, accelerants or fragrances**
- Clearly labeled as natural/untreated

Check the bag carefully. If it doesn't clearly state what's in it, pick a different brand.

## Rule 2: Avoid aromatic woods like cedar

Strongly aromatic woods (like cedar) are commonly advised against for small animal bedding because of their oils. For cats, stick to pellets made from **pine or mixed softwoods/hardwoods without added scents**, or simply buy pellets sold specifically as cat litter if you're unsure.

## Rule 3: Switch gradually

Cats can be very particular about litter texture. A sudden switch can lead to accidents outside the box.

**How to switch:**
1. Mix a small amount of pellets into your current litter.
2. Increase the pellet ratio a little every few days.
3. Keep an eye on whether your cat uses the box normally.
4. If your cat refuses, try a finer pellet or go slower.

Some cats dislike the hard pellet feel on their paws at first. Many adjust within a week or two.

## Pros

- **Cheaper** than most commercial litters, especially bought in bulk
- **Low dust** compared with many clay litters
- **Good odor control** for many households
- **Lightweight sawdust** to dispose of, and it's biodegradable

## Cons

- **Not clumping**, so a different cleaning routine
- **Some cats refuse it**
- **Sawdust can track** a bit
- **Big bags are heavy** to carry

## Disposal

Check your local rules. Used cat litter should **not go in vegetable gardens or compost** used for food, because cat waste can carry parasites like toxoplasma. Don't flush litter.

## FAQ

**Are wood stove pellets safe for cats?**
Plain wood pellets without additives are widely used as cat litter. Avoid pellets with binders, accelerants, fragrances or aromatic woods like cedar.

**Do wood pellets control cat litter smell?**
Many owners find they control odor well, as long as sawdust is removed and pellets are topped up regularly.

**How do you clean wood pellet cat litter?**
Scoop solids daily, sift out the sawdust, and add fresh pellets.

**Can I compost used wood pellet litter?**
Don't use it in compost meant for food gardens, since cat waste can carry parasites.
