# Android's New Motion Assist Could End Car Sickness When You Use Your Phone

If you've ever tried to read your phone in the back of a car and felt your stomach turn within five minutes, Android has a new feature for you.

It's called **Motion Assist**, and it showed up in Google's September 2026 system update. Google's description: it "can help mitigate motion sickness with subtle, visual shapes that move in sync with a vehicle's motion."

Here's how that works, and how to get it.

## Why phones make car sickness worse

Motion sickness mostly comes from a mismatch. Your inner ear feels the car turning, braking and bumping. Your eyes, locked on a still phone screen, say nothing is moving. Your brain gets two conflicting reports and responds with nausea.

That's why looking out the window helps: your eyes and ears agree again.

## What Motion Assist does

Motion Assist tries to bring your eyes back into agreement without making you look away from your screen. It adds **subtle shapes on the screen that move in sync with the vehicle's movement**. When the car turns, the shapes shift. When it brakes, they move accordingly.

Your eyes get a small visual cue that matches what your inner ear is feeling, while you keep reading or scrolling.

If that sounds familiar, Apple added a similar idea to iPhones a couple of years ago, called Vehicle Motion Cues. Now Android has its own version.

## How to get it

Motion Assist appeared in **Google Play services version 26.37**, released September 21, 2026, and it's designed for phones.

Two important caveats:
- **Google's changelogs list features before they reach everyone.** As 9to5Google notes, "a feature appearing in the changelog does not mean it's widely available. Some capabilities take months to fully launch."
- Rollout may depend on your phone and Android version.

To give yourself the best chance:
1. Update your phone: **Settings > System > Software update**.
2. Update Google Play services: open the Play Store, search "Google Play services," and update if available.
3. Look for Motion Assist in your settings (try searching "motion" in the Settings search bar). [ADD: exact location once live, with screenshot]

## Tips that help either way

Whether or not you have Motion Assist yet:
- **Sit in the front** if you can, or in the middle back seat facing forward.
- **Look up at the horizon** every minute or so.
- **Keep the screen brighter** and text bigger so you're not straining.
- **Crack a window** for fresh air.
- **Listen instead of read.** Podcasts and audiobooks don't cause the same mismatch.

## Will it work for everyone?

Probably not completely. Motion sickness varies a lot from person to person. For some, visual cues like these make a big difference. For others with severe motion sickness, they'll help a bit at best.

But it's free, it's built into the phone, and if it lets you answer a few emails on a road trip without feeling awful, that's a win.

## FAQ

**What is Motion Assist on Android?**
A feature that displays subtle shapes on screen that move with a vehicle's motion to help reduce motion sickness.

**How do I turn on Motion Assist?**
It arrived in Google Play services version 26.37. Update your phone and Google Play services, then look for it in Settings. It may take time to reach all devices.

**Is Motion Assist the same as Apple's Vehicle Motion Cues?**
It's a similar idea: on-screen visual cues that match the vehicle's movement.

**Does Motion Assist work for the driver?**
It's meant for passengers. Drivers shouldn't be using their phones.
