# Why Are My Turkey Chicks So Loud? What Constant Poult Peeping Means

Turkey poults are loud birds. But there's a difference between **cheerful background peeping** and the **sharp, insistent, non-stop call** that means something is wrong.

**Loud, continuous peeping is a distress call.** Run through the list below before you assume it's just personality.

## The four classic causes

**1. They're cold**
This is the most common one. Cold poults peep loudly and pile together under the heat source. Add heat or lower the lamp.

**2. They're too hot**
Overheated poults also complain, but they'll be spread to the far corners, panting, wings held away from the body. Raise the lamp or reduce heat.

**3. They can't find food or water**
Poults are slower than chicks to work out feeders and drinkers. Dip beaks in water, use shallow dishes with marbles or stones for visibility, and scatter crumb on a paper towel so they can find it. A hungry brooder is a noisy brooder.

**4. They're lonely**
Turkeys are extremely social and imprint strongly. A single poult, or one separated from the group, will call constantly — the sound carries a very long way. Two or more poults are far quieter than one.

## Less obvious causes

- **They've imprinted on you.** They call when you leave the room and quiet down when you come back. This is normal and eases as they mature.
- **The group was moved.** Poults that can no longer see brooder-mates in the next pen will call for them.
- **Bright light, 24 hours a day.** Dim it once they are feeding and drinking reliably.
- **Something is frightening them:** a cat at the brooder edge, a dog, repeated noise.
- **One bird is unwell.** A poult that peeps and stands apart, eyes closed, not eating, needs checking separately.

## The 60-second brooder check

1. **Where are the birds?** Evenly spread = temperature about right. Piled under heat = cold. Pressed to the walls, panting = hot.
2. **Is the water clean, full and reachable?** Shallow enough that they can't get in and chill or drown.
3. **Is there feed they can see?** Turkey starter crumb, high protein, scattered where they'll find it.
4. **Is anything watching them?** Remove the audience.
5. **Are they alone?** One poult will always be noisier than a group.

## When loud is just loud

Poults chatter, trill and peep while active and feeding — that's a healthy sound and it rises and falls. The noise to act on is the **monotonous, urgent, non-stop** call, especially if it keeps going in the dark, or when all the birds are doing it at once.

## FAQ

**Do turkey poults get quieter as they grow?**
Yes, the constant peeping fades as they mature, though turkeys stay vocal birds.

**Why does my turkey poult peep non-stop when I leave?**
It has bonded to you and is contact-calling. Company from other poults helps more than anything else.

**Is loud peeping a sign of illness?**
Usually it's cold, hunger, thirst or loneliness. But a single bird calling while standing hunched and not eating should be checked.

**Should I keep just one turkey poult?**
Avoid it if you can. Single poults are noisy, stressed and harder to rear.
