# Why Is My Guinea Fowl Limping? Bumblefoot, Injuries and Other Causes

Guinea fowl are tough, busy birds that cover a lot of ground. So a limping guinea stands out right away.

Most limps have a simple cause you can find by looking closely at the foot and leg. Here's how to work through it.

*General information. Contact a vet who treats poultry if you're unsure or the bird is getting worse.*

## Step 1: Catch and examine

Guineas are flighty, so catch at night on the roost if possible, when they're calmer. Gently check:
- **Bottom of the foot**: swelling, a dark scab, heat
- **Toes**: cuts, swelling, anything wrapped around them
- **Legs and joints**: swelling, heat, bruising, wounds
- **Scales**: raised or crusty (possible mites)
- Compare with the other leg

## Common causes

**1. Bumblefoot**
A bacterial infection of the foot pad, often entering through a small cut.
**Signs:** swollen foot pad, often with a **dark scab** in the center, warmth, limping.
**Action:** bumblefoot can worsen if untreated. A vet can advise on treatment, which may include cleaning and draining. Keep the bird on soft, clean bedding.

**2. Injury**
Guineas fly, jump from roosts, run from predators, and squeeze through fences.
**Signs:** sudden limp, possible swelling or bruising, no dark scab.
**Action:** isolate in a small, quiet space with soft bedding, food and water within reach. Many sprains improve with rest. If there's a visible break or no improvement, see a vet.

**3. String, hair or thread wrapped around a toe**
This is common in free-ranging birds and can cut off circulation.
**Signs:** swollen, discolored toe; thread visible when you look closely.
**Action:** carefully remove it with fine scissors or tweezers. If it's embedded deeply, see a vet.

**4. Frostbite (winter)**
Toes exposed to severe cold can be damaged.
**Signs:** discolored, swollen or blackened toes after cold weather.
**Action:** provide a dry, draft-free coop with wide roosts so birds can cover their feet. See a vet for significant damage.

**5. Scaly leg mites**
Mites burrow under leg scales.
**Signs:** raised, crusty scales, sometimes limping.
**Action:** treat the bird and coop as recommended by a vet or poultry specialist.

**6. Nutritional problems (especially keets)**
Young guineas grow fast and have high protein needs. Poor diets can lead to leg weakness.
**Action:** feed an appropriate high-protein game bird or turkey starter for keets, as your supplier recommends.

**7. Infections affecting joints or nerves**
Some diseases cause lameness, sometimes in several birds at once.
**Action:** if multiple birds are limping or birds seem otherwise ill, contact a vet.

## FAQ

**Why is my guinea fowl limping?**
Common causes include bumblefoot, injury, string wrapped around a toe, frostbite, scaly leg mites and nutritional problems.

**What does bumblefoot look like?**
A swollen foot pad, often with a dark scab in the center.

**Can a limping guinea fowl recover on its own?**
Mild sprains often improve with rest. Infections, embedded string or multiple limping birds need attention.

**What do guinea keets need to eat?**
A high-protein starter feed, such as a game bird or turkey starter.
