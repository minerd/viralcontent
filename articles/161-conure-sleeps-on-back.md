# Is It Normal for Conures to Sleep on Their Back? What Owners Should Know

Most parrots sleep perched, standing on one foot. So the first time you see your conure **lying down**, or even **on its back**, it can give you a real scare.

Many conure owners report that their birds, especially when very relaxed or snuggled in a sleep tent or hut, **lie down or roll onto their backs**. It can be a quirky, normal behavior. But lying down can also be a sign of illness, so it's important to know the difference.

*General information. Birds hide illness well; when in doubt, contact an avian vet.*

## When it's probably fine

- Your conure **sleeps in a hut, tent or snuggle bag** and lies down in it
- It rolls onto its back while **playing** or when you're cuddling it
- It **wakes up alert**, climbs normally, and perches easily
- It's **eating, playing and chattering** as usual
- Droppings look normal

Some owners describe their conures as "floppy" or "playing dead" for fun. Certain individuals just like lying down.

**Note:** many avian vets and experienced keepers have concerns about fabric huts and tents, since they can encourage hormonal nesting behavior and some birds chew and swallow fabric threads. Check tents often for loose threads and wear, and discuss with your vet if you're unsure.

## When lying down is a warning sign

Contact an avian vet if your conure:
- **Lies down on the cage floor**, not in a hut
- **Can't perch** or keeps falling
- Is **fluffed up**, sleepy, or less responsive
- Has **tail bobbing** or labored breathing
- Is **eating less** or has changes in droppings
- Suddenly starts lying down when it never did before

## Encouraging healthy sleep

- **10–12 hours of dark, quiet sleep** at night
- A **comfortable perch** at the right diameter for your bird's feet
- A consistent bedtime
- A **dim night light** if your bird has night frights

## FAQ

**Why does my conure sleep on its back?**
Some conures lie down or roll over when very relaxed, especially in huts or tents. Make sure it can perch and acts normally when awake.

**Is it bad if my bird is lying down?**
It can be. Lying on the cage floor, weakness, or fluffed-up sleepiness are warning signs.

**Are sleep tents safe for conures?**
Opinions differ. Some can encourage hormonal behavior, and loose threads can be a hazard. Check them often and ask your avian vet.

**How much sleep do conures need?**
Many care sources recommend 10–12 hours of dark, quiet sleep at night.
