# Why Does My Lovebird Regurgitate His Food? Affection vs Vomiting

Your lovebird bobs his head, makes little pumping movements, and brings up food… for you, for a mirror, or for his favorite toy.

It looks alarming, but **in lovebirds and other parrots, regurgitation is often a sign of love.** Here's what it means, and how to tell it apart from vomiting, which is a health concern.

*General information. See an avian vet if your bird shows signs of illness.*

## Regurgitation: affection and courtship

In the wild, parrots feed their mates and chicks by regurgitating food. Pet lovebirds do the same to anyone or anything they're bonded to:
- **A mate** or cage companion
- **A mirror** (they may think their reflection is a mate)
- **A favorite toy**
- **You**

**Signs it's courtship regurgitation:**
- **Head bobbing** and pumping motions
- Bird looks **focused and calm**, often cooing or chattering softly
- Food is brought up **deliberately** and offered
- The bird is otherwise **healthy and active**

## Vomiting: a health concern

Vomiting is different. It's often messy and involuntary.

**Signs of vomiting:**
- **Head shaking** that flings food around
- Food stuck to the **face, head or feathers**
- Bird looks **uncomfortable or unwell**
- **Fluffed up**, lethargic, sleeping more
- Changes in **droppings**
- Not eating or losing weight

**Possible causes of vomiting** include infections, crop problems, toxins (such as heavy metals from chewing unsafe objects), and other illnesses.

**Action:** see an avian vet promptly. Birds can decline quickly.

## Why regurgitation can still be a problem

Occasional affectionate regurgitation is normal. But frequent courtship behavior often means your lovebird is **hormonal**, and chronic hormonal behavior can lead to:
- Aggression and biting
- Excessive screaming
- In females, **chronic egg laying**, which can be harmful

## How to reduce hormonal regurgitation

- **Remove mirrors** or limit time with them
- **Rotate toys**, so your bird doesn't fixate on one "partner"
- **Avoid petting** anywhere except the head and neck (stroking the back and under the wings can be stimulating)
- **Remove nest-like spaces**: huts, boxes, dark corners
- Provide **10–12 hours of dark, quiet sleep** at night; long days can trigger hormones
- **Don't encourage it**: calmly walk away when your bird starts regurgitating for you

## FAQ

**Why is my lovebird throwing up on me?**
Most often it's affectionate regurgitation, a courtship behavior. If it's messy and the bird seems unwell, it may be vomiting.

**Is it normal for lovebirds to regurgitate?**
Yes, occasional regurgitation for a mate, mirror, toy or person is normal parrot behavior.

**How do I tell regurgitation from vomiting in birds?**
Regurgitation is deliberate with head bobbing; vomiting involves head shaking, food flung around, and signs of illness.

**How do I stop my lovebird from regurgitating?**
Reduce hormonal triggers: remove mirrors and nest-like spaces, limit petting to the head, and ensure long nights of sleep.
