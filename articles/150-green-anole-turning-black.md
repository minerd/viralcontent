# Why Is My Green Anole Black? Stress, Temperature and What Dark Colors Mean

Green anoles are famous for changing color, which is why people sometimes call them "American chameleons." They shift between **bright green** and **brown**.

But when yours turns **very dark brown or nearly black** and stays that way, it's a signal worth paying attention to.

*General information. See a reptile vet if dark coloring comes with other signs of illness.*

## What anole colors usually mean

- **Bright green**: often relaxed, warm, in a bright environment
- **Brown**: can be normal when cooler, resting, or on a brown background
- **Very dark brown or black**: often **stress**, sometimes **cold**

## Common causes of dark coloring

**1. Stress**
The most common reason. Stress triggers include:
- **Handling** (anoles generally don't enjoy being handled)
- **A hand reaching into the enclosure**
- **Other anoles**, especially other males, which may fight
- **Pets** watching nearby
- **A new enclosure** or recent move
- **Seeing their reflection** in glass

Some stressed males also show **dark spots behind the eyes**.

**2. Temperature**
Anoles are cold-blooded and rely on their environment for warmth. A cold anole often turns dark.
**Check:** basking area and cooler areas with a digital thermometer.

**3. Lighting**
Anoles need **UVB** and a proper day/night cycle. Poor lighting can contribute to stress.

**4. Humidity**
Anoles need reasonable humidity and water droplets to drink (many drink from misted leaves rather than bowls).

**5. Illness**
A sick anole may stay dark and lose its usual ability to change color.

## When to worry

See a vet if dark coloring comes with:
- **Lethargy**, not moving much
- **Not eating** for more than a short time
- **Weight loss**, visible hip bones or thin tail
- **Swelling**, sores, or stuck shed
- **Trouble climbing** or weak limbs

## How to help your anole relax

- **Minimize handling**
- **House males separately**
- **Add dense plants and hiding spots**
- **Cover part of the glass** so it can't see reflections or outside movement
- **Correct temperature, UVB and humidity**
- **Mist daily** so it can drink droplets

## FAQ

**Why did my green anole turn black?**
Usually stress or cold. Other causes include poor lighting, dehydration or illness.

**Is it bad if my anole stays brown?**
Brown can be normal, but staying very dark for long periods may signal ongoing stress or health issues.

**Why does my anole have black spots behind its eyes?**
Dark eyespots are often associated with stress, especially in males.

**How do I make my anole green again?**
Reduce stress, keep it warm, and provide proper lighting, humidity and hiding spots.
