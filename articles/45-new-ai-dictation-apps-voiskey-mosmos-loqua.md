# Voiskey vs MosMos vs Loqua: 3 New AI Dictation Apps Challenging Wispr Flow

Something is happening with voice typing. In September alone, three AI dictation apps landed near the top of Product Hunt: **Voiskey**, **MosMos** and **Loqua**.

They all make the same pitch: you talk like a normal person, with the "ums," restarts and half-finished thoughts, and the app hands you clean, ready-to-send text. That's the idea Wispr Flow made popular, and now there's real competition.

## Voiskey: the one for your phone

Voiskey describes itself as "AI voice typing that understands your meaning, not just your words."

**What stands out:**
- It's the only one of the three that runs on **iOS and Android** as well as Mac and Windows. If you want better dictation on your phone, this is the one to look at.
- It handles "fillers, stumbles, change of mind, grammar slips."
- It **learns your names, jargon and spelling preferences** over time.
- It adapts tone and formatting to what you're writing.
- It can turn speech into an "AI-ready prompt," for people who talk to ChatGPT or Claude all day.

**Best for:** people who text and email a lot on their phone, and anyone who works in more than one language.

## MosMos: the one for meetings

MosMos has a simple tagline: "We speak faster than we type."

**What stands out:**
- **Press Fn once** on a Mac (Right Alt on Windows) to start, again to finish. That's it.
- Three levels of "context-aware refinement": Basic, Standard and Advanced.
- **Meeting mode** (Fn+Shift) transcribes meetings with **speaker identification, summaries and to-do lists.**
- Custom keywords for names and terms it would otherwise get wrong.
- On privacy: "Your history stays on this device."

**Best for:** Mac users who want dictation *and* meeting notes in one app, without sending their history to the cloud.

## Loqua: the power-user one

Loqua claims dictation at 220 words per minute, compared with about 45 for typing.

**What stands out:**
- Real-time cleanup that removes "filler words, cuts repetition" and tidies phrasing.
- Automatic formatting for lists and headings.
- **"Capture to Ask":** take a screenshot and ask AI about it.
- **Voice editing:** tell it to rewrite what you just said.
- Text-to-speech, and voice commands for reminders and opening apps.
- Works across 50+ apps, from Google Docs and Slack to VS Code and Terminal.

**Best for:** writers, developers and knowledge workers who want dictation plus a voice-controlled assistant.

## Which one should you try?

- **On your phone?** Voiskey. It's the only one here with mobile apps today.
- **Lots of meetings on a Mac?** MosMos.
- **Want the most features on desktop?** Loqua, using the 14-day trial.
- **Already happy with Wispr Flow?** Try one alongside it for a week before switching. Dictation apps are personal: the "best" one is whichever understands *your* voice and accent.

## A privacy note that applies to all of them

Dictation apps hear everything you say to them, and some can see your screen. Before you commit, check:
- Where your audio is processed (on-device or cloud)
- Whether your recordings are stored, and for how long
- Whether your data is used to train models
- How to delete your history

MosMos is the only one of the three that states on its homepage that history stays on your device. For the others, read the privacy policy before you dictate anything sensitive.
