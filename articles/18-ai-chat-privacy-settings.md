# Your AI Chats Aren't as Private as You Think. Here's What to Check

People tell chatbots things they wouldn't tell their friends. Health worries, relationship problems, money trouble, work secrets, the draft of the angry email they didn't send.

It feels private. You're alone, typing into a box, and the answer is just for you.

But a chat with an AI is not a diary. Depending on the service and your settings, what you type might be stored for a long time, reviewed by people, used to train future models, or handed over if a court asks for it. None of that is secret. It's in the privacy policies. Most people just never look.

So let's look.

## Five things to check in your settings

Every major service puts these in slightly different places, and they change them often. So instead of screenshots that'll be outdated next month, here's what to look for. Search your chatbot's settings (or its help center) for each of these.

### 1. "Improve the model" / training

Many services can use your conversations to train future models unless you opt out, especially on free plans. Look for a setting with words like "improve the model," "training," or "help improve." Decide consciously whether you're okay with it.

Business and enterprise plans usually don't train on your data by default. If you use AI at work, check which plan your company is on.

### 2. Chat history and retention

How long are your chats kept? Can you delete them? Does deleting them in the app actually delete them from the company's servers, or just hide them from you?

Look for "data controls," "chat history," or "activity." Some services let you turn history off entirely or auto-delete after a set time.

### 3. Memory

Many chatbots now remember facts about you across conversations. That's handy. It also means something you mentioned once might keep coming back.

Find the memory settings and actually read what it has stored about you. People are often surprised. Delete anything you don't want kept.

### 4. Connected apps

If you've connected your AI to your email, calendar, drive, or other accounts, it may be able to read far more than you type. Check which connections are active and remove any you don't use.

### 5. Human review

Some services have people review a sample of conversations for safety and quality. This is usually mentioned in the privacy policy or help center. It's worth knowing before you share something sensitive.

## Five things to never paste into a chatbot

Regardless of settings, a good rule is: don't type anything you'd be seriously hurt by if it leaked.

1. **Passwords, PINs, and one-time codes.** No legitimate AI task needs them.
2. **Full ID numbers.** Passport, national ID, social security number.
3. **Full card or bank details.**
4. **Other people's private information.** Your friend's medical situation, a colleague's salary, a client's data. It's not only your privacy on the line.
5. **Confidential work material** unless your company has explicitly approved that AI tool. Pasting source code, client contracts, or unreleased plans into a personal chatbot account has gotten people fired.

## "But I need help with something personal"

That's fair. AI can be genuinely helpful for sensitive topics. You can still get the help while sharing less:

- **Remove names.** "My coworker" works just as well as her real name.
- **Round the numbers.** "About $60k salary" instead of your exact figure.
- **Leave out identifying details.** The AI doesn't need your city, employer, or doctor's name to help.
- **Use temporary or incognito chats** if your service offers them.

You'll get almost the same quality of help with a fraction of the exposure.

## What about AI therapy and health questions?

Lots of people use AI to talk through feelings or understand symptoms. It can be helpful, but remember: a chatbot is not covered by the same confidentiality rules as a doctor or therapist. If it's something you'd only tell a professional, it's worth thinking twice before typing it into a general AI app.

## The five-minute privacy checkup

1. Open your chatbot's settings.
2. Check the training setting. Decide on/off.
3. Check the memory. Delete what you don't want.
4. Check connected apps. Remove unused ones.
5. Delete old chats you'd rather not keep.

Do it for every AI app you use. Then put a reminder to do it again in six months, because the settings will have moved by then.

## FAQ

**Does ChatGPT save my conversations?**
By default, yes, chats are saved to your history. You can delete them and adjust data settings, and OpenAI describes its retention policies in its help center.

**Can AI companies read my chats?**
Depending on the service, some conversations may be reviewed by staff for safety or quality. Check each service's privacy policy.

**Is it safe to use AI for work?**
It depends on your company's rules and which plan you're using. Business plans usually have stronger data protections. When in doubt, ask your IT or security team.

**Does deleting a chat really delete it?**
Usually it's removed from your view immediately, and deleted from the company's systems after a retention period. Legal requirements can sometimes extend how long data is kept.
