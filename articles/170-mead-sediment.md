# Is It Normal for Mead to Have Sediment? Lees, Racking and Clearing Your Mead

You look at your fermenter and see a layer of cream, tan or grayish gunk at the bottom. Or you've bottled your mead and there's a fine dust settling in each bottle.

**Yes, sediment is completely normal in mead.** Here's what it is and how to manage it.

## What the sediment is

The layer at the bottom of your fermenter is called **lees**. It's mostly:
- **Dead and dormant yeast**
- **Particles from honey**, fruit or spices
- Proteins and other solids that settle out as mead clears

During primary fermentation, a thick layer is normal. Over time, more fine sediment keeps dropping as the mead clears.

## Racking: moving mead off the lees

**Racking** means siphoning the mead off the sediment into a clean, sanitized vessel.

**Why rack?**
- Clearer mead
- Less risk of **off flavors** from sitting on a thick layer of old yeast for a long time
- Less sediment in bottles

**How:**
1. Sanitize your siphon and new vessel
2. Place the fermenter higher than the new vessel
3. Siphon carefully, keeping the tube tip above the sediment
4. Leave the sediment behind
5. Top up or minimize headspace to limit oxygen exposure

Many mead makers rack once after primary fermentation slows and again as needed while the mead ages and clears.

## Helping mead clear

- **Time**: many meads clear on their own given enough time
- **Cold**: moving the mead somewhere cooler can help particles settle (often called "cold crashing")
- **Fining agents**: some brewers use products like bentonite or others designed for clearing wine and mead, following product instructions

## Sediment in bottles

A thin layer of sediment in bottles usually means:
- The mead was **bottled before it fully cleared**, or
- The mead was **bottle-carbonated** (sparkling mead), which leaves yeast sediment by design

**To reduce it:** wait until the mead is clear and stable before bottling, and rack carefully.

**Serving tip:** pour gently and leave the last bit in the bottle.

## When to be concerned

Sediment is normal. Be concerned about:
- **Mold** on the surface (fuzzy spots)
- A **bad smell** like rotten eggs that doesn't fade, or vinegar
- **Bulging** caps or corks (possible over-carbonation, which is a safety risk)

## FAQ

**Is sediment in mead bad?**
No. It's mostly yeast and is normal. Rack the mead off it to improve clarity.

**What is lees in mead?**
The sediment at the bottom of the fermenter, mostly dead yeast and other particles.

**How do I clear cloudy mead?**
Give it time, rack off sediment, and consider cold crashing or fining agents.

**Can I drink the sediment?**
It's generally harmless but can taste yeasty. Most people pour carefully and leave it behind.
