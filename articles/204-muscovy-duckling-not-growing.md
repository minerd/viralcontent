# Why Is My Muscovy Duckling Not Growing? Slow by Nature, or a Runt?

If you've raised Pekins or other commercial ducks before, Muscovy ducklings look like they've stalled. They haven't.

**Muscovies grow substantially slower than other domestic ducks** — it's one of the defining things about the breed. Before troubleshooting, check your expectations against the right species.

## 1. The breed is just slow

Pekins are bred to put on weight at speed. Muscovies take their time and fill out over months, and the drakes keep growing well past the point where other breeds have finished. A Muscovy duckling that looks small next to a same-age Pekin is not a problem.

**What to compare against:** other Muscovy ducklings of the same hatch, not other breeds, not a chart from a meat-duck guide.

## 2. One bird much smaller than its hatch-mates

If a duckling is **clearly behind its own siblings**, work through:

**Not getting to the feed**
Bigger siblings crowd a small duckling off the feeder. Add feeder space, use more than one station, and watch a full feeding to see whether it's actually eating.

**Vent blockage (pasted vent)**
Common in young waterfowl and easily missed under down. Droppings dry and seal the vent, the bird can't pass waste and stops growing. Check it, and soak the area clean with warm water, dabbing rather than pulling.

**A true runt**
Sometimes a duckling hatches small and stays small, often from a small or poorly incubated egg. Many catch up partially; some never do.

**What helps either way:** separate the small one with one companion (never alone), offer **chick-crumb-sized feed** it can manage, and **weigh it daily** on kitchen scales. Daily weight gain is the only reliable way to tell "slow" from "failing". Reintroduce it once it's gaining.

## 3. Feed problems

Muscovies are **grazers**. They want access to grass and forage daily, and a lot of their protein comes from that plus insects.

Things that go wrong:
- **Chick starter alone.** Chicken feed is low in **niacin** for waterfowl; shortage shows up as leg weakness and poor growth. Use waterfowl feed, or add brewer's yeast
- **Too much high-protein starter for too long.** With fast-growing ducklings, prolonged high-protein feed is linked to **angel wing** and leg problems. Many Muscovy keepers move off high-protein starter after the first week or two and lean on grazing plus a grower ration
- **Bread, scratch and treats** diluting the ration
- **Medicated chick feed** — avoid it for ducklings

## 4. Water, warmth and space

- **Water they can dip their whole head in**, always available. Ducklings clear their nostrils and eyes by submerging; without it they feed poorly
- But **not deep water unsupervised** — young ducklings chill and drown easily, and Muscovy ducklings are not waterproof until they're older
- Wet bedding chills them and chilled ducklings stop growing
- Space to walk and graze every day

## 5. Illness and parasites

A duckling that's small **and** also quiet, hunched, not eating, with odd droppings, needs a vet rather than a feed change. Coccidiosis, worms and bacterial infections all show up first as failure to grow.

## The practical test

| Observation | Reading |
|---|---|
| Whole group smaller than a Pekin chart | Normal — wrong comparison |
| One bird smaller but **gaining weight daily** | Slow, not failing — keep supporting it |
| One bird **not gaining** over several days | Investigate: vent, feed access, illness |
| Group poor growth + leg weakness | Niacin/feed problem |
| Small, hunched, not eating, bad droppings | Vet |

## FAQ

**How fast should Muscovy ducklings grow?**
Much slower than other domestic ducks, and drakes keep developing for months. Judge against other Muscovies.

**Can Muscovy ducklings have chicken starter?**
Not as the only feed — it's short on niacin for waterfowl. Use waterfowl feed or supplement with brewer's yeast.

**Should I separate a small duckling?**
Yes if it isn't reaching feed or isn't gaining — but always with a companion, and reintroduce once it's gaining.

**Do Muscovy ducklings need swimming water?**
They need water deep enough to submerge their heads, and supervised swimming only. They aren't waterproof when young and chill fast.
