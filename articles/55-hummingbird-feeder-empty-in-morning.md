# Why Is My Hummingbird Feeder Empty in the Morning? 6 Culprits (and How to Tell Which)

You fill the feeder in the evening. By morning it's empty, or nearly. And you know it wasn't the hummingbirds, because hummingbirds don't feed at night. They go into a kind of deep overnight rest called torpor and don't touch the feeder until first light.

So who's drinking your nectar? Here are the usual suspects, and the clues that point to each one.

## First, do this one quick check

Before you blame wildlife, go out at dawn and look at:
- **The ground under the feeder.** Wet, sticky patch? Think leak.
- **The feeder itself.** Tilted, on the ground, chewed ports, scratches? Think raccoon or bear.
- **The hook or pole.** Bent? Think bear.
- **The ports.** Sticky film all around them, but no damage? Think bats.

Now match your clues below.

## 1. Bats (the most surprising answer)

In parts of the US Southwest and Mexico, nectar-feeding bats visit hummingbird feeders at night, especially in **late summer and fall** as they migrate. They can drain a feeder in a few hours.

**Clues:** feeder undamaged but empty, sticky spots around the ports, no mess on the ground, happens mainly in late summer and fall.
**Where:** mostly southern Arizona, New Mexico and nearby areas.
**What to do:** bring the feeder in at dusk and put it back out at dawn. Many people in bat country simply accept a little nightly visit. Nectar bats are important pollinators.

## 2. Raccoons

Raccoons are strong, clever and love sugar water.

**Clues:** feeder tipped, pulled down, or on the ground; ports pried out; muddy prints on posts or railings.
**What to do:** take the feeder in at night, hang it from a thin wire away from railings and trees, or use a pole with a **baffle** (a cone or cylinder that blocks climbing).

## 3. Opossums and other climbers

Similar to raccoons but usually messier and less successful.

**Clues:** feeder disturbed but not completely destroyed, droppings nearby.
**What to do:** same fixes as raccoons: bring it in or use a baffle.

## 4. Bears

If you live in bear country, a sugar-water feeder is an invitation.

**Clues:** bent poles, crushed or missing feeders, claw marks, a very obvious mess.
**What to do:** **bring feeders in every night**, no exceptions. A bear that learns your yard has food will come back, which is dangerous for you and usually ends badly for the bear.

## 5. A leak you didn't notice

This one fools a lot of people. Inverted feeders (the bottle-on-top kind) can leak when **temperature changes** make the air inside the bottle expand. As the day warms up, the air pushes nectar out the ports. Loose seals and cracked bases do the same.

**Clues:** wet ground or a sticky pole, ants gathering below, feeder empties faster on hot days or in direct sun.
**What to do:** tighten or replace seals, move the feeder to partial shade, or switch to a **saucer-style feeder**, which doesn't rely on a vacuum and rarely drips.

## 6. Insects (usually a daytime problem)

Bees, wasps and ants drink nectar too, but mostly during the day. If your feeder is also dropping during daylight, they're likely part of it.

**What to do:** add an **ant moat** above the feeder, choose feeders with bee guards, and avoid yellow parts, which attract bees.

## Still not sure? Set up a camera

A cheap trail camera or even an old phone recording overnight will solve the mystery in one night. It's also genuinely fun to see who visits.

## A note on fall

Don't take your feeder down just because a nighttime visitor shows up. Many experts suggest keeping feeders up for a couple of weeks after you see your last hummingbird in fall, to help late migrants. Just bring it in at night if you've got night-time raiders.

## FAQ

**Do hummingbirds drink from feeders at night?**
No. Hummingbirds rest overnight and resume feeding at first light.

**What animal drains hummingbird feeders at night?**
Commonly bats (in the Southwest), raccoons, opossums and bears. Leaks can also make a feeder look drained.

**How do I stop raccoons from emptying my hummingbird feeder?**
Bring the feeder inside at night, or hang it on a pole with a baffle, away from railings and branches.

**Why does my hummingbird feeder leak?**
Temperature changes can push nectar out of inverted feeders, and worn seals or cracks cause drips. Saucer-style feeders leak less.
