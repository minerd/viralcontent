# Why Are My Guppy Fry Disappearing? Eaten, Hiding or Dying

You counted fourteen fry yesterday. Today there are four, and no bodies. Pages about "guppy fry dying" don't explain this, because with fry **disappearing is usually different from dying** — there's normally nothing left to find.

Four explanations cover almost every case.

## 1. Something ate them (most likely)

Guppies eat their own young, and so does everything else in a community tank. Adults, including the mother, will pick off fry within hours of birth.

Signs: numbers drop steadily over a day or two, the remaining fry are the biggest ones, and the adults look well fed.

What to do:
- **Separate** — either move the pregnant female to a breeding/birthing box or planted tank, or move the fry out after birth
- **Cover** — dense floating plants (water sprite, hornwort, guppy grass), moss, spawning mops. Fry survive in heavily planted tanks far better than in bare ones
- Don't rely on a tiny plastic breeder trap long-term; they stress the female, and fry outgrow them quickly
- Keep the adults **well fed**, which reduces (but doesn't stop) predation
- Also check for hidden predators: dragonfly nymphs, hydra, planaria and large snails can all take fry in a tank you believe is fish-free

## 2. The filter took them

A hang-on-back or canister intake will pull fry straight in. Some survive inside the filter for days — check in there before assuming the worst.

Fix: run a **sponge filter** in a fry tank, or cover the intake with a sponge pre-filter. It also removes the current that exhausts fry.

## 3. They died, and were eaten or broke down

Fry are tiny. A dead fry is gone within hours — eaten by the adults, snails, or shrimp, or simply decomposed. So "no bodies" doesn't rule out death.

The common killers in a fry tank:
- **Ammonia or nitrite.** Small tanks, heavy feeding and lots of waste: even a level adults shrug off kills fry in hours. Test. Change 20% of the water immediately if anything registers
- **Temperature swings.** A 2–3 °C overnight drop can wipe out a batch
- **Starvation.** Fry need feeding several times a day on something small enough: crushed flake, powdered fry food, microworms, baby brine shrimp
- **Flow.** Fry fighting a current burn energy they don't have
- **Jumping or getting stranded** in the lid, a breeder box seam, or behind an ornament

## 4. They're hiding and you can't see them

Fry hide for the first days, especially in a planted or gravel-bottom tank, and come out when the lights dim and they're hungry. Before concluding anything, look:
- At feeding time, with the room quiet
- Along the back glass, behind the heater, under plant leaves
- Inside filter media and spray bars
- In the top corners — fry hug the surface

## Raising more of them

1. **Separate fry tank**, cycled before you need it, 10–20 litres is plenty
2. **Sponge filter**, gentle flow
3. **Lots of plants or moss** for cover and for grazing
4. **Small, frequent feeds** and matching water changes
5. **Stable temperature**, around 24–26 °C
6. Test **ammonia, nitrite, nitrate** regularly while the tank is young
7. Expect losses: in nature almost no guppy fry survive. A good home rate is high, not perfect

## FAQ

**Will guppies eat their own babies?**
Yes, routinely. That's the first thing to assume when fry disappear.

**Do guppy fry need a separate tank?**
Not strictly, if the tank is heavily planted, but survival is much higher in a separate tank or a well-covered nursery area.

**How long until fry are safe from adults?**
Roughly when they're too big to fit in an adult's mouth — commonly around 4–6 weeks depending on growth.

**Why would all my fry die overnight?**
Most often an ammonia/nitrite spike or a temperature drop. Both act fast on fry.
