# Why Are My Geese Eating Dirt? Grit, Minerals and Foraging

If you keep geese, you've probably seen them nibbling at the ground, pulling up mud, or even swallowing small stones. It can look strange, but **a certain amount of dirt-eating is normal goose behavior.**

Here's why they do it and when you should pay closer attention.

## 1. They need grit

Birds don't have teeth. Instead, they have a muscular organ called the **gizzard**, which grinds food. To do that, they swallow small stones and coarse particles called **grit**.

Geese that graze on grass and fibrous plants especially need grit to break down tough food.

**What to do:** provide **insoluble poultry grit** free-choice, especially if your geese don't have access to natural small stones.

## 2. Foraging

Geese are grazers and foragers. When they dig at the soil, they're often after:
- **Roots** and young plant shoots
- **Worms, grubs and insects**
- Seeds

A goose "eating dirt" is often mostly looking for these, and swallowing some soil along the way.

## 3. Minerals

Soil contains minerals. Birds sometimes eat dirt more when they're seeking minerals, including during laying season when calcium needs go up.

**What to do:** feed a **balanced waterfowl feed** appropriate for their age and stage, and provide a calcium source like oyster shell for laying geese.

## When to pay attention

Dirt-eating becomes a concern if:
- It seems **excessive** and obsessive
- Geese are **losing weight** or seem unwell
- They're eating **foreign objects**: nails, screws, wire, plastic, string. Swallowed metal and sharp objects can cause serious internal injuries
- Their droppings change a lot

**Action:** check their diet, remove debris from the yard, and contact a vet if a goose seems unwell.

## Keep the yard safe

- Walk the area and pick up **nails, screws, wire and bits of metal**, especially after construction or fence repair
- Keep **string, twine and fishing line** out of reach
- Avoid treated soil or areas with chemicals

## FAQ

**Why do geese eat dirt?**
For grit to grind food, while foraging for roots and bugs, and sometimes for minerals.

**Do geese need grit?**
Yes, especially if they don't have access to small stones naturally. Provide insoluble poultry grit.

**Is it bad if my geese eat a lot of dirt?**
Some is normal. Excessive eating, weight loss, or eating objects like metal are reasons for concern.

**What should geese eat?**
Grass and forage plus a balanced waterfowl feed suitable for their age, with grit and, for layers, a calcium source.
