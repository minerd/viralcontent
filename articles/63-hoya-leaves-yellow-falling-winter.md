# Hoya Leaves Turning Yellow and Falling Off in Winter? The 5 Likely Causes

Hoyas are famous for being easygoing, so it's alarming when yours starts dropping yellow leaves as the weather cools.

The good news: in winter, the cause is usually one of a handful of fixable things, and one of them isn't a problem at all. Here's how to figure out which.

## 1. Too much water for the amount of light (most common)

In winter, your hoya gets less light, grows more slowly, and uses much less water. If you keep watering on your summer schedule, the soil stays wet for too long, roots start to suffer, and leaves yellow and drop.

**Clues:**
- Soil still damp days after watering
- Yellow leaves that feel **soft or limp**
- Yellowing that starts on older, lower leaves and spreads
- In bad cases, a musty smell or dark, mushy roots

**Fix:**
- Let the soil dry **most or all of the way** before watering in winter. For many homes, that means watering far less often than in summer.
- Make sure the pot drains. Hoyas hate sitting in water.
- If you suspect rot, unpot and check the roots. Trim anything black and mushy, and repot in a chunky, fast-draining mix.

## 2. Cold drafts or a cold window

Hoyas are tropical. Cold glass, drafty windows, doors opening onto winter air, or a spot next to an air conditioner can all stress them.

**Clues:** yellowing or dropping mostly on the side facing the window or door; leaves that touched cold glass; sudden drop after a cold night.

**Fix:** move the plant a little away from the glass, or pull it back from the window on very cold nights. Most hoyas prefer to stay comfortably above about 60°F (15°C).

## 3. Heating and dry air

Radiators and heating vents create hot, dry air right next to the plant.

**Clues:** crispy leaf edges along with yellowing, plant near a heat source.

**Fix:** move it away from direct heat. Grouping plants together or using a humidifier can help.

## 4. Not enough light

Winter light can be a fraction of summer light. Hoyas may drop some leaves to match what they can support.

**Clues:** leggy new growth, gradual yellowing of inner or shaded leaves.

**Fix:** move it to the brightest spot you have without cold drafts, or add a small grow light.

## 5. Normal old-leaf shedding (not a problem)

Hoyas, like all plants, occasionally drop their oldest leaves. One or two yellow leaves on an otherwise healthy, firm plant is usually just housekeeping.

**Clues:** only the oldest leaves, a few at a time, the rest of the plant firm and healthy.

**Fix:** nothing. Remove the yellow leaves and carry on.

## A quick diagnosis table

| What you see | Most likely cause |
|---|---|
| Soft, yellow leaves + damp soil | Overwatering for winter light |
| Yellowing on the window side | Cold drafts / cold glass |
| Crispy edges + near a radiator | Hot, dry air |
| Leggy growth + slow yellowing | Low light |
| One or two old leaves only | Normal shedding |

## One important don't

**Don't cut off the peduncles**, the little stubby spurs where flowers form. Hoyas rebloom from the same spurs, and they can look like dead stems in winter.

## FAQ

**Why are my hoya leaves turning yellow in winter?**
Most often because the soil stays too wet in low winter light. Cold drafts, dry heat and low light are other common causes.

**Should I water my hoya less in winter?**
Yes. Let the soil dry most or all of the way between waterings.

**What temperature is too cold for a hoya?**
Most hoyas prefer to stay above about 60°F (15°C) and dislike cold drafts.

**Should I remove yellow hoya leaves?**
Yes, you can gently remove fully yellow leaves. Leave the peduncles (flower spurs) alone.
