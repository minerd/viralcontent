# Astra, Sol or Luna? Which ChatGPT Model You're Actually Using (and How to Pick)

OpenAI released three new models in September 2026, and a lot of ChatGPT users are confused about which one they're talking to.

On OpenAI's own community forum, paying subscribers have been asking things like "Is Astra no longer available in the web version?" and "Why was Astra announced for all Plus users when I can't find it?" The short answer: it's usually there, just hidden behind labels that don't say what they mean.

Here's the plain-English version.

## The three models

**GPT-6 Astra** (released early September)
OpenAI's most powerful model. It focuses on computer and browser use, coding, and complex multi-step work. It's also the most expensive to run, which is why access is more limited.

**GPT-6 Sol** (released September 22)
Designed for complex tasks like coding. OpenAI says it makes about half as many mistakes as the previous Sol, "reaching Astra-level reliability at much lower cost."

**GPT-6 Luna** (released September 22)
Built for what OpenAI calls clerical work: high-volume tasks with a clear goal, like summarizing documents, pulling out information, or answering quick questions. It's the fast, cheap one.

And to make it more confusing, the **previous versions (GPT-5.6 Sol and GPT-5.6 Luna)** are still around in some places.

## Think of it like this

| Model | Best for | Think of it as |
|---|---|---|
| Astra | Hardest problems, agent tasks, big coding jobs | The specialist you book for serious work |
| Sol | Everyday complex work, coding, analysis | The reliable senior colleague |
| Luna | Quick answers, summaries, simple tasks | The fast assistant |

For most people, most of the time, **Sol or Luna is enough.** Astra is for when the others struggle.

## Why you can't find Astra

This is where users get stuck.

**On ChatGPT's paid plans, the model picker doesn't always name the model.** Options like "Latest" or thinking levels hide which model is running underneath.

According to OpenAI's help documentation, quoted by users on the forum, **"GPT-6 Pro is powered by GPT-6 Astra."** A Pro user worked out that choosing the **"Pro"** option (rather than one of the regular thinking levels) is what gets you Astra, while the standard reasoning options use Sol. As one forum reply put it, it's "a poor UI" rather than Astra being unavailable.

**If you're on Plus**, users report that Astra access is currently limited to specific areas (ChatGPT Work and Codex) rather than the regular chat. There's been no official clarification in the forum thread yet. [ADD: update when OpenAI clarifies Plus access]

## Where each model is available (as of September 22)

According to OpenAI's announcement as reported by TechCrunch:
- The new **Sol and Luna** are available in **ChatGPT Work and Codex** for most paid accounts, and in the **API**.
- **Luna** is also coming to the **desktop app** and to **Free and Go** users.
- The 6-series models cost **half as much** as the 5.6 series through the API.

## How to know which model answered you

- **Check the model label** under or next to the response, if shown.
- **In the desktop app**, check what your selected option maps to.
- **Developers**: log the model name in the API response metadata. Some developers on the forum report requests to Astra being answered by a different model, so logging is the only way to be sure.

## Which should you use?

- **Writing emails, summaries, quick questions:** Luna is fine. Save your limits.
- **Real work (analysis, coding, long documents):** Sol.
- **Something Sol got wrong twice, or a task that needs the agent to click around websites and apps:** Astra.

If a response seems suspiciously fast and shallow on a hard question, users on the forum report that simply pressing **"Try again"** sometimes gets a properly reasoned answer.

## FAQ

**What is GPT-6 Astra?**
OpenAI's most capable model as of September 2026, focused on computer use, coding and complex professional work.

**What's the difference between Sol and Luna?**
Sol is designed for complex tasks like coding. Luna is for fast, high-volume tasks like summarizing and quick answers.

**Can ChatGPT Plus users use Astra?**
Users report that Plus access is currently limited to ChatGPT Work and Codex. OpenAI hasn't clarified further on its forum.

**How do I use Astra in ChatGPT Pro?**
OpenAI's documentation says GPT-6 Pro is powered by Astra. Users report selecting the "Pro" option, rather than a regular thinking level, to use it.
