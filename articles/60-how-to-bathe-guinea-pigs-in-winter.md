# How to Bathe Guinea Pigs in Winter (and When You Shouldn't at All)

First, the most useful fact: **most guinea pigs rarely need a full bath.** They groom themselves, and frequent bathing can dry out their skin. In winter, a bath also comes with an extra risk, because a damp guinea pig can get chilled quickly.

So before you fill a basin, ask whether you actually need one.

*General care information. If your guinea pig has skin problems, hair loss, scabs or intense scratching, see a vet. Those can be signs of mites or fungal infections, which need treatment, not just a bath.*

## When a bath is actually needed

- A guinea pig is **genuinely dirty**, for example with urine staining or something stuck in the fur
- **Long-haired breeds** (like Peruvians or Silkies) with soiled back ends
- A **vet has recommended** a medicated bath

For everything else, spot-cleaning is usually enough.

## Try these first

- **Spot clean** dirty areas with a damp, warm cloth
- **Bottom bath:** rinse just the rear end in a little warm water
- **Trim** long hair around the back end to prevent soiling (carefully, or ask a groomer or vet)
- **Grease gland cleaning** for boars: the oily spot near the base of the spine can be spot-treated rather than bathing the whole pig
- **Brush** regularly, especially long-haired pigs

## How to give a winter bath, step by step

If a full bath is needed, preparation matters more than the bath itself.

**Before you start**
1. **Warm the room.** A warm bathroom or kitchen, no drafts, windows closed.
2. **Get everything ready:** two or three towels, a small basin or sink, a cup for rinsing, and a **guinea pig–safe shampoo** (not human shampoo).
3. **Have a warm, dry spot ready** for afterward, like a towel-lined box or their cage with extra bedding, away from drafts.

**The bath**
4. Fill the basin with **a few centimeters (about an inch or two) of lukewarm water**. Test it with your wrist. It should feel barely warm.
5. Hold your guinea pig gently but securely. Keep a hand under their chest.
6. Use the cup to wet their body. **Keep water away from the face, eyes, ears and nose.**
7. Apply a small amount of shampoo, work it in gently, and **rinse thoroughly**. Leftover shampoo irritates skin.
8. Keep it short: a few minutes.

**Drying (the most important part in winter)**
9. Wrap them in a towel right away and blot, don't rub hard.
10. Switch to a dry towel. Repeat.
11. If they tolerate it, use a **hair dryer on the lowest heat or cool setting**, held well away and kept moving. Check the air on your own hand constantly. Guinea pigs can overheat and burn easily.
12. **Check they're dry to the skin**, especially long-haired pigs.
13. Keep them **indoors in a warm, draft-free space until completely dry**, which can take a few hours for long coats.

## Outdoor guinea pigs

Don't bathe an outdoor guinea pig and put them back outside in cold weather. If a bath is unavoidable, keep them inside in a warm room until fully dry, then return them only if outdoor temperatures and their shelter are suitable. For many owners, winter is a good time to bring outdoor guinea pigs inside anyway.

## FAQ

**Can you bathe guinea pigs in winter?**
Yes, if necessary, but do it in a warm room, keep it brief, and dry them completely before they're exposed to any cold.

**How often should guinea pigs be bathed?**
Rarely. Most only need spot-cleaning. Long-haired breeds may need occasional bottom baths.

**Can I use a hair dryer on my guinea pig?**
Only on the lowest heat or cool setting, held at a distance and kept moving, while checking the temperature on your hand.

**Can I use baby shampoo on a guinea pig?**
It's best to use a shampoo made for small animals or one recommended by your vet.
